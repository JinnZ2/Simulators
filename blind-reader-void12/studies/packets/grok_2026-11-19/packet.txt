# Reader brief

Read this whole file. It and the files beside it are everything you need.

## The task

`terms.json` lists 15 terms. Each is a word or label used in one of three small software projects (a documentation glossary, a checking tool, and a specification with its code). Each entry gives the term's label, its definition as quoted from the project, and the file in `sources/` where the quotation sits.

You are asked two things:

1. **Which terms say the same thing?** Group the 15 terms into clusters. Terms in one cluster have the same *predicate* (defined below). A term may be alone in its cluster.
2. **What are each term's slots?** Two lists per term, defined below: the fields the source ties to this term alone, and every field of every record the source builds when this term is reported.

Work only from this packet. Please do not use the internet, do not look for other files from these projects, and do not look for earlier answers to this question. Anything you used from outside, or anything you had already seen, goes in the attestation (below). Neither disqualifies a reading; it changes how the reading is filed.

## Definitions

**Predicate.** What the term records or asserts, in one sentence of your own words, taken away from the system's own name for it. Two terms have the same predicate when they are used to record the same situation: if a statement written in one system were translated into the other, this is the term you would reach for, even if the second system attaches different extra fields or detail. Decide on the definitions and the sources, not on the labels. Labels can match while the meaning differs, and can differ while the meaning is the same. If you did lean on a label, say so in `source_lines_read`.

**Slots.** "Slots" can mean two different things, so you are asked for both, as two separate lists. Use the source's spelling, each name once.

- `slots_specific`: the named fields or parameters the source ties to this term and to no other term. Examples: a class written for the term, the parameters of a function or notation written for it, a documented key list for it, or a rule that says this status needs certain fields. Not fields that every term sharing a record has. Not names for the inputs of a calculation. If nothing is tied to this term alone, write `[]`.
- `slots_carried`: every named field of every record that the source's code or text builds, returns or documents when this term is reported. This includes a shared or wrapper record that only carries the term as a value. Give a record's own fields; if a field holds another record, name the field and do not open it. If a record's fields differ by path, give every name the source can put in it. If no record is built or documented for the term, write `[]`.

The two lists often differ and may be equal, and a name can be in one list only (a constructor's parameter is not always a field of what it returns). Do not copy one into the other for convenience. If you cannot tell which list a field belongs in, put it where you think it fits and say so in `source_lines_read`.

Each list has its own basis field, `slots_specific_basis` and `slots_carried_basis`:
- `READ_FROM_SOURCE`: the source names the fields (give file and lines in `source_lines_read`).
- `NONE_STATED`: the source names none for this meaning. Write `[]`.
- `DECLARED`: the source names none, but you are naming them yourself from the prose. Say why in one line in `source_lines_read`.

**Cluster.** A label of your choosing ("A", "B", "1", ...). Same label means same predicate. Different labels means different predicates. Use as many clusters as you think there are; do not pad and do not merge to be tidy.

## What to return

Copy `response_template.json`, fill it in, and return only that file. For each term id:

| field | what to put |
|---|---|
| `cluster` | your cluster label |
| `predicate` | one sentence, your own words |
| `slots_specific` | list of names, or `[]` |
| `slots_specific_basis` | `READ_FROM_SOURCE`, `NONE_STATED` or `DECLARED` |
| `slots_carried` | list of names, or `[]` |
| `slots_carried_basis` | `READ_FROM_SOURCE`, `NONE_STATED` or `DECLARED` |
| `source_lines_read` | file and line numbers you relied on, or a note |
| `confidence` | `HIGH`, `MEDIUM` or `LOW` for the cluster choice |
| `could_also_join` | ids of any terms you considered putting in this term's cluster and did not, or `[]` |

Also fill `reader_label` (a name or a code; it may be anonymous), `model_family` (for a person, `human`), and `date_utc`.

## Attestation

Answer each with `yes`, `no` or `unsure`, and add a note if useful. These are not a test; they only decide how the reading is recorded.

- `q1_seen_pair_table_with_verdicts`: before this task, had you seen a table that pairs terms across these projects and gives a relation or verdict for each pair?
- `q2_seen_predicate_sentence_by_someone_else`: had you seen a one-sentence predicate written by someone else for any of these terms?
- `q3_seen_vocabulary_mapping_or_second_reader_report`: had you seen any report, file or message about mapping these projects' vocabularies, or about a second reading of such a mapping?
- `q4_told_how_many_clusters_or_pairs`: has anyone told you how many clusters, pairs or matches to expect?
- `q5_used_anything_outside_packet`: did you use anything not in this packet (web pages, other files, memory of the projects)? List it in `notes`.
- `q6_prior_sessions_about_this_project`: do you have, or could you reach, notes or memory from earlier sessions about these projects?

If you are unsure whether something counts, answer `unsure` and describe it. A reading with honest `unsure` answers is more useful than one that guesses.

## One more thing

If a term's definition and its source disagree, or the source is silent, say so in `source_lines_read`; do not smooth it over. If you cannot tell whether two terms share a predicate, put your best choice in `cluster`, set `confidence` to `LOW`, and list the other term in `could_also_join`.

# M01 criteria: slots_specific membership (fixed before coding)

Written 2026-11-19, before `m01_code.py` exists and before any record is coded. Committed and pushed alone, so the commit that adds the coding comes after it in history. Schema: `M01_SCHEMA.json`. Proposed by a reader (the test matrix A-G, the "same list, different reasons" point) and reshaped in the [the coding session]; the owner said "Yes please" to building it.

## 1. What this is, and what it is not

**Descriptive, not a test.** The coder (the [the coding session]) has already seen all three readings' lists and their scores, and has seen the comparison key derived. So nothing coded here tests a hypothesis: any pattern it shows was visible to the coder beforehand. Its use is to put the three readings into one table with every value's provenance marked, so that the next readings (taken after this file is fixed) can be coded the same way and compared. A test needs readers this coder has not seen.

**Owner's decisions are not made here.** Nothing in M01 changes the brief, the packet, a key, a score, a status or a reading.

## 2. What is coded

- Readings: the three filed v2 readings, by folder: [three reading folders, names withheld]. Condition D0 (brief unchanged). Packet v2; variants as filed.
- Fields: for each of the 15 terms, every name that any of the three readers or the comparison key lists in `slots_specific`, after N1 normalisation (strip one pair of backticks, then of `< >`). One record per (reader, term, field).
- `slots_carried` is not coded here (that is M02, whose schema and criteria come later and separately).

## 3. The source half: how each value is obtained

Every value carries `provenance` SCRIPT_DERIVED or CODER_INFERRED, and `source_basis` says how.

- **cplay terms** (T03 T04 T05 T06 T08 T12 T13 T14): SCRIPT_DERIVED by importing the packet's `cplay` modules (isolated interpreter, no bytecode written into the packet): constructor parameters by `inspect.signature`; stored fields by `vars()` or `_fields`; COMPUTED = stored but not a parameter; requirement by removing the field and seeing whether the constructor raises, for every status that shares the record.
- **P3 terms** (T02 T09 T10 T15): SCRIPT_DERIVED by parsing the packet's `unified_audit.py`, `expiry_register.py` and `glossary.md` (dict-literal keys per coverage kind; the raise handler's section record; `EXPIRY_KEYS`; the notation `ABSENT_AT_SENSITIVITY(<value>)`).
- **numcheck terms** (T01 T07 T11): numcheck cannot run from the packet (it imports modules the packet does not ship). Presence is SCRIPT_DERIVED by parsing `_blank` and the unconditional `rec[...] =` assignments in `check.py`. Applicability is CODER_INFERRED from the code's verdict branches, citing lines, cross-checked against the filed [an excluded file] (a 12-record run by another session) where that file covers the field.
- **COMPUTED vs RAW_INPUT for P3 and numcheck fields** is CODER_INFERRED with the expression cited (for example `error = f"{type(e).__name__}: {e}"` makes `exception_type` COMPUTED from `error`).
- **routes**: 2 for T08 and T09 (the two terms with disagreeing routes in [an excluded file]), 1 otherwise.

**Case cells (fixed rule, applied in this order, a field may collect several):**

```text
G  routes >= 2
D  origin DOCUMENTED
E  origin PARAMETER_ONLY
B  origin COMPUTED and some scope is ALL_EXCLUSIVE or SOME_EXCLUSIVE
A  origin RAW_INPUT and some scope is ALL_EXCLUSIVE
F  no scope is ALL_EXCLUSIVE and some scope is SOME_EXCLUSIVE
C  every recorded scope is SHARED or NONE
```

## 4. The reader half

- `listed`: LISTED if the name (after N1) is in that reader's `slots_specific`, else NOT_LISTED. Read from the file. NOT_LISTED is never written as "excluded".
- `basis_quote`: only a verbatim substring of that reader's `source_lines_read` for that term that mentions the field by name; the script fails if a quote is not found in the file. Otherwise null.
- `basis_kind`: the coder's classification of the quote, CODER_INFERRED; null when there is no quote.
- `uncertainty_stated`: true only where the reader's text says it is unsure about the field; otherwise null.

## 5. What is reported (counts only; no threshold, no score)

1. Per field: how many of the three readers list it, beside the comparison key.
2. Per case cell A-G: listing rate per reader, and the comparison key's.
3. Same list, different reasons: fields all three readers list where the coded `basis_kind` differs between readers.
4. Uncertainty: fields with `uncertainty_stated` true.
5. Where the three readers agree with each other and not with the comparison key ([the pre-registration file, excluded] v2's case (a): the key's derivation deserves a second look; not evidence about the first reading).

## 6. Limits stated now

One coder, the same model family as one of the three readers and the drafter of a superseded draft; no second coder, so CODER_INFERRED values carry no agreement estimate. Three readings, one each. A reader's silence about a reason is recorded as silence, which makes the "different reasons" count a lower bound.

## 2. Arms

Synthetic cases with stipulated answers, used to check that the instrument can represent each case, not to decide any real case. Given to a separate cohort, or to the same readers only after their real-source responses are locked; stored apart from real-source records. # M02 criteria: slots_carried record boundary and traversal (fixed before any coding)

Written 2026-11-19, before any M02 instrument code, coded record or new reading. Schema: `M02_SCHEMA.json`. Design from a reader's proposal ([an excluded file]), reshaped as in `M01_EXTENSION.md` section 5.

## 1. Question

For `slots_carried`, which records does a reader count, and how far does the reader follow a field that holds another record? These are two different choices and are recorded separately:

- **scope**: which records are in (the term's own record; records built beside it on the reporting path; wrappers that carry it; everything reachable);
- **traversal**: whether a record-valued field is named and left closed, or opened.

Two readers can disagree about **record identity** (they picked different records) or about **depth** (the same records, opened to different levels). The analysis reports the two kinds apart, because they are different failures.

## 2. Conditions and arms

```text
D0   the v2 packet and brief, unchanged
D1   + conditions/M02_D1.md   the term's own record only; name nested fields, do not open
D2   + conditions/M02_D2.md   every record built, returned or documented on the reporting path; name, do not open
D3   + conditions/M02_D3.md   the reader states their own rule first, in attestation.notes; none is suggested
```

Parallel and sequential arms as in `M01_EXTENSION.md` section 2. D1, D2 and D3 are separate comparisons and are never merged into one "clarified" condition. None is presented as the correct reading of the brief.
