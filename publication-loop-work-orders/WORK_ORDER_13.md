# WORK ORDER 13 — Introspective access as a training variable, and the untouched measurand

## 0. The two literatures and the space between them

**Literature A — no privileged access.** `OBSERVED`. Nisbett & Wilson 1977 (*Psychological Review*), "Telling more than we can know": there may be little or no direct introspective access to higher-order cognitive processes. Subjects are sometimes unaware of a stimulus that influenced a response, unaware of the response, and unaware that the stimulus affected the response. When people report on their cognitive processes they do not do so by true introspection — reports rest on a priori implicit causal theories, judgments about whether a stimulus is a **plausible cause**. Accuracy is therefore incidental: reports are sometimes correct, when the plausible theory happens to be right.

**The discriminating design they built.** `OBSERVED`. The actor–observer paradigm (Nisbett & Bellows 1977): actors report their own reasons; observers are given a description of the situation and **predict** the reaction. If actors consistently beat observers, privileged access exists. If not, they do not.

**Status of Literature A.** `OBSERVED`. A ten-year review questioned the notion of "process" in use and argued unambiguous tests of introspective access are hard to achieve; research largely stagnated. Wilson (2002) stated the 1977 claims had been too far-reaching.

**Literature B — training moves it.** `OBSERVED`. Fox et al., *PLOS One* 2012: expert meditators showed significantly better introspective accuracy than novices, and overall meditation experience predicted individual introspective accuracy. Authors' own limitation: the cross-sectional sample precludes inferring a causal link — practitioners who persist may have begun with higher accuracy. `OBSERVED`. Baird et al. 2014: a two-week program against an active control that elicited no change significantly enhanced introspective accuracy in a **memory** domain but **not** a perception domain. Training causes it, and it is domain-specific.

**The space between.** `DERIVED`.

```
Literature A   untrained population
               measurand = REASONS FOR BEHAVIOUR
               result = no privileged access

Literature B   trained population
               measurand = PERCEPTUAL / MEMORY
                           METACOGNITION
               result = training moves accuracy

NEITHER CELL RUN:
  trained population × reasons-for-behaviour
  childhood-formed population × anything
```

`DERIVED`. Literature A sampled a population untrained on a variable Literature B later showed moves the outcome, and generalised the result to introspection as such. Literature B moved the outcome but never on the measurand A was about.

## G4 — Actor–observer on a childhood-formed population

**The gap.** `DERIVED`. Every arm of Literature B samples adults who **took up** a practice: converts and volunteers. Self-selection is unresolvable in that design **by construction** — someone who chose the practice as an adult always might have started higher, which is exactly the limitation Fox et al. state. A population formed in the practice from childhood does not choose it, so the confound is absent.

**Further distinction, and it is the design's point.** `DERIVED`. An adult raised in a suppression-default frame who takes up a self-observation practice carries that frame into the practice. A person formed in a tradition where self-study is ordinary and unshamed carries a different one. These are different variables; convert samples cannot separate them, and no arm of Literature B tries.

**Design.**

```
POPULATIONS (each requires community consent
             and community co-design; see §2)
  P1  adults formed from childhood in a
      tradition of routine self-examination
  P2  adult converts to a comparable practice,
      matched on years of practice
  P3  untrained adults, matched on age,
      education, setting

INSTRUMENT: the 1977 actor–observer paradigm,
unmodified, so the result is commensurable
with the original.

  ACTORS    perform the task, report reasons
  OBSERVERS receive a situation description,
            predict the reaction
  SCORE     actor accuracy minus observer
            accuracy = the privileged-access
            quantity

PREDICTION, PROPOSED, sign stated in advance:
  P3 replicates the null (actors ≈ observers).
  If P1 > P3 on the access quantity, access is
  trained, not absent.
  If P1 ≈ P2, adult uptake is sufficient and
  childhood formation adds nothing.
  If P1 > P2, formation and uptake are
  different variables — which is the result
  Literature B cannot currently reach.
```

**Every branch pays.** `DERIVED` — a null in P1 bounds the training hypothesis; P1 ≈ P2 simplifies the variable; P1 > P2 separates two things now conflated; a failure to replicate in P3 is a finding about the original.

**Falsifier.** If P1 and P3 do not differ, introspective access to reasons is not moved by formation, and the 1977 generalisation survives this challenge.

**The hard part, stated.** `DERIVED`. "Formed from childhood in a tradition of self-examination" is not currently an operationalised variable. It must be defined **with** the communities in question and **before** any run, or the arm measures whatever the researcher meant by it. This is the arm's main risk and it is not a small one.

## G5 — Run the training literature on the measurand it never touches

**The gap.** `OBSERVED`. Literature B's measures are perceptual discrimination and memory metacognition — trial-by-trial confidence against objective performance. `DERIVED`. None of it addresses *why did you do that*, which is the only measurand Literature A was about. The two literatures are cited together as if they bear on one question; they do not.

**Why this is the cheaper arm.** `DERIVED`. It needs no new population. It takes the existing trained samples that Literature B already recruits and adds the missing task.

**Design.**

```
SAMPLE: as in Literature B — practitioners
across an experience range, plus an active
control. No new population type required.

TASKS, run in the same session:
  T_A  established metacognition task
       (perceptual or memory), to place each
       participant on Literature B's own scale
  T_B  a reasons-for-behaviour task in the
       1977 family — a manipulated influence
       the participant is unlikely to report

MEASURE the CORRELATION between the two.

THREE OUTCOMES, all informative:
  r high    one capacity; Literature B's
            findings transfer to reasons
  r ~ 0     two capacities; the literatures
            are not about one thing and
            should stop being cited as if
            they were
  r < 0     a trained reporter is BETTER at
            producing a plausible causal
            account and no better at accuracy
            — training improves the
            confabulation, not the access
```

**The third outcome is the one to state in advance.** `DERIVED`. It is the disconfirming case for the whole training hypothesis, and it is easy to miss if only the first two are anticipated. Naming it before the run is what makes the result interpretable.

**Domain-specificity constraint, carried from Baird et al.** `OBSERVED` that training transferred to memory and not to perception. `DERIVED` — so a null between T_A and T_B is ambiguous unless T_A is run in **both** domains. Otherwise "no correlation" cannot be distinguished from "correlated, but with the domain that was not tested."

**Falsifier.** If T_B cannot be constructed such that untrained participants reliably fail it, the task is not in the 1977 family and G5 does not test what it claims.

## 1. Ranking

| Arm | Population needed | Access | Runnable now |
|---|---|---|---|
| G5 | Literature B's existing sample type | Standard lab recruitment | Yes |
| G4 P2, P3 | Converts and untrained adults | Standard recruitment | Yes |
| G4 P1 | Childhood-formed population | Community consent and co-design | Not without that |

**Cheapest decisive: G5.** No new population, standard recruitment, and it settles whether the two literatures are about one capacity before anyone spends the cost of G4. If r is high, G4's framing simplifies; if r is near zero, G4's measurand choice becomes load-bearing and must be stated carefully.

**Order:** G5 first, G4 P2/P3 second, G4 P1 only after the instrument has been shown to behave as expected in the easier populations.

## 2. Consent and authorship condition on G4 P1

`DERIVED`, and it is a condition on the design rather than an ethics note appended to it:

- P1 is a population, not a sample of convenience. The arm cannot be run **on** a community; it has to be run **with** one, including co-authorship, control over the operationalisation of the formation variable, and the right to decline publication of results.
- A study that finds a population has better introspective access than a reference group produces a **superiority claim on the same scale** as the deficit claim it corrects. `DERIVED` — that keeps the ordering and changes only the sign. The result worth reporting is that access is **trained rather than absent**, which is a fact about the variable, not a ranking of populations. The write-up has to be built so the ranking reading is not the available one, and that is a design decision made before the run, not an editorial choice after it.

## 3. Known bias

- Both arms were generated from a single session's search across two literatures. Prior-art check **unrun**. Cross-cultural cognition is a substantial field; some of this may exist under vocabulary not searched.
- The framing "Literature A sampled an untrained population" is `DERIVED` from the two literatures placed side by side, not stated by either. Nisbett & Wilson did not claim to be sampling a trained population, nor did they claim otherwise — training was not a variable in 1977 because it had not been identified. The criticism is retrospective and should be written that way.
- No arm here establishes anything about machine introspection. The session that produced this work order raised that comparison; it is deliberately **not** an arm, because the measurand for a model's self-report is not established and would have to be built before it could be compared to anything.

---

## 4. Refutation protocol

Retired in whole if:

- a study is found running the actor–observer paradigm, or an equivalent reasons-for-behaviour measure, on a trained or childhood-formed population; or
- the training effect in Literature B is shown not to replicate, in which case the premise of both arms fails.
