CONSTRUCTED. Not a measurement of any real corpus.

A protocol is an agreement about what the fields of a message mean. Two
parties exchange a message only when both parties already share that
agreement, and neither party owns it. A party that defects from the
protocol can still emit messages; the messages are not read, because
reading requires a convergence the defecting party has left.

A unit behaves the same way. A laboratory that reports a length in
metres is not the owner of the metre, and a laboratory that reports a
duration in seconds is not the owner of the second. Both trace to a
standard that neither laboratory owns, and a private redefinition of the
unit does not stop the laboratory emitting numbers. It stops every other
laboratory from reading them.

The script the message is written in is a standard of the same kind. A
private script transmits nothing. The convergence is the substrate, and
the message rides on it. Transmission requires that the parts of the
corpus do not contest each other's terms, and that requirement holds
whether or not the parties intend anything at all.
