# NO_ENGLISH_WORD_INDEX

Slots, not coinages. Nearest neighbors are approximations; read the 'misleads' column first.

Every term and slot in the coinage log, one row each:
`source/coinage-log.full.md` (chunks 1-7 delivered 2026-10-11, chunk 8 the
NEW ENTRIES section of 2026-11-14) plus ENTRY 001 of `COINAGE_LOG.md`.
Co-authored. CC0. Persons stripped per the originator's offsite rule; no
row names a person or a family member.

How the columns are filled:

- **status** is the entry's own status where it states one. Where it
  does not, the row reads `unnamed_here (log premise ...)`: the log's
  frontmatter defines it as terms coined because no standard vocabulary
  covered them. `open` marks an entry that keeps an existing word, rejects
  a candidate, or is marked untested or unconfirmed.
- **nearest English neighbors** and **misleads** come only from what the
  entry states; otherwise they read `not stated in entry`. Nothing is
  supplied from general knowledge.
- **frame-shaped** is marked where the log's 2026-08-30 note or the entry
  itself says so. On the seven new rows it is this index's reading,
  PROPOSED, and labelled as such.
- **nsm_paraphrase** (PROPOSED) restates the meaning with Natural Semantic
  Metalanguage primes (Wierzbicka) to strip the English frame. It is
  drafted for the seven new rows only. It was checked by hand against the
  prime list, not by an NSM practitioner, and the result is approximate:
  "do something to a place" leans on DO and PLACE for "build".

Not rows, because they are not terms or slots: the status field
`named_elsewhere | unnamed_here` (it is this index's status vocabulary),
the two STATED FAILURE POINT sections, the FILE NAMING note, and the
2026-08-30 frame-shaped-gap note.
