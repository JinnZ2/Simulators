## CDC_003 -- routing cost, not procedure cost

The order's I-2 note says the block was overshadowing plus ripple
avoidance, and procedure is what makes it legible and defensible, not what
causes it. So I-2 attributes cost to the routing gap directly: an event
whose actual resolver differs from the authorised one and whose resolution
was cheap. On the constructed field events that is the two-hour fuse and
the six-hour reposition, at 150/hour, for 1200; the third event is
resolved on authorisation with a 250 external cost, which is a fault cost
and is not attributed to routing. A design that modelled procedure as the
cause would recommend a procedure change and measure no effect, which the
order names as the mis-specification to avoid.

## CDC_006 -- the wrong node

I-5's ground truth is the resolver; the credited party is who the record
names. On the constructed events credit flows upward entirely, so the
error rate rises with the credited level and every error is an
over-credit. The order's consequence is the load-bearing one: a downstream
statistic built on those reports -- a performance review, promotion data,
or a dataset a model is later trained on -- measures reporting position,
not competence. I-3 is the same shape read from the confidence side, and
the two are not claimed to share more than the structure.
