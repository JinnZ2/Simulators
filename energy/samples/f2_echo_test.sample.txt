==================================================================

Construction the module is fitting against:
  w_desi(z) = w0 + wa * (1 - 1/(1+z))       # payload_bridge.py:156
            = w0 + wa * z/(1+z)             # i.e. CPL by definition
  w0 = -0.86,  wa = -0.53   (DESI_MU)

=== ORIGINAL basis  --  z/(1+z) present (listed twice) ===

  proposed:     -0.3531*z*inv(1+z) + -0.04347*exp(-2*z)
  terms used:   ['z*inv(1+z)', 'exp(-2*z)']
  residual std: 0.05923 -> 0.00315  (94.7% reduction)

=== EXCISED basis  --  'z*inv(1+z)' and '(1-a) [CPL-like]' removed ===

  terms used:   ['log(1+z)', 'exp(-z)']
  residual std: 0.05923 -> 0.01387  (76.6% reduction)

VERDICT
  F2 confirmed. Removing z/(1+z) from the basis produces a
  completely different functional form (-0.214*log(1+z) vs
  -0.353*z/(1+z)) with worse fit (77% vs 95% reduction).
  The original 'discovery' of a CPL w_a term is the greedy
  regressor returning the exact primitive it was seeded with,
  against a target constructed as that same primitive.

  Multiple survivors (inv, log, exp) correlate |>0.99| with the
  residual because at short redshift range they all look similar.
  The 'first term IS the CPL w_a form' phrasing described the
  regressor's basis, not the physics.
