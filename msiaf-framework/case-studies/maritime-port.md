# Case Study: Maritime Port & Intermodal Operations

**Sector:** Container terminal drayage — the third node in the triad with reefer
trucking (long-haul) and last-mile delivery (urban).

## Distinctive Systemic Friction Points

### 1. The Demurrage Clock as a Weaponized Financial Deadline
Demurrage is a layered penalty starting at the ocean-carrier level and trickling
down to the independent drayage driver — often with no contractual privity between
driver and terminal. Panic-driven behavior (e.g., exiting the cab in active lanes)
is a direct product of the clock, not of driver judgment.

**High-leverage fix:** automatically pause the demurrage clock when terminal turn
times exceed 90 minutes. Implementable via Federal Maritime Commission rule or a
port-authority tariff amendment.

### 2. Split-Chassis Pool Fragmentation
A rusted twist-lock exemplifies a maintenance-responsibility vacuum: the driver
didn't cause the defect but must choose between fixing it in a lethal zone or
losing the move and the income. Automated gate audits (optical inspection → repair
bay routing) close the gap; feeding defect data into Intermodal Equipment Provider
(IEP) contract renewals makes proactive maintenance competitive.

### 3. Thermal-Imaging Ground-Level Detection
Radar tuned for container-sized objects and high-mounted LiDAR on straddle carriers
routinely miss a human crouching at wheel level. Thermal imaging with
ground-projected laser borders and a 30-foot automatic braking trigger addresses
the acoustic and optical shadow problem; combined with interlocked safe-excursion
bays, it eliminates the most common port fatality pattern.

## Comparative System Analysis

| Evaluation Focus | Legacy Reactive Model | MSIAF Proactive Maritime Architecture |
|---|---|---|
| Incident cause | Third-party contractor violation; unauthorized pedestrian entry | Demurrage-induced financial urgency; fragmented chassis maintenance; automation blind-zone design; unpaid queue exhaustion |
| Legal & financial liability | Isolated to drayage driver (independent contractor); MTO and ocean carrier insulated | Joint-accountability port tariff with queue-time penalties to MTO; automatic demurrage pause; IEP maintenance performance bonds |
| Operations & scheduling | Overbooked TOS appointment slots; no chassis defect screening at gate; mixed human-automation lanes | Zero-cross TOS lane allocation; automated optical gate audits routing defective chassis to repair bays; dynamic slot throttling by yard congestion |
| Infrastructure & automation | No refuge bays; ground-level humans in LiDAR/RADAR shadow zones; reliance on acoustic beepers | Interlocked safe-excursion cages with sector-wide equipment halt; 360° thermal imaging & ground-projected laser borders on all mobile equipment |
| Physiology & human factors | Extended unpaid gate idling → heat stress, dehydration, cognitive tunneling; no rest infrastructure | Regulated queue-time compensation; mandated climate-controlled driver hubs with hydration and restrooms; cab-door interlock with terminal emergency stop |
| Post-incident investigation | Police and terminal security blame driver "inattention" | Multi-dimensional audit of TOS scheduling, demurrage clock status, chassis pool maintenance records, gate wait-time logs, equipment sensor telemetry |

## Open Stress-Tests

- Vessel-side incident (longshore worker struck during lashing) — do safe-excursion
  bays and thermal fixes cover all high-fatality zones?
- Multi-terminal regional congestion — demurrage clock running across three
  interconnected facilities.
- End-to-end linkage: container path ship → warehouse → last-mile van, cascading
  proactive interventions across nodes.
