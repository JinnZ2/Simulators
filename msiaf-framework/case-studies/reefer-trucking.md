# Case Study: Long-Haul Perishable Goods Freight (Reefer Trucking)

**Incident type:** Run-off-road onto a soft shoulder after debris avoidance.

## Why This Sector

Reefer freight faces a "perfect storm" of pressures aligning directly with the
Systemic Interconnection Pathways: highway maintenance, dispatch feeds, strict
delivery penalties, thin freight margins, and dense traffic.

## Alignment of Friction Points (Before the Incident)

- **D4 → D2:** The contract imposes a strict temperature-excursion penalty clause.
  Deviation of a few degrees for more than 15 minutes means the receiver rejects
  the entire load of produce — creating top-down dispatch pressure on the carrier.
- **D2 → D1:** To avoid a spoilage claim, management schedules 800 miles in 13
  hours of drive time. The dispatcher overrides the driver's mandatory 30-minute
  rest break to "make the delivery window."
- **D3:** The GPS route passes through a rural stretch where an hour-old weather
  report conflicts with live hazards — washout debris on the shoulder after a
  summer rainstorm.
- **D1:** The driver, 8 hours in, moderately dehydrated and fatigued from circadian
  misalignment, suffers the micro-delay in visual processing when tire debris
  appears. They overcorrect; the soft shoulder gives way.

## Post-Incident Investigation (MSIAF Checklist)

1. **Environmental Audit:** Shoulder soil compaction testing shows poor municipal
   maintenance left the shoulder unpaved and unstable for weeks. Tire debris
   documented on the road surface.
2. **Information Feed Verification:** The GPS route was calculated 2 hours prior
   and never received the rain weather warning. The ELD shows the route flagged
   as "high urgency."
3. **Operational Context Review:** Dispatch logs show: *"You can rest at the
   receiver, we need this produce to hit the dock at 5 AM. No exceptions."* The
   dispatcher's quarterly bonus is tied to on-time delivery percentages.
4. **Physiological Baseline Assessment:** Duty hours were legal, but the directed
   truck stop had no healthy food options (micronutrient depletion), and the
   driver slept in the cab during a 105°F day (destroyed sleep quality).

## Takeaway

Not "driver error," but systemic failure: **the financial penalty structure (D4)
forced a rigid dispatch schedule (D2) onto a driver in poor physiological state
(D1), navigating an environmental hazard never communicated due to stale GPS data
(D3).**
