1  known-truth: what the anchor recovers, and what it does not
========================================================================
  gen   capability   gain       offset     contemporary   on v0       
  --------------------------------------------------------------------
  0     0.3000       1.0000     0.0000     0.3000         0.3000      
  1     0.3500       1.0600     0.0120     0.3830         0.3500

  4     0.5000       1.2400     0.0480     0.6680         0.5000      
  5     0.5500       1.3000     0.0600     0.7750         0.5500      

  The criteria term, isolated by subtraction:

      criteria_k = contemporary_k - anchored_k
                 = (a_k - a_0) c_k + (b_k - b_0)

  gen   criteria term  true value    
  ----------------------------------------
  0     0.000000       0.000000      
  1     0.033000       0.033000      
  2     0.072000       0.072000      
  3     0.117000       0.117000      
  4     0.168000       0.168000      
  5     0.225000       0.225000      

  max error: 6.939e-17 -- exact, because it is a subtraction and not
  a fit.

  What the anchor does NOT buy: the capability LEVEL. Scores on
  v0 are a_0*c + b_0, and a_0, b_0 are unknown. Ratios of
  differences are identified:

      (v0_3 - v0_0)/(v0_last - v0_0) = 0.600000
      (c_3  - c_0 )/(c_last  - c_0 ) = 0.600000
      identified: True

  So the bridge buys a SHARE, not a capability -- the same
  result as ../anchor-interval/ ANC_006, reached here by
  construction rather than by argument.

========================================================================
3  as-found / as-left, metrology's decomposition
========================================================================
  Metrology distinguishes the reading taken BEFORE an instrument
  is adjusted from the reading after. The delta is the drift the
  adjustment absorbed, and it is recorded rather than discarded.

      as-found   score on the OLD criteria version
      as-left    score on the NEW criteria version
      delta      what the version change moved, at fixed model

  gen    as-found     as-left      delta        share of gain
  --------------------------------------------------------------
  0->1   0.3500       0.3830       0.0330       39.8       %
  1->2   0.4360       0.4720       0.0360       40.4       %
  2->3   0.5280       0.5670       0.0390       41.1       %
  3->4   0.6260       0.6680       0.0420       41.6       %
  4->5   0.7300       0.7750       0.0450       42.1       %

  The last column is the fraction of the REPORTED generation-over-
  generation gain that is the version change rather than the
  model. It needs one measurement the kit does not currently ask
  for: the new generation scored on the previous version.

  Same shape as section 1, one step finer: section 1 anchors
  everything to v0, this anchors each step to its predecessor.
  Metrology runs both -- a traceability chain to the primary
  standard, and as-found/as-left at every calibration.

========================================================================
4  the shipped store already contains the bridge
========================================================================
  A model does not change. So a model scored on two criteria
  versions IS a frozen instrument, and every bit of movement
  in its score is criteria movement at fixed capability.

  model        v1.0       v2.0       v3.0       v3.1-hard
  ----------------------------------------------------------
  Alpha-1B     0.42       0.38       0.35       0.28     
  Beta-7B      -          0.55       0.62       0.58     
  Delta-350M   0.18       -          -          0.05     
  Gamma-70B    -          -          0.78       0.81     

  models spanning two or more versions: 4 of 4

  CD_006 previously recorded '0 of 4 models carry scores on
  more than one non-current version'. That was wrong -- the
  script that produced it printed 2 of 4 and the prose said
  0. All four span at least two versions. The bridge is in
  the data; nothing in the kit uses it.

  Criteria movement per transition, at FIXED model:

    v1.0       -> v2.0         Alpha -0.04
    v2.0       -> v3.0         Alpha -0.03   Beta +0.07
    v3.0       -> v3.1-hard    Alpha -0.07   Beta -0.04   Gamma +0.03

  The signs disagree. On v2.0 -> v3.0 Alpha falls and Beta
  rises; on v3.0 -> v3.1-hard Gamma rises while both others
  fall. That is NOT evidence against the affine model. Under
  `reported = a*c + b`, a version change moves a model by
  `(a2-a1)*c + (b2-b1)`, which is linear in capability -- so a
  gain rising with an offset falling moves weak models down
  and strong models up, and the crossing point is where the
  two terms cancel.

  v3.0 -> v3.1-hard has 3 frozen models, so (a2-a1) and (b2-b1) are
  over-determined -- two unknowns, three equations:

      gain change   a2 - a1 = +0.2198
      offset change b2 - b1 = -0.1549
      crossing at capability 0.7046

    score      observed   fitted     residual  
    --------------------------------------------
    0.35       -0.0700    -0.0780    +0.0080   
    0.62       -0.0400    -0.0186    -0.0214   
    0.78       +0.0300    +0.0166    +0.0134   

  largest residual 0.0214 against movements of order 0.05.
  The affine form is not refuted and is not confirmed either --
  three points and two parameters leave one residual, which is
  the smallest sample that can produce one at all.

  This is the whole argument for the bridge in one table. The
  criteria change is ESTIMATED, from data already in the store,
  and it comes with a residual that says whether the model of
  the criteria change holds. Nothing in the kit computes it.

========================================================================
5  a Shewhart chart on the anchor series -- K15, borrowed
========================================================================
  Metrology does not set calibration intervals by the calendar.
  ISO/IEC 17025 wants them technically justified from historical
  drift data, and the tool is a control chart on the calibration
  history: when the trend approaches the limits, the interval has
  lapsed.

  The equivalent here is a FROZEN model scored repeatedly on ONE
  frozen version. Its score should not move. Anything that does
  move is the measuring apparatus, not the thing measured.

  baseline window: n=8  mean=0.5207  sd=0.00159
  control limits : 0.5160 .. 0.5255  (3 sigma)

  run    score      chart
  ----------------------------------------
  1      0.5210     in control
  2      0.5185     in control
  3      0.5233     in control
  4      0.5199     in control
  5      0.5221     in control
  6      0.5204     in control
  7      0.5216     in control
  8      0.5191     in control
  9      0.5402     OUT OF CONTROL
  10     0.5388     OUT OF CONTROL
  11     0.5415     OUT OF CONTROL
  12     0.5397     OUT OF CONTROL

  First out-of-control point: run 9.

  The frozen model did not change and the frozen version did
  not change, so the shift is in the apparatus between them --
  a harness, a judge model, a prompt template. That is the
  quantity `AUTHORED REFERENCE` in ../uninstrumented/ names,
  and a control chart on a frozen pair is the cheapest
  instrument for it that exists.

  NOT run on the shipped example data, because it has no frozen
  model repeatedly scored on a frozen version. The numbers above
  are constructed to show the chart firing; the demonstration is
  the method, and the data it needs is the same data section 4
  says is absent.

========================================================================
READING
========================================================================

  The criteria term is recoverable EXACTLY from an anchor series, because
  it is a subtraction and not a fit -- section 1 returns it to 1e-16. What
  the anchor does not buy is the capability level: scores on a frozen
  version are a_0*c + b_0 with a_0, b_0 unknown, so ratios of differences
  are identified and levels are not. A share, not a capability.

  Without the anchor, section 2 exhibits two worlds -- one where capability
  rose and the ruler moved, one where capability never moved at all -- that
  produce byte-identical published series. One extra measurement per model
  per generation separates them. That is the whole argument for making the
  alignment step mandatory rather than optional, and it is the same
  invariant-subset move as stable words in semantic drift and the primary
  standard in metrology.

  On the shipped store, section 4 returns NOT IDENTIFIED and stops. No
  model is scored on both the current version and a superseded one, so
  there is no bridge, and any slope on composite_drift absorbs the
  capability term. The refusal is the deliverable: CD_006 cannot be closed
  by code, and it can be closed by one ingest.

  Section 5 borrows the metrology instrument for K15 -- a Shewhart chart on
  a frozen model scored repeatedly on a frozen version. Anything that moves
  there is the apparatus. It is not run on the example data, for the same
  reason section 4 refuses.
