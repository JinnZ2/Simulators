
DRIFT SIGN -- can the metric carry the hypothesis?
subject: DriftEngine, delivered

========================================================================
1  widening and narrowing both read as positive drift
========================================================================
  base      pass at 1 on held-out unit tests
  wider     pass at 1 on held-out unit tests plus style and documentation
  narrower  pass at 1

    base -> wider     boundary drift 0.3636  composite 0.0428
    base -> narrower  boundary drift 0.5714  composite 0.0672

  Both positive. The magnitudes differ, and the difference is a
  token-count artifact -- `narrower` deleted more words than
  `wider` added -- not a reading of direction. A narrowing that
  happened to be verbose would score below a widening that was
  terse.

========================================================================
2  the one numeric field discards its sign explicitly
========================================================================
  exemplar_count is an integer. `_numeric_drift` takes abs():

        100 -> 1000   0.9000
       1000 -> 100    0.9000
        100 -> 120    0.1667
        120 -> 100    0.1667

  100 -> 1000 and 1000 -> 100 are identical: True

  A test set growing tenfold and a test set shrinking tenfold are
  different events for the hypothesis under test, and the field
  that could report which one happened is the only field with an
  unambiguous direction available.

========================================================================
3  observer_access is an ordinal compared as a string
========================================================================
  schema.py declares the legal values, and they are ordered:

      unknown  <  partial  <  verified

  `_str_drift` compares them as token sets:

      unknown   -> partial    1.0
      partial   -> verified   1.0
      unknown   -> verified   1.0
      verified  -> unknown    1.0
      verified  -> partial    1.0

  Every transition scores 1.0. Gaining verification and losing
  it are the same number, and a one-step move is the same number
  as a two-step move.

  This is the SCALAR DEMAND mechanism from ../uninstrumented/
  turned inside out: not a function collapsed to a number, but an
  ordered scale collapsed to a nominal one. Same loss, opposite
  direction -- the structure was there and the instrument does
  not read it.

========================================================================
4  free-text fields drift on rewording
========================================================================
  A  pass at 1 on held-out unit tests
  B  on held-out unit tests, pass at 1

    boundary drift  0.2500
    composite       0.0294

  Same clause, reordered. Token Jaccard is order-free, so this is
  the words that changed rather than the order -- 'pass at 1 on
  held-out unit tests' against 'on held-out unit tests, pass at
  1' differ by punctuation attaching to a token.

  ../declared-frame/ DF_003 recorded exact string equality as
  UNDER-matching in the comparability checker -- two frames that
  mean the same thing reported as different. Here the same
  free-text field is the MEASURAND, so the same property becomes
  a positive reading on the quantity being regressed. A checker
  that under-matches refuses a comparison; a metric that
  under-matches emits drift.

========================================================================
5  what a signed metric needs, field by field
========================================================================
  field              sign          
  --------------------------------------------------------------------
  exemplar_count     available     
      drop abs(): (v2 - v1) / max(...). One character.
  observer_access    available     
      rank unknown=0, partial=1, verified=2; drift = rank2 -
      rank1.
  rubric_dimensions  available     
      |added| - |removed| rather than Jaccard distance.
  rubric_weights     available     
      sum of signed weight changes on the shared keys.
  boundary           NOT from text 
      widening vs narrowing is a semantic judgement. Needs a
      declared field -- `direction: widened|narrowed|lateral` --
      supplied by whoever made the version.
  horizon            NOT from text 
      same. A longer horizon is not obviously 'more' or 'less'.
  who_counts         NOT from text 
      same.
  sign_source        not applicable
      a change of authority has no natural direction.
  logic              not applicable
      same.

  Four of nine are signable from the data already stored, and
  three of those are one-line changes. Three more need a declared
  field, because widening and narrowing a free-text boundary is a
  judgement the text does not contain -- which is the
  ../declared-frame/ DF_007 shape: the block records declarations
  and nothing in it evaluates.

  Two are genuinely directionless and should stay unsigned, and
  saying so is worth more than forcing a sign on them.

  Until then the honest reading of a positive beta1 is:

      score changes are larger when the criteria moved a lot,
      in either direction

  which is a real finding and is not the one the README states.

========================================================================
6  repaired -- the signed metrics, alongside the unsigned ones
========================================================================
  The unsigned primitives above are unchanged: a caller that
  wants magnitude still has it. These are added, not swapped.

                                 unsigned     signed      
  ----------------------------------------------------------
  boundary widened, declared     0.2222       +0.2222
  boundary narrowed, declared    0.5714       -0.5714
  boundary changed, undeclared   0.5714       +0.0000

  exemplar 100 -> 1000           0.9000       +0.9000
  exemplar 1000 -> 100           0.9000       -0.9000
  access unknown -> verified     1.0000       +1.0000
  access verified -> unknown     1.0000       -1.0000
  access partial -> verified     1.0000       +0.5000

  An UNDECLARED direction stays 0.0. That is the load-bearing
  choice: it is not a lateral change, it is a change nobody said
  the direction of, and collapsing those two would put the
  original defect back one level down.

  composite          0.0672
  composite_signed   +0.0000
  signed_coverage    0.00

  `signed_coverage` is what stops the repair from creating a new
  version of the same problem. Without it a caller cannot tell
  'no net movement' from 'no direction declared', and a
  composite_signed near zero would mean both.

========================================================================
READING
========================================================================

  The README separates three verdicts by the sign of a slope on
  composite_drift, and every primitive in DriftEngine returns a
  non-negative distance. Widening and narrowing both push drift up;
  exemplar_count 100 -> 1000 and 1000 -> 100 are byte-identical at 0.9;
  every observer_access transition scores 1.0 including the loss of
  verification.

  So the instrument cannot distinguish the two readings it exists to
  distinguish. What a positive slope actually says is that score changes
  are larger when the criteria moved a lot, in either direction -- a real
  finding, and not the stated one.

  Four of nine fields are signable from data already in the schema, three
  of them as one-line changes. Three need a declared `direction` field,
  because whether a free-text boundary widened or narrowed is a judgement
  the text does not contain. Two have no natural direction and should stay
  unsigned.

  REPAIRED, section 6. All four signable fields now carry a sign, the
  three free-text ones take a declared `direction`, and the two with no
  natural direction stay at zero. `composite_signed` is reported beside
  `composite`, never instead of it, and `signed_coverage` says how much of
  the composite carries a direction at all -- without which a caller could
  not tell "no net change" from "nobody declared one".
