1  the drop's own worked example of BUDGET BOUNDARY
========================================================================
  The register's canonical BUDGET BOUNDARY case is the leaf vs
  panel comparison, and this drop ships both halves of it as
  declared-frame examples. No corpus, no adjudication, no
  threshold: does the scanner fire on the case its own trigger
  list was written for?

    examples/photosynthesis.json 0 candidate(s)
      result: silicon PV converts ~22% of incident photons; leaf converts 
    examples/tree.json          0 candidate(s)
      result: tree sustains conversion, self-repair, self-replication, loa

  Zero. Now the same case in four phrasings:

    the register's own VISIBLE AS line   SCORED AS WASTE ('inefficient')
    the delivered result string          NO HIT
    stated as a comparative              BUDGET BOUNDARY ('more efficient than')
    stated with the noun                 BUDGET BOUNDARY ('conversion efficiency')

  Two results, and the second is the sharper one.

  (a) The delivered result string does not fire. The trigger list
      catches the RHETORIC of a comparison -- 'more efficient
      than', 'outperforms', 'orders of magnitude better' -- and
      not the comparison. Two numbers placed side by side with no
      comparative is how the claim usually appears in a result
      line, and that form is invisible to every BUDGET BOUNDARY
      trigger.

  (b) The register's own phrasing of the case fires under the
      WRONG mechanism: 'inefficient' is a SCORED AS WASTE trigger.
      A reader triaging that hit is handed the wrong `check`
      question -- 'what does it return, and on what interval' --
      for a case whose actual question is 'are both budgets closed
      at the same boundary'.

  Neither is fatal to the design, and both are cheap: BUDGET
  BOUNDARY needs a trigger for the bare-numbers form (a percentage
  or ratio within N tokens of a second one for a different
  object), and the mechanisms need to be allowed to co-fire, which
  is uninstrumented/ UNI_003 arriving in the scanner.

2  triage load -- the quantity the design actually turns on
========================================================================
  patterns.json states its own standard: 'Every hit is a candidate
  for triage, not a finding.' Under that standard precision is not
  the binding constraint -- how many `check` questions a human has
  to answer per unit of text is.

  corpus: 202 markdown files, 309659 words
    with word boundaries    314 candidates  1.0 per 1000 words
    without (--raw)         431 candidates  1.4 per 1000 words
    cost of dropping \b    +117 candidates (+37%)

  About one candidate per 1000 words. That is a workable triage
  load -- a 5000-word document arrives with roughly five questions
  attached, not fifty. The trigger list is written tightly enough
  that the human step it depends on is affordable.

  Densest files:

    inverseminar/README.md                             776 w   12 hits   15.5 /1000
    declared-frame/v2/README.md                       1480 w   19 hits   12.8 /1000
    msiaf-framework/proxies/proxy-catalog.md           351 w    3 hits    8.5 /1000
    msiaf-framework/models/warehouse-architecture.md   562 w    4 hits    7.1 /1000

3  use-mention: a checked expectation that failed
========================================================================
  Expected before running: a scanner keyed on surface words
  cannot distinguish a document EXHIBITING a failure from one
  DESCRIBING it, so documents about the mechanisms should light
  up entirely with false positives.

    uninstrumented/README.md                   986 w   2 hits   2.0 /1000
        ['AUTHORED REFERENCE', 'SCORED AS WASTE']
    uninstrumented/CLAIM_TABLE.md              698 w   1 hits   1.4 /1000
        ['AUDIT ASYMMETRY']
    declared-frame/v2/FRAME.md                 246 w   0 hits   0.0 /1000
    declared-frame/README.md                   861 w   0 hits   0.0 /1000

  It did not happen. The document that names all seven mechanisms
  and glosses each one returns 2 candidates in 986 words, and the
  v2 frame document returns none.

  Why: the triggers are written as the vocabulary of the FAILING
  document, not as the vocabulary of the mechanism. 'apparatus in
  the wrong channel' is how the register names MODALITY; 'failed
  to demonstrate' is what a paper exhibiting it says. The two
  vocabularies barely overlap, and that separation is doing more
  work than the trigger list looks like it is doing.

  4 triggers produce 178 of 314 candidates (57%).
  38 of 69 triggers never fire on 309659 words.

  Both numbers are CORPUS-CONDITIONAL and neither grades the
  trigger list. SCALAR DEMAND is 7 of 8 triggers silent because
  this corpus contains no survey instruments -- 'on a scale of',
  'rate yourself', 'composite score' are psychometrics vocabulary
  and there is none here. A silent trigger on the wrong corpus is
  not a dead trigger.

  The concentration is corpus-conditional in the other direction
  and worth naming precisely, because it is the shape of every
  false positive this corpus produces:

    `(unverified|uncorroborated)` fires 52 times, almost all on
    the string UNVERIFIED -- a claim-table STATUS CODE in this
    repo, not a hedge attached to an account. The trigger is
    correct and the corpus is adversarial to it.

    `slack` fires 49 times on a mix of Slack the product, 'the
    slack rope', a code identifier, and genuine idle-capacity
    usage. This one is the trigger's own problem: a four-letter
    common noun with a proper-noun homograph.

  Which is why no precision number appears in this file. Getting
  one requires a corpus that is not about its own subject, and
  every corpus in this repository is.

========================================================================
READING
========================================================================

  The one result that needs no corpus: the scanner returns ZERO on both
  halves of the drop's own worked example of BUDGET BOUNDARY, and the
  register's own phrasing of that case fires under SCORED AS WASTE
  instead. The triggers catch the rhetoric of a comparison and not the
  comparison, and two numbers side by side -- the usual form -- is
  invisible to all eight BUDGET BOUNDARY triggers.

  Triage load is the quantity the design turns on, by its own statement,
  and it is low: about one candidate per 1000 words across ~300k. Dropping
  word boundaries costs roughly 40% more candidates for no obvious gain.
  Exact counts are in section 2 and move with the corpus.

  The expectation that use-mention would dominate was checked and did not
  hold. Documents describing the mechanisms return almost nothing, because
  the triggers are written in the vocabulary of the failing document
  rather than the vocabulary of the mechanism. That separation is load-
  bearing and is not visible from reading the file.

  No precision figure is reported. This repository is a corpus about
  measurement failure written in the triggers' own vocabulary --
  UNVERIFIED is a status code here -- so a false-positive rate scored on
  it would measure the corpus. That measurement needs an outside corpus
  and is the next thing to run.

  And the corpus is live. Writing the README that reports sections 1-4
  moved five triggers from never-firing to firing, three of them SCALAR
  DEMAND triggers that section 4 calls silent for want of survey
  instruments -- now firing on the document that says so. The counts here
  are a snapshot, not a fixture.
