# WORK ORDER — removal_closure

License: CC0. Status: OPEN, UNFUNDED, UNGATED. Pre-registered before data collection.
Subject to the method it proposes.

Scope note: this is a work order about a METHOD — how claims of environmental
coupling reach closure — not about any particular constant. No mechanism is
proposed here and no coupling is asserted. Every constant below is a measured
physical quantity with a published literature.

## CLAIM UNDER TEST

    H1  a proposed coupling between an environmental constant and a biological
        rhythm reaches scientific closure only after a REMOVAL demonstration
        (the rhythm persists, or changes measurably, when the constant is
        withdrawn or altered). Correlation volume does not predict closure.

    H2  time-to-closure is predicted by REMOVABILITY of the constant, not by
        effect size, funding, or citation count.

    H3  constants that cannot be removed under available technique have no
        step-2 protocol, and their literature stalls at correlation indefinitely
        — producing an absence of evidence generated by method, not by world.

    H0  closure tracks correlation volume, effect size, or funding; removability
        adds no variance.

## FALSIFICATION

    H1 FALSE if  >= 1 closed row closed WITHOUT a removal or alteration demonstration
    H2 FALSE if  rank(removability) does not track rank(time-to-closure) across rows
    H3 FALSE if  a non-removable constant has reached step 4 (transducer named)
    Negative results are results. Post them.

## THE LADDER  (coding scheme)

    1  behaviour correlates with the constant                    (usually dismissed)
    2  rhythm persists / shifts when the constant is removed     (endogeneity shown)
    3  an organism where the effect is undeniable
    4  transducer named
    5  gene / molecule identified

## PROCEDURE

    P1  rows: >= 12 constant-organism claims spanning all 5 steps, including
        closed, open, and refuted cases. Refuted cases are required — a table of
        winners only measures survivorship.
    P2  code removability BEFORE looking at closure year (pre-register the coding)
    P3  cross-tabs by hand: step_reached × removability, × dismissal_recorded;
        Cramér's V. Time-to-closure by removability rank.
    P4  publish CSV + script + table, versioned

## OUTPUT

    constant | organism | step | removability | removal_method | years_to_closure | transducer

## THE MISSING PROTOCOL  (what H3 predicts is absent, stated so it can be built)

    For a non-removable constant, step 2 requires either:
      (a) a shielded enclosure that attenuates the constant across the relevant
          band, at duration long enough for a free-running rhythm to drift; or
      (b) a natural gradient wide enough to act as a quasi-removal (latitude,
          depth, cave, orbit); or
      (c) an amplitude-tracking design: the constant varies on a known schedule;
          a coupled rhythm should track that variation.
    Row 5 has no (a) at duration in the literature. (b) and (c) are cheap and
    have not been systematically tried.

## ATTACHED GAPS — comparative rhythm measurement

    These came out of reading Burchardt, Paschen & Fuchs 2026, Ann NYAS 1563(1)
    e70383 (DOI 10.1111/nyas.70383), repo:
    github.com/LSBurchardt/Rhythm_across_48_languages. The dataset is open and the
    method restriction ("only studies using the same methodology") is stated and
    correct. These are open questions for anyone with the CSV, not corrections.

    G1  LEVEL MATCHING. The published comparison places a ~2 s human interval
        beside animal mean IOIs. In the human pipeline, word-level IOI (~0.3 s)
        and pause-bounded speech run (IPU, ~2 s) are two different quantities.
        Question: for each animal row, which level was measured — the inner
        pulse or the outer bout? A cross-species comparison needs the level
        stated per row.

    G2  PAUSE THRESHOLD. The IPU split depends on a pause threshold not stated
        in the repo README. Question: does the ~2 s figure move with the
        threshold? Rebuild IPUs at 0.15 / 0.25 / 0.35 / 0.50 s and report the
        mean at each. If it moves, the number describes the cut.

    G3  DISTRIBUTION SHAPE. A mean does not distinguish a breath-bounded run
        (long right tail, hard ceiling) from a clocked window (symmetric, tight).
        Report per language: median, mode in 0.05 s bins, CV, and the right-tail
        ratio (p95 − median) / (median − p05).

    G4  RATIO AS THE INVARIANT. If word IOI ~0.3 s nests inside IPU ~2 s, that is
        ~6-7 units per window. Question: is the cross-language invariant the
        duration, or the ratio? Report IOI:IPU per language. Then ask, per animal
        row, whether the two published values for one species (where they exist)
        are the same two levels.

    G5  BODY-SIZE PROXY. The repo carries country mean male height as a covariate.
        Corpus speakers are not sampled from country means; the chain is
        country → mean height → lung volume → speaker. Question: does the proxy
        carry any variance at all? If not, the physiological explanation is
        resting on a decorative covariate.

    G6  GENERATOR INDEPENDENCE. The species named in the comparison do not share a
        sound-production mechanism — lungs, swim bladder, syrinx. Question: if a
        window holds across generators that differ this much, what is the shared
        term? Candidates to separate: producer constraint, receiver integration
        window, cost-of-a-burst on the local fuel supply. These make different
        predictions about which level is conserved.

    G7  FREQUENCY PLACEMENT (connects to the main table). Human syllable rate
        (4-8 Hz) and several bat IOIs (8-33 Hz) fall in bands that overlap known
        environmental periodicities; the ~2 s window (~0.5 Hz) does not. Any
        coupling question therefore concerns the INNER clock, not the outer
        window. Stated here to keep the two levels from being merged again.

## KNOWN LIMITS

    - historical rows are coded from secondary literature; dates are approximate
      and should be sourced individually
    - survivorship: closed rows are easier to find than abandoned ones; the
      refuted-case requirement in P1 is the partial fix, not a complete one
    - "removability" is a judgment coded by the authors; pre-register it and
      publish the coding rules so it can be re-coded by others
    - the attached gaps are questions about an open dataset, raised without having
      run the code; some may be answered inside the scripts
