========================================================================
2  REGIME II -- downstream provenance: sampling longer is inert
========================================================================
  Pool is a mix. Fraction f of samples came through the system
  being corrected and carry its bias (0.35 here); the rest are
  independent. Averaging removes the variance, not the bias.

  n       f=0.00      f=0.10      f=0.30      f=0.60      f=1.00      
  --------------------------------------------------------------------
  10      0.3580      0.3580      0.2180      0.1830      0.0080      
  100     0.0470      0.0260      0.0510      0.1595      0.3030      
  1000    0.0289      0.0594      0.1290      0.2368      0.3789      
  10000   0.0068      0.0425      0.1140      0.2196      0.3568      
  100000  0.0009      0.0359      0.1062      0.2105      0.3509      
  floor   0.0000      0.0350      0.1050      0.2100      0.3500      

  Every column with f > 0 converges to f x bias and stops. The
  10,000x increase in sample count between the first and last row
  buys nothing once the floor is reached.

  Sample count needed to reach an error of 0.05:
    f          n required      
    ------------------------------
    0.00       400             
    0.10       785             
    0.14       10102           
    0.30       unreachable at any n
    0.60       unreachable at any n
    1.00       unreachable at any n

  Above f = 0.143 the target is not slow to reach. It is outside
  the reachable set, and no schedule changes that.

========================================================================
3  the measurement that decides which regime you are in
========================================================================
  ONE number separates them:

      f = fraction of the re-acquisition pool that is downstream
          of the system being corrected

  and it is a provenance audit, not a statistical estimate. It is
  answered by labelling the pool, not by analysing it.

  Verdict column is against a STATED tolerance of 0.05, which is
  a choice and not a fact. Change the tolerance and the boundary
  moves; the floor does not.

  f          error floor            at tolerance 0.05             
  ----------------------------------------------------------------
  0.00       0.0000                 retrain remedy is sound       
  0.02       0.0070                 retrain remedy is sound       
  0.10       0.0350                 retrain remedy is sound       
  0.35       0.1225                 irrecoverability holds        
  0.70       0.2450                 irrecoverability holds        

  So the contradiction resolves into a precondition nobody states.

    If f is near zero, K15 (baseline_freshness, in
    ../measurement-fork/) collapses into an ops step and the
    mediation prediction that rests on it fails.

    If f is not near zero, the retraining remedy has a hidden
    precondition. f is not among the quantities the remedy asks
    you to report, so a pipeline can satisfy every published step
    of it and still sit above the floor. Whether any given paper
    reports f is a literature question and is NOT checked here --
    the source drop's citation markers are unresolvable as
    delivered, so no literature claim in this folder is verified.

  Both sides lose something on a measurement neither has run.

  Second axis, separate from f and NOT settled by it: the
  irrecoverability position also asserts no stable plateau is
  guaranteed -- change is continuous, or the next shift arrives
  before the sample completes. That is section 1's t_shift, and
  it is a timing argument. f decides whether timing is even the
  right axis. Only if f is near zero does t_shift get to matter.

========================================================================
READING
========================================================================

  The drift literature's remedy and the irrecoverability claim contradict
  each other only if they are read as claims about one regime. They are
  claims about two, and the regimes are separated by a single measurable
  quantity -- the downstream fraction f of the re-acquisition pool.

  In REGIME I the argument is entirely about scheduling: the optimal
  acquisition length is interior, finite, and moves with the shift
  interval.

  In REGIME II scheduling is inert. Error floors at f x bias, the floor is
  independent of sample count, and above f = 0.14 a 0.05 target is outside
  the reachable set at any n. Recovery is by re-grounding on a reference
  the system did not generate -- which is the anchor interval in
  corpus_loop.py, arrived at from the other end.

  f is a provenance audit, answered by labelling the pool. Nobody reports
  it, and both positions have something to lose depending on how it comes
  back.
