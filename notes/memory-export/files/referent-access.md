MARKER, not a position under defense. Uncoalesced.

## Origin

An elders' saying: **a community working together is strongest when the common goal is outside
the community.**

## Versions of the same cut

- **Relationship:** partner-with-someone-toward-a-goal versus the STATUS of the relationship
  being the goal. When status is the goal, the frame shifts to what others see, and everything
  becomes internal drama rather than the exterior goal.
- **Historical:** founding-era and immigrant groups had an exterior goal, and the goal was
  energy-intensive enough that division of labor FELL OUT of it and worked.
- Generalizes to institutions, governance systems, corporations, nation states. Something
  there, not coalesced.

## Apparent counterexamples that fail

- **Hospice and monastery both have external referents.** Hospice: families critiquing care,
  regulations, funders. Monastery: denomination and religion.
- **Hospice gradient depends on who pays** — patient, outside party, or a representative
  closely tied to the internal. Many hospices do not care for patients in a way the patient
  would qualify as consistent. Rules, regulations, and the funding state are all external
  markers.
- **Monastery:** how tightly coupled to the external church's values at a given moment, and
  when it was last tightly coupled, may explain variance rates and why some monasteries went
  their own way. It monitors itself as a group, plus visitors, plus its religion.

**Open question:** is the monastery's reference the state of the CHURCH or the state of the
TEXT? Reference point location matters.

## Coupling rate

Discordance between coupling events — papal visit rate vs text cycling rate vs church cycling
rate — differs from CONTINUOUS external monitoring (social media, friends, family judging a
couple), which produces constant emotional up-and-down on value judgments that shift post to
post.

Related split: focal-on-pair with OUTSIDE OPINION as referent behaves very differently from
focal-on-pair with the PARTNER'S OWN VIEW as referent, because the references differ.

Open: whether there is a gradient with certain failure rates, rather than a binary flip.

## Primary cut

**Does the referent have DIRECT ACCESS to the quantity, or is it reading a REPRESENTATION?**

This cuts across the inside/outside line, which is why inside/outside produced the false
counterexamples above.

Secondary terms: coupling strength; referent update rate; sampling rate.
