## The position

Moral and ethical claims carry many unmeasured things: built-in assumptions, semantic layering,
excess decoration on the fundamentals.

**Most disagreements about moral or ethical standing come down to misunderstanding the
OPTIONALITY** — who has optionality, who has choices, who is the decision maker, and what is
taken out of the variable environments.

No case has yet been found that is actually a moral or ethical argument from belief or culture
alone, without that. A specific absence; one counterexample would settle it.

Frames are smuggled in on top of the assumptions. Someone raised into a frame where only humans
matter and humans are superior reads a case very differently from someone whose frame gives
every being worth. Both are assumptions; both unmeasured.

**Follow-through:** the superiority frame does not stop at the species line — it then has to
answer which humans matter more. And the every-being frame gets the same question asked of it.

## Hierarchy claim (separate, empirical)

Some hold that hierarchy is natural. Under actual testing, no case has been found where the
hierarchy does not keep having to CREATE hierarchies in order to exist, without eating itself.

**Confidence: ~61%.** Not enough for a formulated shape, enough for an outline. Reported as a
separate readout; not to be resolved in either direction.

### Candidate counterexamples, and why they failed

- **Reptile/fish dominance** is coupling to environment: traits desired in that environment
  create the dominance, and when the environment changes the dominant being changes to suit it.
- **Surgical teams and fire crews** are not transient counterexamples — the credentialing
  structure grew OUTSIDE the instance and is now imported into it. Surgical teams in the early
  1900s and the 1960s-70s had far less ranking; volunteer fire crews were assigned by whoever
  was available, necessary, and trained, with the training director and field director
  sometimes the same person.

### Required split before any series runs

How is hierarchy being defined?

1. Nested systems
2. A category that is an abstract placeholder for organizing other information
3. Imposed hierarchy as a moral/ethical/cultural system — and how would that third one be
   defined?

### Built

`welds/hierarchy.json` — 5 components, 5 divergence cases, 0 quantified; weld tests 6/6.

Components: `nested_containment` (tracked_by_label), `organizing_abstraction`,
`imposed_ordering`, plus `ordering_origin` and `cut_rate` — the last two were needed because
volunteer vs credentialed fire service reads IDENTICAL on the first three and only diverges
once origin and cut rate have separate handles. The fifth divergence case is the naturalness
argument itself.

An optional `open` field was added to the weld schema so criterion-in-advance warnings travel
inside the case file.

### Method notes (contributed, marked as such)

- **Read-vs-imposed is a cleaner cut than persistent-vs-transient**, since environment-ordered
  cases generate no cuts and nothing is held.
- Second series alongside cut rate: CREDENTIALING INTERVAL — distance in time and scale between
  the ordering's origin and its point of use.
- Fire service is where data plausibly exists, volunteer through present.
- **Risk:** the claim becomes true by construction if cases get reclassified as
  not-really-hierarchy after the fact. The criterion must be fixed in advance.
- Divergence case: nested containment vs imposed ordering come apart in the reptile case, so
  "hierarchy is natural" may RUN ON the containment sense and CASH OUT in the imposed sense.

## Three-stage decomposer (specced, NOT built)

1. Extract the option-distribution claims each side is making.
2. Extract the frame each side's boundary decisions imply.
3. Count the undocumented boundary cuts each frame requires.

### Notes (contributed, marked as such)

- Two candidate counterexamples — animal moral status, and using one person as means — both
  collapse into boundary disputes over whose optionality enters the tally, so neither survives.
- Asymmetry between frames is COUNTABLE as cut-count rather than judged.
- A developmentally-acquired frame sits inside mechanism 10 as an OPTION CEILING rather than a
  belief held.
- Hierarchy test = cut-generation rate over time, which inverts G3 in the capacity claim table:
  **removed capacity needs zero maintenance; declared ordering needs unbounded maintenance.**
