## Core cut

**The searchable unit is the SHAPE, not the vocabulary.** Cross-domain matches are missed by
construction when a tool keys on terms.

**Premise:** recurrence across domains is not analogy. Where both domains are downstream of the
same physical constraint, the ADMISSIBLE GEOMETRY is the same — names come from the field,
shape comes from the constraint.

**Against the "you're just pattern matching" objection:** "it doesn't hold across domains" is
usually a NULL SEARCH OVER ONE VOCABULARY, not an absence of the structure. The stronger the
physics rooting, the more the shape recurs under other names.

**Open question:** holographic, fractal, and branching-directory descriptions may be one shape
sampled at different resolutions.

## Entry — RECOVERABLE-DIALECT SUBSTITUTION

**Signature:** two systems share a vocabulary, but one has a SETTLEMENT PATH after failure and
one does not. Terms carrying an implicit recoverability premise — margin, risk, buffer,
efficiency, optimization, acceptable loss — get imported into the terminal-failure system with
the premise intact and INVISIBLE, because no word changed.

**Detection test:** take the term and ask what survives to use the settlement. If nothing does,
the term is carrying a premise the system does not satisfy.

### Instances

- **Margin as buffer stock** vs **margin as slack in a coupled hard-limited control system.**
  In the second, cutting margin REDISTRIBUTES LOAD across coupled axes and obligates accounting
  on each. It does not degrade the operation, and it never touches the fixed safety-critical
  terms.
- **Insured industrial farming** (crop failure = late mortgage, seed financing, next year
  happens) vs **subsistence gardening** (failure TERMINATES the system that would retry).

**Consequence:** a listener infers "cut margin = took shortcuts," which is sound in the
recoverable dialect and false in the other.

### Why this recurs in communication

Standard English usage IS the recoverable dialect — developed in and for insured, settleable,
retryable systems. Someone operating in unrecoverable systems speaks the unrecoverable dialect
with the same words.
