## Field observation (longitudinal operator report)

- Rest area truck side is safer than truck stops on BOTH axes — property/theft AND person.
  Not a tradeoff.
- Boundary on the claim: truck side only, cab only. No claim made about the car side of
  rest areas.
- A truck stop concentrates anyone whose activity requires trucks — cargo theft,
  solicitation, property crime. The concentration that reads as safety is the same
  concentration that makes the site worth working.
- Truck stops carry heavy car and retail traffic: anonymous entry, no standing required,
  no dwell floor. The rest area truck side is the MORE screened lot of the two — the reverse
  of how it is usually described.
- Movement risk at truck stops: dense maneuvering, a high proportion of drivers in their
  first months or under training, pedestrians between trailers. Being struck on foot in the
  lot is routine exposure, not an edge case. A rest area is a parking geometry, not a
  traffic geometry.
- Dead spots get staked out and triangulated by people targeting trucks.
- This read accrues only through years on the road. New operators read amenities and lighting
  and conclude "safe."

## The two terms the literature does not have

1. **Jurisdiction / standing.** Rest area = public property; enforcement enters without cause.
   Truck stop = private commercial; response is by operator request, and the operator has a
   revenue reason not to call.
2. **Incidental use, not assigned patrol.** Officers along a corridor stop at rest areas
   because they need the restroom — all hours, no route, no period. A dedicated patrol has an
   exploitable schedule; incidental use has none. The deterrent is UNPREDICTABILITY of
   presence, and the cheapest source of it is a facility the enforcing party personally uses.
   Not purchasable. Absent from CPTED, which is built-environment: sightlines, lighting,
   territoriality.

## Camera endogeneity

- Cameras get installed where crime already concentrated. Camera presence is a marker of prior
  incident rate, not a treatment. Every before/after study selects sites on the outcome
  variable — which is a candidate explanation for the small and null published effects.
- Read forward as advice ("stop where the cameras are"), this inverts the signal: the
  observable is a scar, not a shield.
- Rest areas have minimal cameras sized to officer and facility protection — the same signal
  read correctly. Nothing forced an escalation.
- Second-order: a camera defines its own complement, and the complement is stable. Camera plus
  no patrol means the unwatched geometry is mappable and permanent. Installation publishes
  the gaps.

## Correction on the natural-surveillance read

The intuition "more ambient population = protection" fails here on its own source: roughly 20%
of documented cargo incidents occur at truck stops and fuel stations. The natural-surveillance
literature assumes bystanders are uncorrelated with the threat; at a truck stop they are
screened FOR the target.

## Queued measurement

- **Incidents per truck-night by facility type**, four bins: property/vehicle, cargo theft,
  person-crime, struck-by/pedestrian. THE DENOMINATOR IS THE HARD PART — everything published
  is incidents per site, which ranks big sites dangerous and says nothing per night spent
  there.
- Parking counts per site: state DOT truck parking inventories / Jason's Law data.
- Incidents by address: local law enforcement records. Never joined to the above.
- Jurisdiction/entry term: public vs private, consent-required or not. Free from property
  records.
- Incidental patrol proxy: corridor structure — which facilities sit on patrol routes with
  restrooms.
- Camera presence as COVARIATE with install date, never as treatment.
- Pedestrian exposure: from lot geometry and maneuvering density.

First step: pull the Jason's Law parking inventory and test how far the address-level join
goes.

## Exposure window — cab-closed vs cab-open

A separate quantity from facility risk.

- The walk to the shower is the exposure. The cab boundary is only open on exit and re-entry.
  A rest area with no amenities generates no such trip if the operator stays in the cab: part
  of the truck stop's amenity value is what generates the exposure.
- Cost bin the incident record does not hold: a lot backing incident costs hours down —
  clock, not property. Repair time and reschedule appear nowhere except as a repair figure.
- MISSING DENOMINATOR: incidents per exit-and-return event. Everything published is per driver
  or per site. Per-exposure-event is the form that would show a facility you never leave
  beating a facility with a shower.
- Published advice converts the walk into a lighting recommendation — park under lights, near
  the front, avoid the back of the lot. The purchasable-observable reflex again.
- Routing cost is real and lands untagged: risk gets paid in refused work, and shows up in
  turnover statistics as attrition with no cause attached.

## Isolation vs targeting — a confound in the perception data

Surveys asking "where do you feel least safe" score truck stops and rest areas nearly equal,
while incident-location data separates them hard. Read: the perception question measures
ISOLATION, and isolation reads as threat. But isolation is a signal-to-noise property, not a
risk level.

- Low ambient activity → anything that moves is information.
- High ambient activity → nothing that moves is information; every approach is masked by
  legitimate approaches.
- Discomfort at a remote facility is about absence of backup — a separate variable from being
  targeted. The instruments stack both into one score, and policy runs off the stacked number
  because that is the one collected with both facilities in the same question.

## Third term — sentinel habitat at the parking area

- Facility preference can be INSTRUMENT-AVAILABILITY driven rather than category judgment:
  rest areas preferred even over rural truck stops because the ambient alert network
  (birds, insects, frogs, small mammals) is most abundant there, and calibration to it is
  lifelong. See the borrowed-instrument entries in [[unnamed-instruments]].
- SCREENING RULE in use: rest areas that are not wildlife friendly are avoided.
- This is a third facility term alongside jurisdiction and incidental patrol, and the cheapest
  one to compute. A rest area mowed to the pavement edge, fully lit, no cover, no water sits
  on public land with the same patrol term as any other and still does not work — nothing
  living there to report.
- COUNTABLE AT NATIONAL SCALE, FREE: vegetated edge and canopy within a few hundred metres of
  the truck lot, from NLCD land-cover or aerial imagery, joined to the parking inventory. No
  survey, no incident records required. Produces a facility ranking nobody has — not lighting,
  not cameras, not amenities, but SENTINEL HABITAT PER FACILITY.
- Facility gradient for the instrument: rest area with intact habitat > rural truck stop (few
  spaces, synanthropic species, displaced baseline) > large-chain truck stop (network killed —
  noise, constant in-and-out, diesel volatiles saturating the olfactory threat channel).
