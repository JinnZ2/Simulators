MARKER, not a position under defense. Test fit, extend, or report where it breaks.

## Study under audit

- Nature 2026, "Sleep chart of biological ageing clocks in middle and late life"; UK Biobank, ages 37–84
- 23 biological ageing clocks from in vivo imaging, plasma proteomics, metabolomics; nine brain and body systems
- U-shaped relation between self-reported sleep duration and biological age gaps; lowest gaps between 6.4 and 7.8 h
- Inflection point varies by organ and by sex — the band is an aggregate of many minima, not one number
- Sleep duration is SELF-REPORTED; design is cross-sectional

## Gaps identified

- **G-CONTAINER** — duration treated as one thing; no distinction between consolidated and
  interrupted sleep. Eight hours with five awakenings and eight consolidated are the same
  data point.
- **G-SPAN** — asked "how long did you sleep," respondents answer with the span, not itemised
  segments. Error is systematically LARGER at the long end, which is where fragmented sleepers
  pile up. Part of "long sleep is bad" could be "fragmented sleep reported as long sleep."
- **G-CYCLES** — is this measuring amount of sleep, how sleep is categorized, or REM cycles?
  Cycles ~90 min with deeper stages front-loaded, so wake timing within the night matters and
  does not survive a single number.
- **G-PARITY** — parous vs nulliparous comparison, split by cycles rather than hours, never
  run. Biobank holds parity, so it is answerable with existing data. If mothers cluster
  fragmented-but-not-ageing-faster, the fragmentation-equals-damage read breaks.
- **G-DIRECTION** — cross-sectional, so poor health lengthening sleep is equally consistent
  with the data.

## Open read

- If the waking were not functional or adapted, why does the body still do it?
- Fish-and-water limit: an observer cannot read frequency off their own experience, because
  their sense of normal was set by a sample that all does it. The same limit applies to the
  researchers' sample — only theirs got written down as the reference range.
