## The pattern

Adversarial structure gets demanded as an ENTRY REQUIREMENT for collective cognition, with no
independent justification. Instance in the literature: plant collective root behavior rejected
as swarm intelligence on the grounds that plant "individuals" have no genetic conflict, unlike
insect colonies.

## Reframe — the constraint sits in the environment

The same resource distribution reads as competition or as allocation depending on where the
observer stands. Anything pushing against an environmental constraint looks adversarial
through an adversarial prior.

Zero-sum framing is continuous with the brain-in-a-jar move: adversarial structure requires
discrete bounded agents with separable interests, which is the same atomization that puts a
controller in each unit and the environment outside all of them.

## Contested is a STATE, not an IDENTITY

Hummingbirds contest a flower with pollen and sit in the same tree together once it is not in
play. Same birds, different relation, depending on whether the resource is currently active.
A framework that assigns "competitor" as a standing property cannot represent this.

## Field observations (resident position, multi-year, not visitation)

- Ant colonies do not go looking for each other. On contact they test boundaries and pick off
  individual members rather than going to battle. Usually resolves as one colony conceding
  the contested thing and moving to another part of the field.
- Wolves give up a piece of older territory to acquire another.
- Solo male wolves raise other animals and partner with other species.
- Net read: far more cooperation and boundary-testing than adversarial relation.

## Methodological point — the encounter denominator

Researchers writing this are typically not living among it, not in active participation.
Field observation is EVENT-SAMPLED: encounters get recorded, null time does not. So the
denominator is missing and the framework gets built on the tail.

**Measurable version:** what fraction of encounter-time is actually contested? A small
fraction plus a framework describing the system as competitive means the framework is built
on the rare case.

Same denominator problem as [[facility-risk-index]] — and again, resident position vs visitor
position.

## Buck rut

Presented as adversarial every year, but the lethal approach is available and not used — no
going for fatal organs. Older bucks are not actively killing younger bucks.

## YEARLING ANTLER CALIBRATION

Distinct from the literature's reading. First-year bucks are not actively in procreation; they
are LEARNING — building proprioception for new hardware, getting the ratio and mass
distribution of the antlers on their head correct. **The contest is the training surface, not
the point.**

The literature frames the same behavior as low-rank competition. Losing has no skill curve
attached, so nothing gets measured.

Analogy: it is like a child learning to walk. How would you test your legs if you don't?

### Proposed natural experiment — most immediately runnable

Deer raised or supplemented with human food never test their antlers in year one, so by year
two they start backwards and are not at peer level.

- Design: fed/rehabilitated cohort vs wild control
- Measured on: second-year antler-handling competence at equal mass, NOT on rank
- Prediction: calibration predicts a MOTOR deficit, not a social one
- Data path: rehab facilities generate this cohort as a byproduct, and release records exist

### Discriminator for the general case

If yearling engagement is competition, it should track access to does. If it is calibration,
it should track antler growth and drop off once the model is built, regardless of rank
prospects.

## Doe choice

Multi-year observation: does follow a small-stub-antlered buck and move away from a
larger-antlered buck across the field — choosing the male that apparently knows it will not
win the contest. Not what the antler-rank model predicts.

Female choice in cervids is thinly studied next to male-male contest. The received framing
treats the doe as the resource being contested rather than as an agent selecting, so this
observation is exactly what does not get sampled.

## Literature context (retrieved, re-verify before building)

- Basal cognition literature decomposes cognition into a toolkit — sensing, memory, valence,
  learning, decision-making, communication — each operationalized independently across
  prokaryotes, single-celled eukaryotes, plants, animals. Partial fingerprint-stripping
  already done; no summit and no center required.
- Swarm cognition treats cognition as a distributed self-organizing phenomenon.
- The motor-training hypothesis is ALREADY VALIDATED in cervids at the fawn-play stage (play
  peaks at or under 3 weeks, timed to cerebellar development). Established in the taxon, never
  extended to yearling sparring — where new hardware is grown annually.
- Deer literature is management-framed: antler size as heritable trait, nutrition/genetics/age,
  spike-buck harvest policy. Sparring is described as behavior and its function assigned to
  dominance. Nothing on skill acquisition.
- Genetic paternity work finds yearlings and young bucks siring a substantial share of fawns
  (some studies around one third) against a dominant-buck model predicting near-zero. **Model
  wrong by a third of offspring; correction available since the genetic work landed; model
  kept.**
- Harvest policy is built on the antler-rank model — cull spikes, protect big racks. If the
  model is wrong, the applied selection pressure is not the intended one. That is the version
  with a number and a budget attached.
