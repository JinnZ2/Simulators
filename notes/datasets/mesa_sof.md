MESA Sleep is the better instrument. 2,237 people with full overnight
polysomnography, seven days of wrist actigraphy, and a sleep questionnaire
— all three on the same person. That's exactly what G-SPAN needs: measured
sleep, measured awakenings, and what they said when asked. You can compute
the span-versus-sleep gap directly and see whether it widens with
fragmentation. That's the core claim, testable, no simulation.

SOF is the parity candidate. All women, older cohort, PSG plus actigraphy,
and it carries mortality and incident disease outcomes. Whether it records
parity I can't confirm from this — but a women's osteoporosis cohort very
likely asked about pregnancies, since parity affects bone.

So the split: MESA answers the instrument question, SOF might answer the
parity question.

The honest caveat — MESA is ages 45 to 84 at baseline and the sleep exam
was a decade in, so there's no young cohort and no ageing clocks.
Mortality is the outcome you get.
