Right. Here's the schema, spoken — and Claude Code can take it from here.

Every claim record carries seven fields. One: the assertion itself, stated without hedges, since hedges are what get shed. Two: the measurement — the actual observation, with its interval, not a point. Three: the instrument, meaning what produced the measurement and what its known error characteristics are. Four: the domain of validity — the conditions under which it was measured, which is the field that always gets stripped. Five: the clock — the timescale over which the claim is expected to hold, plus a next-check date, and no claim without one. Six: the derivation, meaning parent claim identifiers, so the load path is walkable upward. Seven: the collapse record — if a distribution was reduced to a point, which point and why, the upper-quartile field.

Two hard rules. A claim with an unresolvable parent doesn't validate. And no field is optional, because optional is how the domain of validity disappeared in the first place.
