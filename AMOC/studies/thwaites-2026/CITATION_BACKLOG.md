# Thwaites and AMOC: multilingual citation backlog

**Search cutoff: 23 September 2026. Status: citations for later investigation, not completed simulator analyses.**

The search found **three papers published in September 2026 beyond the original attached audit**, but all three are written in **English**. No relevant paper actually written in Chinese, Japanese, German, French, or Spanish and first published online during **1–23 September 2026** was verified. That is a limited search result, not evidence that such papers do not exist.

Non-English institutional and news sources remain useful discovery routes. The entries below distinguish **article language, article publication date, and coverage language/date**. Three older 2026 papers are retained separately because they are useful for Thwaites and Atlantic Meridional Overturning Circulation (**AMOC**) work.

**Repository overlap:** While this search ran, `Simulators/AMOC/research.md` was added with Kasuya and Nian already cited. Those two are retained here for verified metadata and follow-up, not counted as new discoveries for that note. **Otosaka and Matsuoka are the two additional September entries beyond it; Zeising and Höse are additional older entries.** The Nian publisher dates below are March, rather than the April date in that note. The note itself is left unchanged. [15]

## September 2026 papers

### THW-LIT-01 — Ice-sheet mass balance and observing baseline

**Citation:** Otosaka, I. N., Shepherd, A., Amory, C., et al. (2026). *Mass balance of the Greenland and Antarctic ice sheets from the 1970s to 2023*. **Scientific Data, 13**, 1301. DOI: **10.1038/s41597-026-08088-0**. Published and version of record: **16 September 2026**. [1]

**Language route:** English paper; Chinese ScienceNet coverage dated **23 September 2026**. The Chinese coverage is not a Chinese-language version of the scholarly article. [2]

**Why save it:** This provides an observational baseline against which to compare Antarctic mass-loss assumptions. The article describes released time series for Antarctica, the Antarctic Peninsula, East Antarctica, West Antarctica, and Greenland; those regional products should not be mistaken for a Thwaites-specific time series. [1]

**Work later:** Use the West Antarctic record and its uncertainties as a regional mass-budget constraint. Identify the additional basin-scale observations needed to isolate Thwaites rather than assigning the entire regional signal to one glacier. The article links its data at **10.5285/128c5e33-5224-4197-82f0-19dcc95b80a0**. [1]

### THW-LIT-02 — AMOC slowdown and Southern Hemisphere ice discharge

**Citation:** Kasuya, T., Kuniyoshi, Y., Nagashima, K., et al. (2026). *Patagonian Ice Sheet discharge enhanced by AMOC slowdown through thermal bipolar seesaw*. **Proceedings of the National Academy of Sciences, 123**(38), e2532733123. DOI: **10.1073/pnas.2532733123**. Published online: **14 September 2026**; issue date: **22 September 2026**. [3] [12]

**Language route:** English paper; Japanese JAMSTEC institutional release dated **15 September 2026**. The release separately identifies the paper's 14 September publication date. [4]

**Why save it:** Sediment evidence and coupled atmosphere–ocean experiments address AMOC slowdown, Southern Hemisphere warming, westerly winds, precipitation, and ice-sheet discharge. This is a **Patagonian paleoclimate analogue**, not a Thwaites study or a measurement of Antarctic freshwater reaching the North Atlantic. It investigates an AMOC-to-Southern-Hemisphere response, not the reverse Thwaites-freshwater-to-AMOC pathway. [3]

**Work later:** Separate the proposed ocean-temperature, wind, and precipitation pathways. Test which pathways remain relevant under Antarctic geometry and modern boundary conditions, rather than transferring the Patagonian response magnitude directly.

### THW-LIT-03 — Antarctic coastal-zone data gaps and survey priorities

**Citation:** Matsuoka, K., Moholdt, G., Arthur, J. F., et al. (2026). *Toward an Improved Understanding of the Antarctic Coastal Zone and Its Contribution to Future Global Sea Level*. **Reviews of Geophysics, 64**(3), e2022RG000803. DOI: **10.1029/2022RG000803**. Published online: **2 September 2026**. This is a **review**, not a new single-site experiment. [5] [13]

**Language route:** English paper found during the multilingual search. No non-English coverage page was verified for this entry; it is included for its September date and direct relevance, not as a non-English finding.

**Why save it:** The review evaluates coastal bed-topography gaps and aerogeophysical, ground, ship, satellite, and modeling approaches. It offers a wider basis for evaluating the original audit's measurement-gap questions. [5]

**Work later:** Compare the review's Amundsen Sea survey priorities against THW-02 and THW-04. Identify which proposed observations actually overlap in measured quantity, which reduce bed uncertainty, and which still leave basal water pressure unmeasured.

## Older 2026 papers — not September publications

### THW-LIT-04 — Direct Thwaites bed and hydrology evidence

**Citation:** Zeising, O., Eisen, O., Hofstede, C., et al. (2026). *Hard rocks and deep wetlands beneath Thwaites Glacier in Antarctica*. **Communications Earth & Environment, 7**, 366. DOI: **10.1038/s43247-026-03502-2**. Published and version of record: **24 April 2026**. [6]

**Language route:** English paper. A German Alfred Wegener Institute page provided related Thwaites research context, but its retrieved text did not establish its own date or an exclusive connection to this DOI. It should not be treated as September coverage of this specific article. [7]

**Why save it:** This directly addresses Thwaites basal heterogeneity. The article also links vibroseismic observations and derived reflection/acoustic-impedance products at **10.1594/PANGAEA.987704**, providing a concrete data-retrieval starting point. [6]

**Work later:** Compare these measurements and spatial scales with the radar and magnetotelluric studies already in the supplied audit. Test whether any shared observables or joint inversions qualify its current “no shared quantity” framing; do not assume agreement or disagreement in advance.

### THW-LIT-05 — A coupled-model test that does not show immediate Antarctic destabilization

**Citation:** Höse, A., Kreuzer, M., Huiskamp, W., Petri, S., & Feulner, G. (2026). *Simulating the impact of an AMOC weakening on the Antarctic Ice Sheet using a coupled climate and ice-sheet model*. **Earth System Dynamics, 17**(4), 1025–1059. DOI: **10.5194/esd-17-1025-2026**. Published online: **30 July 2026**. [8] [14]

**Language route:** English paper retained during the Spanish-language search. No Spanish-language paper or Spanish coverage was verified for this entry.

**Why save it:** This is an important test of simple cascade assumptions, not just supporting evidence for them. In the reported artificial North Atlantic freshwater-forcing experiment, the authors find little subsurface-temperature change around most of Antarctica and **no change in total Antarctic ice volume during the first eight centuries after AMOC shutdown**. Later Ross Sea cooling reduces basal melt, while increased calving offsets the mass change. These are model-specific results with explicit forcing and coupling limitations, not a real-world safety guarantee. [8] [14]

**Work later:** Compare freshwater insertion, forcing duration, ocean resolution, cavity treatment, and ocean–ice coupling with the proposed Thwaites/AMOC pathway. Identify which assumptions produce the weak or delayed response and formulate a falsifiable alternative experiment.

### THW-LIT-06 — AMOC collapse and Southern Ocean carbon feedback

**Citation:** Nian, D., Willeit, M., Wunderling, N., Ganopolski, A., & Rockström, J. (2026). *Collapse of the Atlantic meridional overturning circulation would lead to substantial oceanic carbon release and additional global warming*. **Communications Earth & Environment, 7**, 295. DOI: **10.1038/s43247-026-03427-w**. Publisher date: **27 March 2026**; version of record: **31 March 2026**. [9]

**Language route:** English paper; German Potsdam Institute for Climate Impact Research coverage. The fetched German page did not expose its own date, so no September publication or coverage date is assigned. [10]

**Why save it:** It supplies a separate AMOC–Southern Ocean carbon-feedback question. It is not a Thwaites-specific paper, and carbon-cycle responses should not be substituted for Antarctic shelf-temperature or freshwater-transport measurements. [9]

**Work later:** Extract the experiment's forcing geometry and carbon/temperature diagnostics. Determine whether Antarctic shelf and cavity processes are resolved, parameterized, or outside its scope before reusing any response in CEED.

## Unverified lead — not a citation-ready paper

The HAL record titled **“A Southern Ocean Animal-Borne Bathymetry Dataset”** may be useful for ocean observing and bathymetry work. Its retrieved landing page did not establish sufficient authorship, venue, DOI, first-publication date, or peer-review status. Identify the publication record before treating it as a September paper or using it as evidence. [11]

## Reading status and limits

All six papers remain **to investigate** in the repository context. Citation metadata were checked against publisher pages and, where needed, Crossref records; relevance notes draw on retrieved article text or abstracts, not a new reproduction of their experiments. This backlog does not establish a calibrated Thwaites-to-AMOC transfer and does not modify the supplied risk audit or any simulator.

The search covered Chinese, Japanese, German, French, and Spanish sources, with English query variants. It was not exhaustive, and indexing or access limitations may hide relevant work. Papers attributed to Goldberg/Holland/Naughten, Killingbeck, Phạm, Pierce, and Bradley in the supplied audit are not counted as additional entries here.
