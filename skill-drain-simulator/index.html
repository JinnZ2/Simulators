How the model works
  • This is an illustrative model with assumed numbers, not plant data. It shows the shape of the mechanism, not a forecast.
  • Each machine drifts about 3 times a month. The operator catches a drift early with probability equal to their skill (0 to 1). A missed drift becomes a fault and 40 scrapped parts.
  • Machines also fail outright about 0.3 times a month regardless of who runs them. Nobody could have caught those.
  • Skill grows toward 1 with time on the machine. Learning slows when there are too few veterans to mentor the new hires (fewer than 1 per 3).
  • Written screen: new hires start near zero skill and quit twice as often in their first year. Hands-on trial: new hires start with some mechanical skill and stay at the normal rate.
  • Reserve line: that many senior veterans don't quit, always mentor, and hand off to their replacement before retiring (the replacement starts at skill 0.5).
  • Repairs stay in-house in proportion to veterans on site (4 covers every shift). Inspectors are added for each 400 extra scrapped parts a month above the starting level.
  • Early-warning indicator: veterans on the floor down 30% from the start. Scrap alarm: scrap 50% above its starting level.
