# GAP HANDOFF v2 — Dyslexia × spatial/mechanical ability: measurement gaps

Status: co-authored (Kavik + Claude), 2026-10-22. Pass 2 (verification): Kimi, 2026-10-23. CC0.
Tags: OBSERVED = floor observation | FOUND = verified against published source | PROPOSED = untested | CONFIRMED = PROPOSED upgraded by direct check

## CONTEXT
- The literature on whether dyslexia carries spatial or mechanical strengths is "mixed."
- FOUND: von Károlyi/Winner attribute the inconsistency to very different measures.
  Dyslexic groups excel on global/holistic tasks (e.g., impossible figures).
- FOUND: Rüsseler et al. 2005 (Child Neuropsychology 11:6, 497–512) report German
  dyslexic children impaired in mental rotation for letters, pictures, and "3D objects."
- OBSERVED: floor workers with dyslexia carry strong mechanical/spatial skill;
  hiring screens on read/write ability filter them out.

## GAP 1 — Stimulus confound: letters
- Many rotation tasks use rotated letters/digits (normal vs mirror judgment).
- This is the b/d discrimination, the core dyslexia difficulty, relabeled spatial.
- FOUND (pass 2): the separation has been run in the primary literature, and it cuts
  exactly as predicted:
  - Rusiak et al. 2007 (Perception 36:617–631): dyslexic impairment restricted to
    letter stimuli — slower reaction times, no accuracy difference; the rotation
    slope (RT × angular disparity) was intact in both groups. Deficit located in the
    normal/mirror *decision*, not in rotation itself.
  - Counter-result: Rüsseler 2005 found accuracy deficits across all three stimulus
    classes in an untimed psychometric task. Both patterns are in the literature;
    reviews still pool letter and non-letter stimuli.
  - Lachmann & van Leeuwen 2007: on same–different symmetry judgments, dyslexic
    children were FASTER, especially with letters — the b/d "failure of suppression"
    becomes an advantage when symmetry is the target.
  - "Mirrors are hard to break" (J Exp Child Psychol 2017, review + behavioral):
    for plane rotations (d–p) dyslexics behaved as controls; the confusion is
    mirror-specific, not rotation-general.
  - Recent replication keeps the Rüsseler line alive: a 2020 study (ERIC EJ1258568)
    found dyslexic readers lower AND slower across pictures, letters, and symbols.
- Status: primary studies have split stimulus types; review practice has not.
  Needed (unchanged): separate letter-stimulus from non-symbolic results in all reviews.

## GAP 2 — 2D projection decode step — CONFIRMED
- CONFIRMED (pass 2): Rüsseler 2005's "3D objects" were "three-dimensional figures
  sensu Shepard and Metzler" — i.e., perspective line drawings of cube assemblies —
  plus "colored pictures of animals or humans." No physical objects anywhere in the
  battery (PubMed abstract, PMID 16306024). Independently described by Wiedenbauer &
  Jansen-Osmann 2008 as "three-dimensional block stimuli or drawings."
- Every stimulus class in the study anchoring the "impaired across stimuli" claim
  carries a print-decode step before any rotation happens.
- Residual verification item: presentation medium (screen vs. cards) and practice-item
  format sit in the full text, not retrieved this pass.

## GAP 3 — No physical, functional objects
- Still open. Two passes found no study using real handled objects with function,
  weight, force paths, or sound as the rotation/assembly stimulus.
- FOUND adjacent — results flip with interaction channel:
  - Wang & Yang 2011: interactive virtual 3D (mouse-rotate figures to find a hidden
    ball) — no group difference in accuracy; dyslexic children substantially faster.
  - Giovagnoli et al. 2016 (discussion): paper-pencil rotation tasks → deficit
    (Winner et al. 2001); computer-game format → no deficit.
  - Wiedenbauer & Jansen-Osmann 2008: hands-on manual rotation training improves
    mental-rotation scores — physical manipulation is a proven lever, but only ever
    used as training, never as the test stimulus.
  - Near-miss, unresolved: a study employing a Rubik's Cube "to minimize symmetry"
    (physical but a puzzle, not a functional part; citation not pinned this pass).
- Needed (unchanged, rationale strengthened): rotation/assembly task with physical
  mechanical parts, untimed vs timed arms, no written instructions.

## GAP 4 — Sample selection
- Still open. No adult working sample in mechanical trades with matched controls
  found in two passes.
- FOUND adult-side:
  - Gilger et al.: fMRI of adults with dyslexia during dynamic 3-D spatial problem
    solving — atypical processing; reported as the first imaging study of dynamic
    3-D spatial reasoning in dyslexia (see also Diehl et al. 2014, impossible
    figures processed in neurologically unique ways).
  - Logan 2009 (Dyslexia 15:4, 328–46): 35% of US entrepreneurs screened positive
    for dyslexic traits vs <1% of corporate managers. Self-report checklist
    (Vinegrad 1994), 7% response rate — incidence-by-checklist, not ability
    measurement; v1 survivorship caveat applies.
- Needed (unchanged): adult working samples in mechanical trades, matched controls.

## GAP 5 — No in-situ measure of the actual skill
- Untouched in pass 2. Field measure (fault-detection lead time, first-fix rate,

  Links to the floor-capability instrument.

## GAP 6 — Timing and instruction channel
- Partially FOUND:
  - Rüsseler 2005 was a psychometric task with NO time pressure (per the Lachmann-
    group account), and still found child deficits — the timing confound does not
    explain that result; letter-decode (Gap 1) and print-decode (Gap 2) remain as
    the candidate loads.
  - Rusiak 2007's speed/accuracy split (RT differs, accuracy doesn't) is the
    demonstration of why speed and accuracy must be reported separately — a mixed
    dependent variable hides the locus of the deficit.
  - Instruction channel (written vs oral/demonstrated) was never manipulated as a
    factor in anything found. Precedent only: a VU classroom study used a physical
    example item to teach the Vandenberg MRT before the standard paper test.
  - Adjacent: 2025 FIU eye-tracking work measured rotation strategy (holistic vs
    piecemeal) in pre-reading children with no verbal demand — a no-weak-channel
    measurement precedent.
- Status: timing arm narrowed; instruction-channel arm stands.

## GAP 7 — Records attribution (links to the floor-capability instrument)
- OBSERVED: repairs done by untitled workers get written up by titled mechanics.
- Repair records therefore misplace the skill; any study using job records inherits it.
- Untouched in pass 2; no literature located. Needed (unchanged): attribution-
  corrected skill data (direct observation, not write-ups).

## PASS-2 SYNTHESIS
The "mixed" literature is not noise. Results co-vary with measurement choices in
exactly the directions the gaps predict:

| Design feature                     | Result pattern            |
|------------------------------------|---------------------------|
| Letters in stimulus set            | deficit (b/d decision loaded) |
| Static print projection            | deficit                   |
| Interactive / dynamic 3D           | parity or speed advantage |
| Symmetry as the target             | dyslexic advantage        |

Rüsseler 2005 — the strongest "impaired across the board" result — used drawn
stimuli exclusively, in clinic-referred 2nd graders. Gaps 1, 2, and 4 are stacked
in that single design, and it is the study most often cited against a dyslexic
spatial strength.

Untested cells remain: physical functional objects (3), adult trades samples (4),
in-situ field measure (5), instruction channel (6), attribution-corrected records (7).
