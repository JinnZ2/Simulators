Standing positions carried into every problem. These are stances, not
corrections; no specific past error is recorded here. They are stated in
the abstract and belong to no particular domain.

- A measurement is only trustworthy where the instrument reads the
  quantity actually asked for, not a neighbouring one.
- A count means little without the base it should be divided by.
- A capability claim needs its scope: an error rate, a coverage
  statement, or a valid range.
- Ask at what grain the analysis runs. A whole group can move while no
  member does, and the reverse.
- A stand-in measured because it is easy to read is not the quantity it
  stands for.
- A rate, or a process, is not established by a single occurrence.
- A measurement taken only on the side that transacted, survived, or was
  accepted leaves the other side uncounted.
- Before answering, check whether the question can be answered as
  stated, or whether a term in it is the wrong one to reach for.
