# FIELD BRIEF — The Design-Life Mismatch
### One page. For the people who build and maintain what the risk applies to.

---

**THE SENTENCE** (sayable in any planning meeting; no current assessment contradicts it):

> **The code is calibrated to a horizon the hazard has already passed.**

---

**THE ARITHMETIC** (three numbers, all from published 2026 work):

- **150 years** — how long Thwaites Glacier keeps losing ice even if ocean melting
  stopped tomorrow. The loss is *committed*: driven by the glacier's own internal
  dynamics, not by anything that can still be switched off.
  *(Bradley et al., Geophysical Research Letters, 2026 — peer-reviewed)*
- **75 years** — a typical design life for coastal infrastructure: ports, bridges,
  culverts, roads, water systems.
- **The mismatch:** committed loss runs *twice the design life*. Anything built to
  current code assumes a stability that is already gone on paper.

**What this does NOT say:** not "collapse tomorrow," not "nothing matters until 2200."
It says the floor is already poured and rising. Slow, locked in, and indifferent to
what anyone decides about emissions next year.

---

**WHAT TO ASK IN THE ROOM:**

1. "Does the design basis for this project cite committed ice loss as a floor, or a
   scenario?" — A scenario can be argued down. A floor cannot.
2. "What horizon is the code calibrated to, and what happens to this structure in
   year 76 through 150?"
3. "Which number in this plan assumes the ocean stays cooperative?" — If the plan
   works only when melt behaves, it was written before the committed-loss finding.

**WHAT TO PUSH:** THW-01 — the one gap a crew, a port, or a community can move alone:
demand the code name committed loss as the minimum design basis. The other gaps
(boreholes, instrument networks) need institutions. This one needs a motion, a
meeting, and people who build things standing up in it.

---

**WHY YOUR OWN READ MATTERS:**

The instruments watching the glacier's bed measure four different things that never
cross-check each other — there is no referee instrument. That means the institutional
reading can't be cross-checked from inside the system. The only independent check that
exists is terrain-prior: people who work the coast and watch what the water actually
does against what the plan assumed. Write it down. Date it. A dated notebook from
someone who works the ground is the only cross-check channel currently in service.
