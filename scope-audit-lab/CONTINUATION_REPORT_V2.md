## 3. The ETH Claim as of October 27, 2026

### 3.1 What was actually announced — and what was not

OpenAI's October 26 announcement states that an internal model produced a natural-language disproof of ETH on October 16, 2026, and that the result was formalized in Lean the following day; both the model's write-up and the Lean proof were posted to the company's public GitHub repository, with the materials framed as "a form of raw research data" ([OpenAI](https://openai.com/index/building-scientific-understanding-with-academic-communities/)). ETH, posed by Impagliazzo and Paturi around 1999–2001, asserts that 3-SAT admits no subexponential-time algorithm; it underpins hundreds of conditional hardness results across graph theory, computational biology, databases, and computational geometry ([OpenAI](https://openai.com/index/building-scientific-understanding-with-academic-communities/)). The claimed algorithm is a deterministic, polynomial-space procedure that *exactly counts* satisfying assignments of fixed-width k-CNF formulas in subexponential time — Williams summarized the claimed 3-SAT bound as roughly exp(n/(log n)^{1/2+o(1)}) ([CellCog](https://cellcog.ai/blog/openai-yang-mills-eth-kakeya/)).

Three boundary statements deserve emphasis because early coverage repeatedly blurred them. First, a subexponential 3-SAT algorithm does **not** imply P = NP: polynomial-time reductions can blow up instance size enough to erase the subexponential gain, and OpenAI's own president stated P probably still does not equal NP ([daily.dev](https://daily.dev/posts/openai-s-internal-model-claims-a-disproof-of-the-exponential-time-hypothesis-xrd0xvwjn), [MindStudio](https://www.mindstudio.ai/blog/exponential-time-hypothesis-disproven)). Second, the result is asymptotic and would not by itself produce practical SAT-solver speedups; OpenAI says converting it into useful algorithms "will require significant further research" ([OpenAI](https://openai.com/index/building-scientific-understanding-with-academic-communities/)). Third, the same announcement bundled two further claims — local smoothing (hence Kakeya) in all dimensions, and existence proofs for four-dimensional QCD and pure Yang–Mills that do **not** address the mass gap required by the Clay Millennium Prize — each with a different release and review arrangement ([AI Weekly](https://aiweekly.co/alerts/openai-hands-eth-kakeya-yang-mills-proofs-to-reviewers)).

### 3.2 The parallel Anthropic claim: one target, two instruments

One day before the OpenAI announcement, a preprint dated October 25, 2026 appeared claiming the same theorem — a deterministic 2^{o(n)} algorithm for #3-SAT — attributed to "an internal research Claude model," with a Lean 4 development of 1,050 modules and an explicit disclosure that "at the time of writing no person is known to have checked it" and that its runtime constant is not explicit ([Hexagon](https://hexagonmath.org/pdf/2610.00577v1)). The paper cites OpenAI's preprint, so the two claims are not independent discoveries in the strong sense; but they are two separately generated proof artifacts aimed at the same target, with different stated bounds and different disclosure postures ([Hexagon](https://hexagonmath.org/pdf/2610.00577v1)).

For the scope-audit project this is an unplanned natural experiment. The project's instruments were designed to ask: when a generator produces a claim, what external anchor forces revision, what observation mechanism checks it, and what survives into the evidence record? Here we have two generators, two artifacts, one shared verification bottleneck — the human reader of the *statement* — and a public timestamp difference of roughly ten days between generation and announcement on the OpenAI side ([CellCog](https://cellcog.ai/blog/openai-yang-mills-eth-kakeya/)). Whatever the working groups conclude, the episode supplies the project with a documented case where the constraint (logical validity), the instrument (Lean), the record (the repository and its scope notes), and the revision mechanism (working groups, withdrawals, registries) are all separately visible — the four-part decomposition the earlier sessions argued for.

### 3.3 What the Lean check covers — the specification gap, documented

The strongest finding of this research cycle is that the "specification gap" discussed abstractly in the transcript — a Lean proof certifies that *some formal statement* follows from the axioms, not that the formal statement matches the headline claim — now has a peer-reviewable literature of worked examples. The arXiv paper *Navier–Stokes lost in translation* (Oct 6, 2026) dissects OpenAI's earlier Navier–Stokes formalization and shows concrete cases where the natural-language paper asserts an estimate with m+4 derivatives while the checked Lean theorem requires m+5 — a strictly weaker statement that nonetheless compiles ([arXiv](https://arxiv.org/html/2610.08144v1)). In the October 6 mathematics repository, an independent reader documented Family 266, whose summary claims a complete resolution of the dimension-six mutually-unbiased-bases conjecture (N(6)=3), while its own Lean scope note states that the linked formalization proves only a weaker bound of at most five and "does not establish the paper's upper bound of three" ([Medium analysis](https://medium.com/@andy.a.g/openai-published-722-ai-written-math-papers-lean-checked-a-statement-who-checked-the-statement-758db13fc2e7)).

The scale indicators are equally relevant. The October 6 release contained about 722 manuscripts in 372 result families drawn from roughly 4,000 posed problems, with approximately 42% of top-line results formalized at launch and the README warning that "some of the unformalized results could have issues" ([Developers Digest](https://www.developersdigest.tech/blog/openai-math-repo-lean-proofs-what-they-verify-2026), [tech-insider](https://tech-insider.org/openai-math-papers-lean-verification-42-percent-2026/)); the formalization catalogue itself lists 162 papers with a formalized main result and 235 families with Lean scope notes ([Medium analysis](https://medium.com/@andy.a.g/openai-published-722-ai-written-math-papers-lean-checked-a-statement-who-checked-the-statement-758db13fc2e7)). Two days later OpenAI withdrew three of the results ([Hacker News](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=50002650)). The design detail that matters for instrument-building is the repository's *challenge-file* mechanism: the theorem statement lives in a fixed file with a `sorry` placeholder, and tooling verifies that the agent-written proof discharges exactly that statement — pinning the specification so the generator cannot move it, while leaving the semantic match between statement and conjecture to human readers ([Developers Digest](https://www.developersdigest.tech/blog/openai-math-repo-lean-proofs-what-they-verify-2026)). In scope-audit vocabulary: the *declared domain* is pinned and machine-checked; the *mapping from the informal target to the declared domain* remains an open audit item.

### 3.4 The verification infrastructure that formed in eight weeks

Between August and October 2026 the mathematical community built, at speed, institutions that operationalize "record, don't verdict." The Palomar registry (announced by Terence Tao on August 18, incubated by the Lean FRO and ICARM, with a board including Avigad, Morrison, Vakil, Venkatesh and others) accepts snapshots of Lean repositories and runs three automated checks: the Comparator tool verifies the proof discharges exactly the recorded challenge-file statement against a pinned Mathlib using only permitted axioms; every exported proof is replayed through a second, independently implemented kernel (`nanoda`); and a language model judges whether the informal description in a required `formalization.yaml` fairly renders the formal statement — with the explicit caveat that registration is not certification of novelty, relevance, or semantic match ([Tao's blog](https://terrytao.wordpress.com/2026/08/18/palomar-a-registry-of-lean-verified-mathematics/), [Palomar statement](https://palomar-registry.org/statement), [ICARM](https://icarm.io/news/announcing-palomar-a-registry-of-lean-verified-mathematics/)).

The Advisory Group on Mathematics and AI (AGMAI) — nine mathematicians including Gowers, Hairer, Tillmann, Vakil, Witten, and Matchett Wood, unpaid and independent — issued general guidelines on September 29, 2026 (informed by 600+ survey responses) recommending: posting on non-lab-controlled scholarly repositories with citable identifiers and versioned modifications; disclosure of model, prompts, chains of thought, attempt counts, and compute; Lean formalization where possible under community standards; a statement of how problems were chosen and how many were attempted; and independent nonprofit governance of follow-up funding ([AGMAI](https://agmai.org/), [Sep 29 guidelines](https://agmai.org/general-sep29/)). On October 25–26 the group argued against the "designated mathematician" release model and called for immediate public release of the withheld Yang–Mills materials ([AGMAI](https://agmai.org/)). Meanwhile the ETH working group's open letter states that its involvement "should not be taken as certification of the claim," and Williams confirmed the three reviewers signed no NDAs and will not be paid ([TNW](https://thenextweb.com/news/openai-exponential-time-hypothesis-false-kakeya-yang-mills)). The pattern across all four institutions is the project's own pattern: separate the record from the verdict, make the provenance inspectable, and keep the human judgment step explicit rather than pretending it has been automated away.

### 3.5 A scope-audit reading of the ETH case

Applying the lab's audit variables (D, A, X, C, I, Y, M, U, R) to the ETH episode shows what the framework buys beyond a news summary. **D (declared domain):** the formalized statement — currently reported to cover a weaker runtime bound than the paper's strongest theorem, the same shape of gap documented in the Navier–Stokes and MUB cases ([MLQ](https://mlq.ai/news/openai-puts-three-ai-generated-breakthroughs-before-academic-review/), [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/html/2610.08144v1)). **A (assumptions):** the machine model and cost convention chosen for the formalization; the Anthropic paper flags its own implicit constant. **Y (predicted output):** "#k-SAT ∈ DTIME(2^{o(n)}) ∩ DSPACE(poly(L))." **M (measured output):** the Lean kernel's acceptance of the compiled development — a mechanical fact about the formal artifact, not about the conjecture. **R (discrepancy):** the interval between the formalized bound and the headline bound, and between "compiles" and "means what ETH says" — recorded, attributed, and pending the working groups. **U (unmeasured):** whether the natural-language proof's strengthening steps survive independent reconstruction; whether the Anthropic development's definitions match its own abstract.

The framework's discipline also cuts against over-reading in the other direction. If the working groups eventually certify the ETH claim, that verdict will attach to the formalized statement as examined, and the residual question — whether the stronger unformalized bounds hold — remains a separate open record, not a detail to be swept into "ETH is false, full stop." If they refute it, the artifacts still enter the record with their scope notes, as the three withdrawn October 8 results did ([Hacker News](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=50002650)). Either way, the episode is the project's thesis demonstrated at field scale: the scarce resource is not generation but *scoped verification* — stating exactly what was checked, against which declaration, with what uncertainty, and what remains untested.

## 4. The Academic Ground Under "Scope"

### 4.1 Adequacy-for-purpose: the philosophy the lab already implements

The transcript's central intuition — that a model is not "valid" or "invalid" but adequate for a purpose under conditions — is a developed position in philosophy of science. Wendy Parker's adequacy-for-purpose view holds that model evaluation should determine "whether a model is adequate for one or more purposes of interest," analyzing adequacy as a relation among target T, user U, methodology W, background circumstances B, and goal P jointly — a "problem space" in which the model is or is not a solution ([Parker 2020](https://www.cambridge.org/core/journals/philosophy-of-science/article/model-evaluation-an-adequacyforpurpose-view/CA91669E7CAC8BE4332A2B6D99BC9DB0)). The view traces to a US National Research Council characterization of evaluation as deciding "whether and when a model is suitable for its intended purpose" and to parallel statements in environmental modeling ([Parker 2020](https://www.cambridge.org/core/journals/philosophy-of-science/article/model-evaluation-an-adequacyforpurpose-view/CA91669E7CAC8BE4332A2B6D99BC9DB0)).

Two of Parker's points directly inform the lab's design. First, the assessment is **holistic** in a way representational accuracy is not: an error in one component may be compensated elsewhere or by the surrounding methodology, so component-wise scoring cannot be aggregated into a verdict — which is exactly why the lab refuses to collapse records into a score ([Parker 2020](https://www.cambridge.org/core/journals/philosophy-of-science/article/model-evaluation-an-adequacyforpurpose-view/CA91669E7CAC8BE4332A2B6D99BC9DB0)). Second, when adequacy for a hard purpose cannot be established, the recommended move is to test adequacy for a related, easier purpose (revealing real possibilities rather than predicting within margins) — a strategy the lab's "candidate region → proposed next test" loop mirrors mechanically. The beach-plastic benchmark in the transcript (count bottles vs. estimate debris vs. model moisture transport) is a textbook adequacy-for-purpose triad: same observations, different purposes, different adequacy criteria.

### 4.2 Applicability domains: the regulatory and spatial precedents

In cheminformatics, the *applicability domain* (AD) of a QSAR model is the region of chemical space within which predictions are considered reliable, and the OECD's validation guidance makes a defined AD a condition for regulatory acceptance of a (Q)SAR model ([Wikipedia: Applicability domain](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Applicability_domain)). The concept is explicitly purpose- and assumption-linked: predictions are reliable for interpolation within the training domain, not for extrapolation beyond it, and no universal boundary definition exists ([OpenADMET](https://openadmet.ghost.io/applicability-domain-how-to-know-when-your-model-is-actually-relevant/)). This is the regulatory ancestor of the transcript's "declared scope": a boundary that must be stated before predictions are trusted, defined relative to what the model was built and validated on, and treated as part of the model's regulatory identity rather than as a marketing caveat. The lab's `scope.included` / `scope.excluded` lists are the same object in a plainer serialization — declared, versioned, and attached to the record instead of reconstructed after a failure.

The spatial-statistics version is even closer to the transition engine. Work on the *area of applicability* (AOA) for spatial machine learning finds a systematic **error peak at the applicability boundary** itself — local errors rise as the boundary is approached from inside, spike at the transition from interpolation to extrapolation, then mechanically fall again far outside as predictions become weakly contrasted and uninformative ([HAL preprint](https://hal.science/hal-05457625/document)). This is a documented empirical regularity that the v0.2 engine's design deliberately does not assume: the engine records candidate transition regions from adjacent-case deltas and leaves the shape of the error profile — peak at boundary, monotone degradation, or anything else — to be characterized per case rather than baked in as an expectation. If future lab data does show boundary-peaked residuals, the AOA literature provides both an explanation candidate and ready-made diagnostic quantities (dissimilarity index, threshold distance Δ).

### 4.3 Model discrepancy: Kennedy–O'Hagan and the residual's meaning

The transcript's insistence that a residual r = M − f(X; θ) is "data, not a verdict" has a canonical statistical framing: Kennedy and O'Hagan's Bayesian calibration framework separates a simulator η(·,·) from an explicit discrepancy term δ(·) capturing what parameter calibration *cannot* explain — model-form error as a first-class, modeled object rather than noise to be averaged away ([arXiv review context](https://arxiv.org/html/2603.11960v1)). Subsequent work debates whether discrepancy is genuinely decoupled from the simulator or entangled with parameter uncertainty, and recent formulations embed the discrepancy inside the simulator for state-aware calibration ([arXiv](https://arxiv.org/html/2603.11960v1)). The lab sits deliberately upstream of this debate: its `observation` records keep the residual, the uncertainties, and the conditions without committing to any distributional model of δ, so a future analyst can adopt Kennedy–O'Hagan, a state-aware variant, or neither — the record outlives the modeling choice, which is exactly the property an append-only evidence stream should have.

The mapping to the lab is direct: the observation record's `comparison` block computes the residual and its normalized form against combined uncertainty, and then stops — `comparison_status: "computed; interpretation unresolved"`. The KOH literature explains why stopping is correct: the same residual can arise from parameter misfit, model-form error, or measurement error, and attributing it requires additional structure (priors, replicate experiments, independent measurements) that the record deliberately does not presume ([arXiv](https://arxiv.org/html/2603.11960v1)). Where the lab currently records residuals pointwise, the KOH tradition suggests a v0.3+ direction: treating *sets* of residuals across a factor range as a discrepancy field with spatial structure — which connects back to the transition engine's factor-ordered deltas.

### 4.4 Change-point detection: what the engine is, and is not

The scope-transition engine occupies a deliberately minimal corner of a mature field. Change-point detection (CPD) is traditionally divided into offline methods, which segment a complete signal retrospectively, and online methods, which detect changes as data arrives; Aminikhanghahi and Cook's survey organizes the families (probabilistic/Bayesian run-length methods, likelihood-ratio, subspace, kernel, clustering, graph-based) along an ε-real-time continuum ([Aminikhanghahi & Cook, PMC](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5464762/)). Truong, Oudre and Vayatis's selective review of offline methods decomposes every algorithm into a cost function, a search method (Opt, PELT, BinSeg, BotUp, window-sliding), and a constraint on the number of changes, implemented in the `ruptures` package ([Truong et al., arXiv](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1801.00718)).

The v0.2 engine is, in that taxonomy, a first-order windowless detector with a single-sample "cost" (adjacent output delta) and a user-declared threshold — strictly weaker than CUSUM or PELT, and intentionally so. Three differences are principled rather than primitive. First, it **preserves order as given** instead of optimizing a segmentation, because factor order in an experiment log is a provenance fact, not a nuisance to be optimized away. Second, it **records missing and non-finite inputs as unresolved items** instead of dropping them, which standard CPD implementations typically handle by implicit deletion or interpolation. Third, it reports *candidate regions with cause unassigned* rather than estimated change points with an implicit significance claim — the audit posture applied to detection. The literature supplies the upgrade path: if candidate regions from simple delta scans prove noisy on real data, robust cost functions (L1, kernel-based, rank-based) can replace the raw delta without changing the record schema or the no-verdict rule ([Truong et al., arXiv](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1801.00718)).

### 4.5 Measuring the measurement: MSA, the evaluation gap, and introspection evidence

The transcript's "instrumentation gap" — a system unable to detect that its own measurements are wrong — is standard doctrine in industrial metrology. Measurement Systems Analysis separates calibration (traceability of bias to a reference standard), Gage R&R (repeatability and reproducibility of the whole measurement *process*, operators and fixtures included), and uncertainty budgets as three distinct questions, with the AIAG conventions (%GRR under 10% acceptable, 10–30% marginal, over 30% unacceptable; ndc ≥ 5) as decision gates — and with the explicit warning that a calibrated instrument can still fail Gage R&R because the process around it is noisy ([ASQ](https://careers.asq.org/career-resources/on-the-job-3/measurement-system-analysis-and-gage-rr-for-quality-engineer-2026-110), [Net-Inspect](https://www.net-inspect.com/blog/msa-studies-explained/)). The provenance failures that sink MSA studies in audits — study disconnected from the specific gauge, stale after method changes, broken operator blinding — are the same failure modes the transcript lists as confounds ([Net-Inspect](https://www.net-inspect.com/blog/msa-studies-explained/)).

For AI systems specifically, the International AI Safety Report 2026 documents an "evaluation gap" — pre-deployment test performance does not reliably predict real-world behavior — and systematic under-recording of incidents, with reluctance to report (embarrassment, liability) identified as a contributing factor and most evidence coming from incident databases rather than systematic measurement ([International AI Safety Report 2026](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2602.21012)). The EU AI Act's incident-reporting regime shows the same hole legally: analysis of the RubyGems agent incident of May 2026 suggests at least one reportable event went unreported because the provider read the obligation narrowly ([Lawfare](https://www.lawfaremedia.org/article/the-eu-ai-act-mandates-ai-incident-reporting-if-we-re-lucky)). And on the specific question of AI self-reports — thread B's confound list — Anthropic's introspection research provides causal evidence of limited but real self-access: concept-injection experiments show models can sometimes detect injected representations (about 20% of the time under the best protocol, highly context-dependent), while follow-up work on "introspection adapters" confirms that self-reports of learned behaviors are often unreliable yet carry usable signal for auditing covert fine-tuning (7/9 ciphered-attack models identified) ([Ken Huang summary](https://kenhuangus.substack.com/p/is-ai-becoming-self-aware-anthropics), [Anthropic Alignment](https://alignment.anthropic.com/2026/introspection-adapters/)). Independent analyses similarly find that apparent self-knowledge is often driven by input cues and heuristics rather than genuine introspection ([LessWrong](https://www.lesswrong.com/posts/QkHgAjrgJssTrqjBB/how-self-aware-are-llms)). The design consequence the transcript drew — treat self-reports as recorded outputs requiring independent verification, never as evidence of internal states — is now the empirically supported default, not a philosophical preference.

### 4.6 The mapping table

The subsections above can be compressed into a single reference table that pairs each home-grown project term with its closest established concept, states the difference the project adds, and links the strongest source. ## 5. Scope Audit Lab v0.2: The Scope-Transition Engine

### 5.1 From specification to implementation

The v0.2 package adds `scope_audit_lab/transitions.py` (~150 lines, standard library only) implementing the five specified behaviors exactly, plus a `transition_scan` record type integrated into the append-only JSONL event stream and CLI. Three semantic decisions the specification left open were locked and pinned by tests, each chosen to keep the instrument from smuggling judgment into mechanics: the exceedance test is strictly greater-than (a `>=` rule would let a probing analyst's threshold silently encode curiosity about a specific pair); relative change is measured against the left, earlier-in-order output; and non-finite values are distinguished from missing ones in the recorded reasons. The specification-to-code mapping is one-to-one:

| Specified behavior (final GPT message) | Implementation |
|---|---|
| Vary a recorded factor, preserving case order | `scan_transitions` consumes cases as an ordered sequence; no sort anywhere; `order_index` recorded per case |
| Compare adjacent cases, calculate output changes | `adjacent_deltas` computes delta, absolute delta, relative delta (vs. left output) per adjacent pair |
| Record changes exceeding a specified threshold | Exceedance test is **strictly greater-than**; threshold kind is `absolute` or `relative`; the rule is stored in the record's `config` block |
| Preserve missing inputs and unresolved explanations | Missing/non-finite factor or output → delta status `not_computed` with an explicit reason string; case retained in the record; reasons aggregated into `unresolved` |
| Report candidate transition regions without declaring causes or significance | Each region carries `classification: "candidate transition region; cause and significance unassigned"`; the record's `interpretation` is `unassigned`; a `limitations` block states what the scan cannot show |

Backward compatibility is maintained deliberately: `validate_record` now accepts schema versions "0.1" and "0.2", and the 0.1 demo stream validates unchanged under v0.2 (verified against the original file). Records written by v0.2 carry version "0.2". This matters beyond tidiness: an append-only evidence stream is only trustworthy if old records never need to be rewritten to remain legible, so the validator's supported-version set is itself part of the lab's no-silent-revision discipline — new record *types* may be added, but old record *content* is never invalidated, migrated, or reinterpreted by a version bump. The v0.3 cycle kept this promise in a stricter form: four new experiment procedures shipped with no schema change at all, because the existing `experiment` type already carried everything they produce.

The engine is deliberately decoupled from the runner, continuing the first design decision of the 0.1 cycle: the experiment runner establishes what happened, and the auditor examines the relationship between what happened and the model's declared scope — neither may silently rewrite the other. In practice this means `scan_transitions` knows nothing about models or experiments; it sees an ordered list of cases with factor and output values. The link back to the scientific context is made at record time through `linked_record_ids`, so a scan can cite the model and experiment records it bears on without importing their semantics. This separation is what allows the same engine to be run over a beach-count series, a historical cascade's dated steps, or a formalization's bound-strengthening history without code changes — only the labels and the provenance links differ.

### 5.2 Semantics and edge cases

Three semantic choices were fixed where the specification left room, and each is documented in the record itself. **Threshold equality is not an exceedance.** A delta exactly equal to the threshold is computed and stored but not flagged — "exceeds" is implemented as strict `>`, and a test pins this (a +10 delta against threshold 10 is not flagged; +15 is). The alternative (≥) was rejected because it makes the flagged set change when a user probes with the observed delta as the threshold, which would let the record silently encode the analyst's curiosity as a finding. **Relative change references the left output.** For a pair (left, right) in given order, relative delta = |Δ| / |output_left|; when |output_left| ≤ zero_tolerance the relative value is recorded as undefined with the reason retained, while the absolute delta still stands — this is the mandated zero-valued-output behavior, and it means a relative-kind scan and an absolute-kind scan over the same cases can legitimately flag different pairs, with both results preserved rather than reconciled. **Non-finite is not missing.** A NaN or infinite value produces a `not_computed` delta with a distinct reason ("missing or non-finite"), keeping the three situations — never measured, measured but unusable, measured and finite — distinguishable in the stream.

The demo CLI gained a `transitions-demo` command that appends a six-case synthetic visibility scan (including one zero-valued output and one fully missing case) plus a linked `audit_event` whose unresolved questions are drawn mechanically from the scan's own `unresolved` list — demonstrating, not asserting, the record linkage. A general `transitions` command reads an external cases JSON file, runs the scan, prints the record, and optionally appends it to an event stream, so the same code path that runs the labeled demo also runs real case files; there is no separate "real mode" whose behavior could diverge from what the tests exercise. Both commands print the record JSON to stdout before any file write, keeping the runner visible in the loop: the analyst sees exactly what will be appended and chooses to append it, which preserves the runner/auditor separation described above at the smallest possible scale.

The figure below visualizes that demo run and is worth reading the way the engine writes: the left panel shows outputs in the order the cases were given, with the missing case drawn as a marked absence rather than skipped; the right panel shows the adjacent deltas against the declared threshold, with the two exceedances (vis_050→vis_025 and vis_025→vis_000, deltas 27 and 61 against threshold 10) flagged as candidate regions and the final pair drawn as "not computed" rather than zero. Nothing in either panel asserts that a visibility boundary exists — the synthetic series was fabricated to exercise the record format, and the equivalent real run remains the benchmark experiment the lab has not yet performed.

### 5.3 Verification status

The full suite is **31 tests, all passing** (11 inherited core tests + 20 new engine tests). The mandated four areas are covered as follows. **Threshold behavior:** strict-greater-than at, above, and below threshold; absolute and relative kinds; negative/NaN thresholds and unknown kinds rejected. **Input-order preservation:** deliberately non-monotonic factor order confirmed preserved in cases, deltas, and region references; adjacency follows the given sequence. **Missing measurements:** missing output, missing factor, and non-finite values each produce recorded reasons without dropping cases; scans continue past gaps so later valid pairs are still evaluated. **Zero-valued outputs:** absolute delta computed, relative undefined with reason, absolute-kind scan still flags, relative-kind scan records the metric as undefined rather than silently passing. Integration tests confirm records validate, round-trip through JSONL, link to existing model/experiment records, keep `interpretation: unassigned`, and that 0.1 records still validate.

What has *not* been verified is equally recorded: the engine has seen only synthetic data; no detection-accuracy, false-alarm, or localization measurements exist yet; and no claim is made that thresholded adjacent deltas are an adequate detector for any particular real system. The README states this under "Not implemented in 0.2": statistical change-point estimation, coupling tests between factors, dimensional analysis, compression adapters, uncertainty propagation, and cryptographic log chaining all remain future stages. This second list is load-bearing, not decorative — it is the difference between an instrument that has been tested and an instrument that has been *validated for a purpose*, and §4.1's adequacy-for-purpose analysis is precisely about why conflating the two is the standard failure mode of evaluation pipelines. The v0.3 addendum (§8) addresses the first gap instrumentally, by recording the engine's own behavior on known-null and permuted inputs; the adequacy question itself remains, correctly, unanswered.

## 6. The Open Threads, Re-Examined

### 6.1 Thread A — Knowledge-loss cascade: the order problem is real, and it is the historians' problem too

The proposed cascade (restrict women → restrict outsiders + nostalgia framing → fracture/tribalism → keepers of knowledge lost → compounding loss) was correctly left PROPOSED, and this cycle's research shows why it must stay that way for now: the flagship historical case in the thread's notes — post-Roman Britain — sits inside an unresolved historiographical dispute about whether the fifth-century West underwent catastrophic collapse (Ward-Perkins, Heather, arguing from material culture: the disappearance of pottery, coinage, tiled roofs, and graffiti-verifiable literacy) or cultural transformation (the Peter Brown "Late Antiquity" school, arguing from religious and textual continuity) ([Ward-Perkins interview, BU](https://www.bu.edu/historic/hs/perkins.pdf), [TMR review](https://scholarworks.iu.edu/journals/index.php/tmr/article/view/16453)). Ward-Perkins explicitly frames his own book as a counter-revision to Brown, and reviewers note the debate turns on which evidence class — material indices or textual/religious production — is taken as the measure of "knowledge" ([TMR review](https://scholarworks.iu.edu/journals/index.php/tmr/article/view/16453)). Even the narrower empirical question flagged in the thread (collapse dating in Britain, ~400s vs. the earlier Christianization steps in the 300s) maps onto the same source-class disagreement.

The constructive output for the project is methodological. The thread's "first test: does the order hold across cases? date each step separately" is precisely a scope-transition scan over historical time: each cascade step is a case, each case needs its own dated evidence class, and adjacent-step comparisons must be recorded without assuming the cascade's direction. The thread also needs its evidence classes separated *before* sequencing — material culture, textual production, institutional restriction events — because the historiographical debate shows that different classes can imply different orders for the same society. That is a `transition_scan` per evidence class, with cross-class comparison deferred to an audit event. The cascade hypothesis remains testable but untested; this report adds no new historical evidence and assigns it no probability.

### 6.2 Thread B — Self-assessment protocol: the confound list now has citations

The thread's ten-confound checklist (corpus prior, framing pull, trained reflex, narration-not-causal, context contamination, sampling variance, version drift, evaluator sharing, term freight, observer effect) was saved but not yet executable. This cycle's literature pass attaches empirical anchors to most of them: introspection success under the best concept-injection protocol is ~20% and context-dependent, quantifying why any single self-report is weak evidence ([Ken Huang summary](https://kenhuangus.substack.com/p/is-ai-becoming-self-aware-anthropics)); models exploit question-side shortcuts and input cues that masquerade as self-knowledge (corpus prior, framing pull) ([arXiv](https://arxiv.org/html/2509.15339v1), [LessWrong](https://www.lesswrong.com/posts/QkHgAjrgJssTrqjBB/how-self-aware-are-llms)); high-repetition — i.e., confident, consistent — responses are still sometimes wrong, undercutting "consistency = knowledge" (trained reflex, narration) ([EMNLP Findings](https://aclanthology.org/2025.findings-emnlp.527.pdf)); and evaluator-sharing risk is now institutional, with LLM judges documented marking figures "verified" by coincidental numeric match ([Medium analysis](https://medium.com/@andy.a.g/openai-published-722-ai-written-math-papers-lean-checked-a-statement-who-checked-the-statement-758db13fc2e7)).

The thread's stated next step — a runnable confound record in the instrument family — is now a small, well-defined next-version task: a `confound_record` type (confound id, test applied, result, status, evidence, date/version stamps per confound 7) sharing the lab's no-verdict schema. The introspection-adapter methodology offers a design hint: aggregate many weak self-reports through a scaffold and score against ground truth where ground truth exists (7/9 detection on planted ciphers) — i.e., self-reports are admissible as *screening* signals with known failure rates, not as measurements ([Anthropic Alignment](https://alignment.anthropic.com/2026/introspection-adapters/)). The v0.3 coupling scan (§8) covers the *behavioral* half of this agenda — recording where two factors' candidate regions coincide — while the `confound_record` remains the right home for the *interpretive* half: the running log of which alternative explanations have been tested, with what result, and which remain open. Keeping those halves in separate record types is deliberate, since conflating "regions coincide" with "a confound is present" is precisely the leap the lab exists to prevent.

### 6.3 Threads C and D — unchanged, by design

Thread C (driving-test time item) depends on ELD arrival/departure data only the user can collect at their next stop; nothing this cycle can do substitutes for it, and it remains UNKNOWN. Thread D (routinization-of-charisma: women's role latitude vs. time since founding across movements) received no new evidence this cycle; a targeted literature pass on charismatic-authority routinization and gender in new religious movements is a candidate for the next research round, and should be framed as dated cases per movement (the same evidence-class discipline as thread A) rather than as a single cross-movement regression, given the known sensitivity of such comparisons to source class and period definition.

It is worth recording why leaving two threads untouched is the correct outcome rather than a shortfall. The instrument family's premise is that an UNKNOWN status with a stated evidence requirement is a finished state, not a backlog item: thread C's requirement is a specific data collection event with a known protocol, and thread D's is a literature pass whose framing constraints are now written down. Both threads therefore already satisfy the only demand this cycle can legitimately make of them — that what is missing be named precisely enough to be recognized when it arrives — and any attempt to fill them from available sources would import exactly the unverifiable filler the lab's schema exists to exclude.

## 8. Addendum: Four Instrument-Level Experiments (Scope Audit Lab v0.3)

### 8.1 From engine to experimental subject

The v0.2 engine was built to a specification; v0.3 treats the engine itself as an experimental subject and records how it behaves under controlled manipulation. Four procedures were added in `experiments.py`, each wrapping `scan_transitions` and emitting an `experiment` record under the unchanged 0.2 schema — a deliberate versioning decision, since the record structure needed no changes. The **null-baseline scan** runs the engine over seeded synthetic noise with no planted structure and records, per replication, how many candidate regions are flagged and where; the record is labeled synthetic with "planted structure: none (by construction)" and exists so that any future real-data scan can be recorded alongside what the instrument reports on known-null input. The **threshold sweep** scans one case set at several thresholds — in the order given, never sorted, extending the engine's own ordering discipline to the sweep itself — and records for each distinct between-pair the thresholds at which it appeared, without characterizing that pattern as stability or fragility. The **order-permutation probe** scans the given order plus seeded permutations and records pair appearances across all scans; because the engine's contract makes order load-bearing, this probe measures how much of a region set is an artifact of case arrangement. The **two-factor coupling scan** realizes §7's second recommendation in preliminary form: one case set scanned against two recorded factors, with coincident, factor-A-only, and factor-B-only region pairs recorded under an explicit note that coincidence does not establish a shared cause, a confound, or its absence.

Every procedure is deterministic given its seed, every synthetic case carries the synthetic label into the payload, and every payload ends with `interpretation: "unassigned"` plus a limitations list — the same discipline as the engine, now applied to the instrument's own outputs. The test suite grew from 31 to **56 tests, all passing** (25 new), pinning the properties that make the records trustworthy rather than the conclusions strong: determinism given a seed, summary statistics that exactly match their replications, non-increasing region counts under ascending thresholds, exact coincidence partitioning against the underlying scans, case-membership preservation under permutation, and rejection of invalid thresholds, distributions, and duplicate case IDs. CLI parity was kept: `null-scan`, `sweep`, `permute`, and `couple` accept real JSON case files, while `experiments-demo` emits a five-record synthetic stream (four experiments plus a batch audit event whose unresolved questions are the four honestly open ones — null comparison, threshold persistence, order effects, and confound status — each deferred to real data).

### 8.2 Reading the v0.3 demonstration figure

The figure below shows the packaged demo run, and each panel rewards the record-not-verdict reading. The left panel (threshold sweep over eight synthetic cases with two planted steps, of size ~26 and ~51) shows the recorded region count falling from 4 to 3 to 1 as the threshold rises through 1, 5, 10, 20, 30: at threshold 1 the engine records noise-scale wiggles alongside both planted steps, and only the largest step survives threshold 30. The middle panel is the null record: across 25 replications of seeded uniform noise with no planted structure, the engine at threshold 10 records between 7 and 11 candidate regions per replication — a recorded property of the instrument-plus-generator combination, labeled as such, and the reason the lab's README warns that candidate regions are "recorded observations, not detected boundaries." The right panel shows the permutation probe: the two planted-step pairs (amber) appear in 9 and 8 of 26 scans respectively, while permutation-induced pairings (gray) appear up to 11 times — including pairings that never occur in the given order — recording directly that adjacency, not just membership, drives what the engine flags.

None of the three panels licenses a conclusion about any real system: the data are fabricated and labeled, the generators are chosen rather than measured, and the payloads say so. What the panels do establish is that the instrument's behavior is now itself recorded under the same schema as the evidence it processes — which is the precondition for the honest next step, running all four procedures unchanged on a documented real dataset and appending the records to the same stream. The middle panel deserves emphasis because it inverts a common intuition: a thresholded adjacent-delta scan on pure noise *does* produce candidate regions, copiously, and any future real-data run must therefore be read against a null record of the kind shown here rather than against an implicit expectation of zero — which is exactly the discipline the MSA literature (§4.5) imposes on measurement systems and the evaluation-gap literature finds missing in AI assessment.
