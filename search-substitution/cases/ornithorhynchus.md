# CASE — ORNITHORHYNCHUS ANATINUS

*Domain:* sensory neurobiology

*The field's own name for it:* electroreception; electrosensory–mechanosensory
convergence in the platypus bill.

## What happens

The platypus forages with its eyes, ears and nostrils closed, in turbid
water and mud. The bill carries two receptor populations in an interleaved
arrangement: electroreceptors responding to the fields generated by prey
muscle activity, and push-rod mechanoreceptors responding to pressure.

A prey movement emits both at once. The electrical component arrives
effectively instantaneously over these distances; the pressure wave arrives
at the speed of sound in water. The offset between the two arrivals is
proportional to distance.

## Readout

You do not model the environment and then locate the prey. The two signals'
disagreement *is* the position. No intermediate representation.

## The arithmetic

At 1480 m/s in fresh water:

     50 mm  ->   33.8 us
    100 mm  ->   67.6 us
    200 mm  ->  135.1 us

Range recovers by one multiplication. A map-then-query approach over a
half-metre hemisphere at 1 mm resolution would carry ~2.6 x 10^8 cells —
affordable, and unnecessary.

## Standing — read this before using the case

This is the weakest case in the folder. The dual receptor arrangement is
established. Range-from-offset is **Pettigrew's proposed mechanism, not a
measured one**. The "no intermediate representation" half is a claim about
what is absent in a nervous system, and absence was not what the cited work
measured; bill representation in cortex has been described. The case is
carried because the structure is the clearest of the three, and flagged
because that is why it should be distrusted.

## What would break it

A ranging mechanism that does not use the offset. Or a topographic map shown
to be built and queried, which would make the case an ordinary
model-then-locate system wearing an unusual pair of sensors.
