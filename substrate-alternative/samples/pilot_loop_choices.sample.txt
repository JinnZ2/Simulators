pilot_loop [CHOICE n]
  [CHOICE 1] UNMET carries `resource` in addition to the three fields asked for (node, quantity, reason), because a node may need two resources and a bare quantity would not say which one went unfilled.
  [CHOICE 2] Needs are served in order of (horizon_cycle, node_id, resource) -- earliest deadline first, ties broken by id.  Deterministic and declared.  This ordering IS the allocation rule; it is ours, not ICS's.  ICS specifies a request channel (ICS-213RR) and an assignment authority.  It does not specify who wins when two requests meet one pallet.
  [CHOICE 3] Within a need, the nearest eligible capacity is drawn first, ties broken by node id.  Nearest, not largest and not fullest: the quantity this loop reports is lag, so the rule minimises the quantity it reports.  A different rule (drain the largest holder first, to keep small holders in reserve) is defensible and would give a different log.
  [CHOICE 4] A route is directional and is looked up as (from, to).  With symmetric=True (the default) the reverse pair is accepted as a fallback.  An ABSENT pair is UNREACHABLE.  It is never a large number and never zero -- both would put an undeclared route on the same scale as a declared one.
  [CHOICE 5] Arrival is max(ready_cycle, declared_cycle) + travel.  Capacity that is not ready yet delays the arrival rather than being silently available.
  [CHOICE 6] Lag is arrival_cycle - declared_cycle: cycles from the need being declared to the food being there.  Not from departure, and not from the start of the run.
  [CHOICE 7] Per-node readouts are a list of lags plus a max and a min.  Nothing is summed across nodes and nothing is ranked.  One number per node would be a standing for the node, which is a second allocation signal arriving through the readout.
