Variables to pull first. Actigraphy gives you measured sleep, sleep
efficiency, and — this is the one — number and duration of awakenings per
night, separately. Duration of awakenings is exactly your wake_cost,
measured rather than assumed. There's also a fragmentation index, and worth
noting it's built as a sum of two different things, movement index plus
fragmentation index, so it's a composite you'd want to decompose rather than
use whole.

PSG gives you the arousal index and the stage distribution.

The questionnaire gives you self-reported usual hours.

The three-column test: reported hours minus measured sleep, regressed on
awakening count and awakening duration. If the gap grows with fragmentation,
G-SPAN is confirmed on real people and the simulation becomes a supporting
argument rather than the argument.

One flag — seven days of actigraphy per person, so you can also check
night-to-night variability, which the single-number reports throw away
entirely.
