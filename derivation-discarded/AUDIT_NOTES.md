## What the drop is

The third proposed exclusion mechanism for [`../uninstrumented/`](../uninstrumented),
after CATEGORY WELD (9, [`../category-weld/`](../category-weld)) and
GENERATION CAPACITY REMOVED (10,
[`../generation-capacity/`](../generation-capacity)).

**DERIVATION DISCARDED** — alternate handle UNPRICED PRECONDITION, name
not settled. A structure that has persisted is a record of every
constraint that had to be satisfied simultaneously for it to exist, and
the structure is the only copy of that derivation. Removing it removes a
readout; the computation cannot be rerun because the inputs are gone. The
accounting books the structure at extraction or replacement price, has no
line for the discarded derivation, and is therefore *arithmetically
correct and structurally blind* — the removal registers as a gain.

Like mechanism 10's first drop this arrives as a document: statement,
distinctions from three adjacent mechanisms, an anchor case, a null test,
three seed measurements, four open sub-questions, four falsifiers,
cross-links. No scorer, no cases, no README, no claim table.

## 1 — DD_001, the distinguishing test

> after the removal, can any party — including one who wanted to — recover
> what was lost? If the loss is uncountable in principle rather than
> uncounted in practice, it is mechanism 11.

The distinctions from 6, 8 and 10 hold, and the one worth keeping is the
last: **mechanism 10 is a future that cannot be generated, mechanism 11 a
past that cannot be re-derived.** Same direction of loss, opposite side of
the clock.

Where it is weaker than it reads: *"uncountable in principle"* is a modal
claim, and modal claims are not decided by looking. Every other
distinguishing test in this family resolves against a document or a count.
This one is answered by failing to think of a recovery route, and failing
to think of one is exactly what `UNI_005` warns is an absence rather than a
result.

The drop half-anticipates this twice. Sub-question 3 asks for the class of
structure whose derivation *is* recoverable and says that boundary is worth
more than the central case — correct, and the operational form of the same
question. Falsifier 4 states the decidable version outright: *"the 'only
copy' claim fails for any structure whose constraint set is independently
documented."* So the checkable test is already in the document, and the
distinguishing test is stated in the undecidable form.

## 3 — DD_003, three narrowings, never composed

    predictions made                      865
    auditable                             488   (56% of made)
    'accurate' or 'nearly accurate'       383   (78% of auditable)
    unpredicted impacts recorded            6

and, reported separately in the same literature: *"only 30% of the impacts
were unqualifiedly close to their forecasts, with almost as many rated
accurate principally by virtue of the vagueness of the forecasts."*

| narrowing | size | note |
|---|---|---|
| all impacts → predictions made | unmeasured | the drop's point |
| predictions made → auditable | 56% | **non-random**: lack of data, vague or ambiguous predictions, time dependency |
| 'accurate' → unqualifiedly close | ~30% | almost as many scored accurate *by virtue of* vagueness |

    headline                                 ~79%
    accurate as a share of predictions made   44%
    unqualifiedly close (30% of auditable)    17% of predictions made
    ratio, headline to strict reading         4.6x

Narrowing 2 is the sharpest addition, because **the filter selects against
exactly the predictions most likely to be scored wrong.** A vague
prediction is unauditable; a vague prediction that survives to be audited
is scored accurate for being vague. The same property removes it at step 2
or earns it a pass at step 3.

This makes the anchor case stronger than the drop states it, and for a
different reason. The drop says the instrument *"is constituted so that
[the gap] cannot appear."* The literature is more interesting: the gap
**does** appear, in pieces — 56%, ~30%, six unpredicted impacts, the
vagueness caveat — all published, in the same reviews. What never happens
is the multiplication. Each piece reads as a caveat on the headline rather
than as a factor in a product.

That is [`../thermal-sensor-degradation-audit/`](../thermal-sensor-degradation-audit)'s
result on a different substrate: `corruption(trend) =
corruption(measurement) × corruption(framework)`, multiplicative, and
invisible while each term is reported alone.

The 30% figure's base is not unambiguous in what was located, so the
composition is labelled illustrative arithmetic on published components
rather than a recovered statistic.

## 4 — DD_004, the falsifiers

Falsifier 1 stands unfired: every study surfaced scores against the
prediction list, and one review states the constraint directly — the
denominator is *reduced* by data quality and methodological limits, never
widened to total observed impacts.

But R1's numerator is less missing than the drop says. One study reports it
plainly: **six unpredicted impacts** in 865 predictions across 28 projects.

Six is small, which engages falsifier 3 — *"weakens substantially if R1
comes back small."* The literature answers ambiguously, in its own words:

> the reported incidence of such impacts varies greatly across studies …
> unanticipated impacts often only become apparent while investigating
> actual impacts during post-auditing

A count that varies greatly across studies of the same kind of object is
the signature of a quantity set by **search intensity** rather than by the
world, and a count that only appears "while investigating actual impacts"
is conditioned on someone having looked outside the prediction list.
**R1-small is not yet evidence that unanticipated impacts are rare. It is
evidence that R1 is not yet a measurement.** Neither the expected null nor
the feared refutation.

## 5 — DD_005, R2 is also the null test's control

The drop's ranking is right — R2 needs library access, R1 and R3 need
someone to re-read source EISs against monitoring records or to sample
removal approvals.

Worth adding a job the drop does not claim for it. THE NULL TEST expects a
structural null, and a search returning nothing has `UNI_006`'s problem: it
has not been shown the search would find the thing if it were there.

**R2 is the positive control.** It runs the same corpus asking for
something that plainly does exist in some documents — an explicit statement
of the denominator — so a fraction strictly between 0 and 1 demonstrates
the reading protocol can distinguish present from absent. If R2 also
returns zero, the instrument is the problem and not the field.

## 6 — DD_006, the calibration constraint, pre-flagged

Same shape as mechanism 10's, which `../generation-capacity/capacity.py`
implements as `scored_against`. `GC_003` recorded what that buys: a
**declaration**, not a unit check — nothing derives the scoring frame from
the data, so a case that declares the right frame and uses the wrong one
passes clean.

Mechanism 11 states the constraint before any code exists, which is the
cheapest point and a real improvement in sequencing. The limit is
inherited unchanged: *"score against the document's own uncertainty
vocabulary"* is a judgement about which words constitute an uncertainty
vocabulary, and no rule is given.

The three-state readout — documented / asserted / absent, no verdict
computed — is `../presented-binary/binary_audit.py`'s vocabulary exactly,
and `PB_008` recorded that its defaults all run toward `absent`, which is
the safe direction and what R3 would want.
