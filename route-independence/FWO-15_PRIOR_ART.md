## Item 1 -- WEIRD-sample literature: "likely NOT the provision-regime axis. Confirm."

Result: CONFIRMED for the WEIRD paper itself. NOT CONFIRMED for the
research program around it.

- Henrich, Heine & Norenzayan 2010, *Behavioral and Brain Sciences*
  33(2-3):61-83. The paper criticizes broad claims about human psychology
  drawn from Western, Educated, Industrialized, Rich and Democratic
  samples. The axis is cultural sampling. "Industrialized" and "Rich"
  name an economic position; neither names how subjects meet water, food
  or thermal needs. (philpapers.org/rec/HENTWP; henrich.fas.harvard.edu)
- The same research group MEASURED the food leg of the provision regime
  as a variable. Henrich et al. 2005, *BBS* (15 small-scale societies),
  and Henrich et al. 2010, *Science* 327:1480: "market integration
  (measured as the percentage of purchased calories) positively covaries
  with fairness". (econ.uzh.ch Henrich_et_al_in_BBS_Dec_05.pdf;
  science.org/doi/10.1126/science.1182238)

What this does to the order's object. The order's DERIVED line says that
for humans the provision-regime caveat is absent, and marks that as
Claude's read, NOT searched. Search finds the axis present as a measured
covariate in one human sub-literature, for food only (not water, thermal
regulation or homeostasis), and with the share of purchased calories
moving the behavioral outcome. That is evidence the gate changes what is
measured. It is not a CAVEAT on generalization, which is what E4 codes.
So the claim narrows: the axis is not unmeasured in human behavioral
science; whether it is declared as a validity limit on samples is still
unsearched. EXPECTED E15.3 registers the consequence and is marked not
blind.

## Item 2 -- captive-animal external-validity standard

Result: LOCATED, two layers.

- Reporting standard. ARRIVE 2.0, Recommended Set item 15 "Housing and
  husbandry": details of housing and husbandry conditions including
  environmental enrichment; the elaboration lists temperature, humidity,
  light cycle, food and water. That covers the water, food and thermal
  legs of the order's E2 for animal studies. (journals.plos.org
  pbio.3000411; arriveguidelines.org)
- Validity literature. Snippets state that captive environments produce
  behavioral changes that may limit generalization back to natural
  populations, and that the effects of the captive environment are rarely
  assessed. (wellbeingintlstudiesrepository.org "Behavioral Research on
  Captive Animals"; PMC11949117; PubMed 15775020 on stereotypies and
  validity, reliability and replicability)

The second layer matters for Build A. The animal arm's standard is a
DECLARATION of housing (E2). The CAVEAT (E4) is described in the same
literature as rarely assessed. EXPECTED E15.4 registers that split before
any coding.
