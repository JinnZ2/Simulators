
THE SHADOW-SIM PATTERN, TESTED
A study of a proposed protocol. No triad experiment was run.

==========================================================================
1  step 5 forbids the design step 6 requires
==========================================================================
  The protocol says:
    step 5  'Never upgrade all three simultaneously (can't
             attribute variance).'
    step 6  'Cross-gradient: did conclusion change with dial
             setting?'

  Step 5 is one-factor-at-a-time. Step 6 asks for an interaction.
  OFAT cannot estimate an interaction, because no run in an OFAT
  design varies two factors together.

  Truth planted in the response: main effects P=1.0 I=0.5 R=2.0,
  interaction P*R = 3.0

  OFAT, 4 runs, baseline plus one dial each:
    P      1.00
    I      0.50
    R      2.00
    P*R     n/a   <- no run has both dials up

    OFAT predicts y(P=1, R=1) = 3.0. Truth = 6.0. Error = 3.0,
    which is the entire interaction, invisible and unattributed.

  2^3 factorial, 8 runs, orthogonal +/-1 contrasts:
    P      2.00   truth   2.00
    I      1.00   truth   1.00
    R      4.00   truth   4.00
    P*R    6.00   truth   6.00
    P*I    0.00   truth   0.00

  Every effect recovered exactly, interaction included, for four
  extra runs. The protocol's stated reason for OFAT -- 'can't
  attribute variance' -- is exactly backwards: a factorial design
  attributes variance to each factor AND to their interaction,
  which OFAT cannot do at any number of runs.

  Fix: replace step 5 with a 2^3 factorial over the three dials at
  low/high, then upgrade the axis with the largest effect. Eight
  runs is still cheap, and it is the only design that answers
  step 6.

==========================================================================
2  consensus among shadows is blind to what shadows share
==========================================================================
  The protocol: 'Do the three agree? If not, the axis is
  underdetermined.' Read as a test, agreement is the pass.

  But the four shadows -- you rested, you fatigued, AI-low,
  AI-high -- are not independent. They read the same physical
  declaration, the same instrument output, and for the AI shadows
  the same prompt, written by one of the human shadows.

  Model each shadow as   truth + shared_bias + individual_noise.
  Spread across shadows measures individual_noise. It cannot see
  shared_bias at all, because shared_bias moves every shadow the
  same way.

     shared bias    mean shadow         spread error vs truth
             0.0         100.01           2.04         0.01
             2.0         102.00           2.04         2.00
             5.0         105.01           2.06         5.01
            20.0         120.00           2.02        20.00

  The spread column does not move. The error column tracks the
  bias one-for-one. Four shadows agreeing tightly at 120 when the
  truth is 100 is exactly what this looks like from inside.

  So 'do the shadows agree?' has no null. Before agreement means
  anything you need the disagreement between two runs of the SAME
  observer at the SAME dial -- the noise floor -- and a way to
  detect the shared term, which consensus cannot supply.

  Both already exist in this repo. ../divergence-playground/ is
  this protocol built with the null: readings are hash-sealed
  before reveal so later readers cannot anchor on earlier ones,
  spread is computed on three declared axes rather than eyeballed,
  and null_ensemble.py supplies shuffle and permutation nulls.
  Its agree_by_accident flag is the cell this protocol needs most:
  shadows that reach the same verdict by different mechanisms.

==========================================================================
3  the first experiment cannot fail its own skip condition
==========================================================================
  Aluminium bar, 1 m, 23.1 ppm/K, 60 K swing:
    expansion               1.386 mm
    dial division           0.010 mm
    signal / division         139     -- the expansion is easy

  But the experiment is not about the expansion. It is about
  observer variance, and the skip condition reads:

    'If all four observers agree within instrument resolution,
     observer variance is negligible.'

    observer reading spread   ~0.0050 mm  (half a division)
    instrument resolution      0.0100 mm
    ratio                      0.50

  Four people reading one mechanical dial agree to within a
  division because a division is the quantum of what the dial can
  say. The skip condition fires whether or not observer variance
  exists, so 'negligible' is a statement about the dial.

  This is null-harness's CONSTANT_SILENT: a gate that cannot fire
  has not been shown to work. It is also G-RES -- a null from an
  instrument that could not have seen the feature.

  The fix is cheap and does not need a better bar. The instrument
  must record independently of the observer reading it:
    - digital indicator with a data log, or a photograph of the
      dial timestamped at each reading
    - observer writes down a value without seeing the log
    - observer error = |observer value - logged value|, directly
  Observer variance stops being inferred from consensus and
  becomes a measured residual against a recorded reference.

  With that change the skip condition can fail, which is what
  makes running it worth anything.

==========================================================================
4  the worked example shows a different failure than claimed
==========================================================================
  From the drop, one physical dial setting, three reasoning dials:

    low     'crack at 200 cycles'        pattern-matched from a
                                          previous casting
    medium  'crack at 1,800 +/- 400'     propagated uncertainty
    high    'no crack; the heuristic was wrong because this is
             wrought, not cast'

  The drop reads this as a reasoning-dial cross-gradient, and as a
  G-LAYER violation: the reasoning claim outruns what the physical
  evidence carries.

  Run 3 says something else. It reports that runs 1 and 2 were
  answering a question about cast aluminium while the specimen is
  wrought. That is not the observer's gain varying against a fixed
  system -- it is the physical declaration being wrong, and the
  high-dial observer catching it.

  The distinction is load-bearing for the whole protocol. If a
  mis-specified physical system is scored as reasoning-dial
  variance, then every physical error the reasoning agent catches
  inflates the measured observer variance -- and the playground
  concludes the observer is unreliable when what happened is that
  the observer was right.

  Both are worth measuring; they are different quantities. The
  separator is whether the physical declaration survives the run.
  In triad.json that is `physical.state_declared` versus
  `physical.state_revised_during_run`, and a run that revises it
  reports a physical finding, not a reasoning gradient.

==========================================================================
READING
==========================================================================

  The triad framing holds and is the useful part. Naming the reasoning agent
  as an instrument with its own calibration is the move the rest of this repo
  has been circling: reasoning-gate tags quantities by layer,
  instrument-epistemology grades instruments, and neither has a slot for the
  observer. This does.

  The shadow-sim pattern, as specified, does not yet measure it.

  Step 5's one-factor-at-a-time rule forbids the only design that estimates
  the cross-gradient step 6 asks for. Consensus among shadows measures the
  error they do not share and is blind to the error they do -- which, for
  shadows reading one declaration through one prompt, is the larger term. And
  the proposed first experiment's skip condition fires regardless of the
  truth, because four people reading one mechanical dial agree to within a
  division by construction.

  All three have cheap fixes, and none of them requires abandoning the idea:
  a 2^3 factorial instead of OFAT; hash-sealed readings with a null ensemble,
  which ../divergence-playground/ already implements; and an instrument that
  logs independently of the observer reading it, which turns observer variance
  from something inferred by consensus into a measured residual.

  What this does not establish: that observer variance matters at any
  particular scale. Section 3's fix makes it measurable; nobody has measured
  it. The playground's first real result is still unrun, and it is one bar,
  one dial indicator, one afternoon.
