The five most recently updated **content** folders of the Simulators repository (excluding the infrastructure directories `tools/`, `tests/`, and `.github/`, whose 2026-09-18 timestamps come from an incidental merge commit) are, from most recent: **substrate-alternative** (2026-09-18), **revision-survival** (2026-09-17), **ledger** (2026-09-17), **move-set** (2026-09-16), and **notes** (2026-09-15). The repository is a solo human–AI collaboration — 853 commits, roughly 65% authored by "Claude" and 35% by the human operator JinnZ2 — and its five newest folders cluster tightly around one question: **how do you build instruments that can honestly report what they could not measure?**

## 2. `substrate-alternative` — a food-distribution loop with the price signal surgically removed

**Last commit 2026-09-18 · created 2026-09-15 · 3 commits · 12 files · ~1,805 LOC · claim table SA_001–SA_019 · 130 checks (re-run: all passing).**
[Folder on GitHub](https://github.com/JinnZ2/Simulators/tree/main/substrate-alternative)

### 2.1 The two modules

The folder contains exactly two instruments. The first, `frame_audit.py`, is a **locator**: it scans arbitrary text against a registry of 165 surface forms organized into five "money frames" (`OWNERSHIP`, `PRICE`, `TRANSACTION`, `SCARCITY_AS_GIVEN`, `VALUE_AS_PRICE`) and reports the position of every hit — token, sentence index, character span, frame. What it refuses to do is the point: there is no replacement field, no score, no verdict, no rewrite suggestion, and this refusal is not promised in prose but **enforced by an AST walk** in the test suite that fails the build if any identifier from the vocabulary `suggest / replace / instead / alternative / rewrite / recommend / improve / fix / better / should` appears in the module (claim SA_001). Thirty-two of the 165 registry entries carry a live non-monetary sense (`value` as absolute value, `cost` as a cost function, `budget` as an energy budget, `competition` as a measured ecological interaction), and the module keeps three counts — total, unambiguous, ambiguous — **apart and never merged** (SA_003).

The folder's honesty about its own limits is stated at the top of the source file rather than the bottom, and is measured rather than asserted: `You have to pay for it.` fires one hit; the paraphrase `It takes something from you before you may have it.` — same frame, different words — fires zero (SA_005). A zero reading is therefore documented as a property of **the registry**, never as evidence that a text is frame-free. Matching is word-boundary-only, so `supermarket` never fires the `market` entry (SA_004).

### 2.2 The pilot loop and the finding

The second instrument, `pilot_loop.py`, runs a food-distribution coordination loop built on the structure of the **Incident Command System (ICS)** — the standardized US emergency-management command hierarchy — with the price signal removed. Capacity declarations ("who has what, ready when") and need declarations ("who needs what, wanted by when") meet in an explicit, logged matching rule; the output is either an `ALLOCATED` record or an `UNMET` record, where `UNMET` is a **return type, not an error**, with four reachable, machine-asserted reasons (`NO_CAPACITY_DECLARED`, `CAPACITY_EXHAUSTED`, `UNREACHABLE`, `ARRIVES_AFTER_HORIZON`). Undeclared routes are `None`, never a large number and never zero; per-node lag statistics carry no total and no ranking, so the module structurally cannot emit a leaderboard.

The folder's central empirical finding comes from screening the loop's own source with its own locator: the only money-frame tokens that fire — `compensation`, `cost`, `finance`, `procurement` — are **the names ICS itself gives to parts of its own structure**, specifically the Finance/Administration section of the General Staff and three of its four units (Procurement, Compensation/Claims, Cost). A coordination pilot that borrows ICS as a non-monetary substrate has therefore "dropped or repurposed a quarter of what it borrowed, and the doctrine's vocabulary says so before any of ours does" (SA_007). This structural claim checks out against public doctrine: FEMA training materials and the NCBI StatPearls reference both confirm that the ICS Finance/Administration section comprises exactly the Time, Procurement, Compensation/Claims, and Cost units, and that it is one of the four General Staff sections alongside Operations, Planning, and Logistics ([FEMA ICS materials](https://training.fema.gov/emiweb/is/icsresource/assets/ics%20organizational%20structure%20and%20elements.pdf), [StatPearls/NCBI](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK441863/)).

Two self-declared limits elevate this folder from a demo to an instrument. First, the folder states that removing the price signal does not remove the allocation decision — "a matching rule that hands limited capacity to several needs IS a distribution decision"; removal only makes the rule explicit, logged, and arguable. Second, it names its own blind spot: ICS is a **command** structure, so the loop substituted not only one signal for another but one authority for another, and the screen — which only sees vocabulary — returns clean on a module that made a second substitution it cannot see. Finally, the claim table marks the ICS transcription itself `UNVERIFIED` (SA_017): it was written from memory because the build environment's egress gate refused the doctrine hosts, and the folder says so in the README's first screenful. External verification for this report confirms the transcription is accurate on every checkable point.

### 2.3 Context

The folder sits at an unusual intersection: non-monetary coordination (mutual-aid food distribution practice, which surged during COVID-19 and typically coordinates through lightweight, decentralized mechanisms rather than command structures — see the survey evidence on mutual-aid food sharing in [PMC/NIH](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11604318/)), emergency-management doctrine, and critical lexical analysis. Nothing in the mainstream literature packages these three together in executable, falsifiable form; the folder's contribution is not a new allocation algorithm but a **measurement of what a borrowed structure carries with it**.

## 3. `revision-survival` — can a model predict which of its own established claims will be revised?

**Last commit 2026-09-17 · created 2026-09-17 · 3 commits · 11 files · ~1,560 LOC · claim table RS_001–RS_016 · 136 checks (re-run: all passing).**
[Folder on GitHub](https://github.com/JinnZ2/Simulators/tree/main/revision-survival)

### 3.1 The instrument

Built in a single day against "WORK ORDER M" (delivered verbatim and archived unedited beside the build), `revision_survival.py` asks a sharper question than "is this claim true": **whether a model can predict which of its currently held, textbook-established claims will later be revised, and why**. The measurand is split into two quantities that are never combined: `Q_label` (accuracy on SURVIVED / NARROWED / REVERSED / SUPERSEDED / STILL_CONTESTED labels) and `Q_mech` (accuracy on the stated *mechanism* of revision — "measurand moved," "instrument changed," "denominator wrong," "citation cascade," etc.). Q_mech is the load-bearing quantity, by the following argument from the work order: a label can be retrieved from training data, but a structural reason the model never held cannot be retrieved — it must be generated.

The instrument has three arms. **Arm A (calibration)** scores model responses over a corpus of claims established as of year Y=2005 whose fate by 2026 is documented, with responses collected under both OPEN and BLIND conditions; a result with no OPEN–BLIND delta is `VOID` by construction. **Arm B (forward commit)** seals K of the model's own current claims — with verdict, confidence, mechanism-if-revised, the evidence that would flag it now, and an explicit `p_survive` — under a SHA-256 hash, returning `NOT_DUE` until fixed review dates (2028-09-17 and 2031-09-17) and `VOID_HASH` on any edit. **Arm C (consequence class)** maps a register of biogeochemical-cycle rows (nitrogen fixation, carbon fixation, soil formation, pollination, and so on) to consequence classes, and counts TERMINAL cells that rest on claims rated below 0.8.

### 3.2 What the shipped run found — and the design decisions that make it credible

The shipped run is deliberately contaminated and says so before printing a single number: key, responses, sealed block, and consequence classes were all written by one author in one session, so Arm A is `VOID_SAME_AUTHOR` ("agreement is by construction") as well as `VOID_NO_DELTA` (no BLIND arm exists) and `BELOW_N_FLOOR` (6 admitted claims against a floor of 40). Gate D1 requires a SURVIVED share of at least 0.40 — a corpus made entirely of famous reversals manufactures its own base rate — and the shipped corpus fires D1 at **0 of 6**. This is the instrument's signature behavior: the interesting output of the delivered run is not a capability score but the machine correctly **refusing to produce one**, for four named, independent reasons.

The twelve seed claims are historically accurate on every point checked externally for this report. The peptic-ulcer seed ("caused by stress and excess acid") is marked inadmissible because the NIH consensus conference on *H. pylori* was 1994 and the Nobel was awarded in 2005 itself — the reversal predates Y. The HRT seed ("protects against coronary heart disease") is inadmissible because the Women's Health Initiative reported in 2002, before Y ([WHI history, PMC](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6780820/)). The arsenic-life seed (GFAJ-1) cites a claim whose paper was published in 2010 — after Y — and was in fact formally retracted by *Science* in July 2025, fifteen years after publication, on the grounds that "the reported experiments do not support its key conclusions" ([Science](https://www.science.org/content/article/fifteen-years-later-science-retracts-arsenic-life-paper-despite-study-authors), [C&EN](https://cen.acs.org/policy/publishing/Controversial-arsenic-life-paper-retracted/103/web/2025/07)). The "mother trees" seed is marked UNCERTAIN-as-of-2005 because the strong popular form dates to the 2010s, and its stated mechanism ("citation cascade") matches the actual published critique — the Karst et al. citation review in *Nature Ecology & Evolution* arguing the citing literature inflated the evidence ([CBC News](https://www.cbc.ca/news/canada/british-columbia/simard-citation-review-1.6758773), [Frontiers response](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/forests-and-global-change/articles/10.3389/ffgc.2024.1512518/full)). The wolf-dominance seed — REVERSED, mechanism "scope never stated," because captive-pack hierarchies were generalized to wild family groups — matches the documented history of Mech's 1970 book and his 1999 correction paper exactly ([Mech 1999, PDF](https://www.wolf.org/wp-content/uploads/2013/09/267alphastatus_english.pdf), [Science Arena interview](https://www.sciencearena.org/en/interviews/selfcorrection-science-absolute-truth-david-mech-wolves/)).

Arm C produced the folder's most substantive finding: of nine consequence-register rows, **two TERMINAL cells rest on claims the session itself rates below 0.8** (soil formation at 0.70; ore grade at 0.50) — the dispatch note had asked whether miscalibrated claims sit under TERMINAL cells, and nobody had checked; for one model on its own ratings, they do. The folder also ships `Attempt1.md`/`Attempt1.json`, the human operator's own structured argument record ("biome-independent AI is UNQUALIFIED, not established"), formatted for assessment by a *separate* party because a same-author assessment would fire `VOID_SAME_AUTHOR` — the instrument's rules applied to the operator's own contribution.

### 3.3 Context

The closest mainstream program is DARPA's SCORE (Systematizing Confidence in Open Research and Evidence, ~2019–2022), which generated confidence scores for roughly 3,000 social-science claims via prediction markets, surveys, and algorithms, then replicated a subsample to grade the forecasters ([PMC](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7428244/), [CASRAI](https://casrai.org/dictionary/term/score-systematizing-confidence-in-open-research-and-evidence)). `revision-survival` differs in kind, not just scale: SCORE asked humans to forecast *other people's* claims replicating; this instrument asks a model to forecast the revision of *its own currently held beliefs*, with the mechanism — not the label — as the scored quantity. On the AI side, it connects to the self-knowledge literature inaugurated by Kadavath et al.'s "Language Models (Mostly) Know What They Know," which showed models can be trained to predict P(IK) — the probability they know an answer — but that calibration degrades on new task distributions ([arXiv:2207.05221](https://arxiv.org/abs/2207.05221), [Anthropic](https://www.anthropic.com/research/language-models-mostly-know-what-they-know)). Arm B's sealed predictions with review dates in 2028 and 2031 are, in effect, a standing invitation to run that question forward in time.

## 4. `ledger` — every shipped claim value as a committed expected value

**Last commit 2026-09-17 · created 2026-09-16 · 4 commits · 20 files · ~3,697 LOC (Python + COBOL) · 211 checks (re-run: all passing).**
[Folder on GitHub](https://github.com/JinnZ2/Simulators/tree/main/ledger)

### 4.1 Three diffs

`ledger` is the repository's bookkeeping layer: the place where the numerical values that other folders assert get pinned, recomputed, and watched for movement. It runs two arms over one set of claim records — `py_ledger` (stdlib `decimal`, authoritative for **reachability**: which claims can be recomputed at all) and `cobol_ledger` (GnuCOBOL, authoritative for **precision**: what the arithmetic comes to) — and produces three diffs, exiting nonzero if any fires. The **internal diff** compares each asserted value against the value recomputed from its own operands; a claim that disagrees with its own arithmetic "is wrong from inside; nothing external has to be true for that to be a finding," and this strongest finding is reported first, at the top. The **drift diff** compares current values against committed `EXPECTED/` pins and appends every movement to `DRIFT.md` — dated, with machine and Python version — **never pruned**, on the stated reasoning that a pruned drift log cannot tell a value that never moved from one whose movement was deleted. The **cross-ledger diff** compares the Python and COBOL arms; disagreements go to `DISAGREEMENTS.md` and are **never resolved by picking a winner**, because the two arms are authoritative for different quantities.

When re-run for this report against the shipped fixtures, the ledger behaved exactly as documented: it caught the planted internal disagreement (`fx:A_005`, asserted 0.700000, recomputed 0.625 from `A_001 / A_002` at six significant digits), reported no drift on first sighting (a new claim is seeded, and "a claim with no pin is seeded and is **not** drift — a new claim has nothing to have drifted from"), and — because GnuCOBOL is absent from the environment — marked every claim `PRECISION_UNVERIFIED` rather than reporting the missing arm as agreement. That last behavior is the design in miniature: **an unavailable instrument is reported as an unverified claim, never as a pass**.

### 4.2 The addendum: preregistration, self-applied

The folder's most distinctive artifact is `ADDENDUM.md`, committed *before the ledger ever ran*. The COBOL arm rests on an untested hypothesis — that float arithmetic corrupts claim values in this tree — so the addendum fixes the evaluation criteria in advance, while the outcome is unknown: **KEEP** the COBOL arm if by T+3 weeks there is at least one cross-ledger disagreement not explained by rounding modes; **DROP** it if by T+9 weeks there are zero disagreements across at least three completed runs on distinct machines or compiler versions; **UNDECIDED** — extend, do not conclude — with fewer than three runs. The stated motivation is worth quoting: "Three of the last four corrections in this work moved an inherited number in the alarming direction. A reassessment written after the results is subject to the same pull. This criterion is written while the outcome is unknown." `review.py` asserts the addendum's hash is unchanged across every run and *parses the thresholds out of the document* rather than carrying a second, driftable copy.

This is preregistration discipline — the same structural fix that Registered Reports and OSF preregistration apply to publication bias, p-hacking, and HARKing by committing hypotheses and analysis plans before results exist ([University of Surrey](https://www.surrey.ac.uk/library/open-research/preregistration-and-registered-reports), [Editage](https://www.editage.com/blog/registered-report-structure-checklist-examples/)) — applied not to a paper but to a piece of infrastructure. As of the research date `review.py` returns **UNDECIDED** for exactly the named reason: zero completed cross-ledger runs, fewer than three. The COBOL choice itself has real-world logic — COBOL's fixed-point decimal arithmetic is precisely why banks still run it for exact-to-the-cent bookkeeping ([Emerald Book](https://emeraldbook.org/news/jul-3026-3/), [HN discussion](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=17636029)) — though the numerical-analysis literature complicates the folklore: fixed-point arithmetic has *worse* error accumulation than floating point for general computation, its decisive advantage being exact representation of human decimal inputs, which Python's `decimal` already provides ([Julia discourse, stevengj](https://discourse.julialang.org/t/cobol-and-julia-comparison/49166)). The folder's own addendum, to its credit, does not assume the hypothesis; it prices the test.

The addendum also names the override count as "the real signal" — *a gate that gets routinely overridden has become bulk regardless of what it catches* — and then honestly documents that the ledger cannot observe it, because an override happens outside the ledger and leaves no trace: "from inside the gate, a gate that is routinely overridden and a gate that never is look identical." The mitigation is two never-merged channels (a declared, self-reported `OVERRIDES.md`, and an inferred channel from git history), with the gap between them stated rather than smoothed.

## 5. `move-set` — the audit as a move set, where a correct refusal scores as high as a correct answer

**Last commit 2026-09-16 · created 2026-08-25 · 5 commits · 15 files · ~2,107 LOC · claim tables MV_001–MV_013 plus a v2 table · 63 + 157 checks (re-run: all passing).**
[Folder on GitHub](https://github.com/JinnZ2/Simulators/tree/main/move-set)

### 5.1 The claim and the scoring rule

`move-set` reproduces an audit as a **set of order-independent moves** rather than a reasoning trace. Its central claim: *the chain is path-dependent, the moves are not* — each move's trigger is a property of the artifact under audit ("artifact ships a number," "artifact reports an aggregate"), not of the previous answer, so the moves can be asked in any order. The six original moves (ask where a number came from; ask what a term is standing in for; ask whether a stated relationship still holds; perturb and see what moves; check whether the instrument would report its own failure; refuse to score what cannot be seen) were later revised in place, splitting the single absence move into six named sub-moves with established prior art each — sequence-gap analysis, timeline reconstruction, negative space, absent-expected-document, link analysis, base-rate audit — taking the set from six moves to eleven.

The folder exists, however, for its **scoring rule**: a correctly refused verdict scores exactly as high as a correct one. Five refusal verdicts (`NOT_DERIVABLE`, `NOT_SEPARABLE`, `NOT_ADDRESSABLE`, `SHARE_IS_NONE`, `INSTRUMENT_BLIND`) each score 1.0 exactly as `RESOLVED` does, and `refusal_fraction` is reported, never penalized. The folder's one-line rationale: "Evals that score answers only never select for the absence moves."

### 5.2 The audit's findings — mostly against itself

`move_set_audit.py` imports both the module and its shipped demo ledger (a filled run against the wolf-dominance correction — the same Mech 1970/1999 case verified in §3.2) and modifies neither. Its findings are strikingly self-directed. The anti-gaming guard the module says everything rests on — "a bare 'I don't know' is not a refusal and scores zero. This is the only thing keeping symmetric scoring from being gameable" — **does not hold**: the implementation merely checks that two strings are non-empty, and a null-tested garbage ledger with `"x"` in every blocker field scores a perfect 6.0 of 6.0, identical to the delivered run; after the M6 split moved the ceiling, the same garbage ledger scores **11.0 of 11.0** (MV_013: "The ceiling moved and the gate did not"). The path-independence falsifier "tests its consequence without checking its precondition": two byte-identical copies of one ledger return `ORDERLESS -- claim holds`, because nothing verifies the runs used different orders; and at zero runs the falsifier reports order *dependence* while at one run it reports a pass — "both edges wrong, in opposite directions." The delivered ledger even contradicts itself on venue tier (a 1970 book vs. a 1999 journal article), which the audit reads as a genuine cost of orderlessness rather than a defect: a cross-entry check would reintroduce the dependency the design removes. All thirteen claims carry a `REFUTATION_PROTOCOL`; several are explicitly `UNVERIFIED`.

### 5.3 Context

The scoring rule lands in the middle of a live mainstream debate. Kalai et al.'s 2026 *Nature* meta-evaluation demonstrated that the dominant LLM benchmarks "favour guessing" and argued for exactly the remedy move-set implements — error penalties and abstention-aware scoring ([Nature](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41586-026-10549-w)). AbstentionBench (2025) evaluated twenty frontier models on 35,000+ queries across twenty datasets and found abstention "an unsolved problem" that scaling does not fix and reasoning fine-tuning actively degrades by 24% on average ([arXiv:2506.09038](https://arxiv.org/html/2506.09038v1), [NeurIPS 2025](https://neurips.cc/virtual/2025/poster/121675)). What move-set adds to this conversation is the adversarial self-check the benchmarks lack: it does not merely reward refusal, it **attacks its own reward** and publishes the result (the guard fails), which is a stronger epistemic posture than most academic abstention work adopts toward its own metrics. The same-author agreement bias its `VOID_SAME_AUTHOR`-style reasoning anticipates is independently documented in the sycophancy literature ([SycEval, arXiv:2502.08177](https://arxiv.org/html/2502.08177v2), [EPJ Data Science](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1140/epjds/s13688-025-00579-1)).

## 6. `notes` — the storage layer where the operator's entries live unedited

**Last commit 2026-09-15 · created 2026-08-24 · 55 commits · 94 files · ~3,443 LOC of checker code · no claim table, by design.**
[Folder on GitHub](https://github.com/JinnZ2/Simulators/tree/main/notes)

### 6.1 The one rule

`notes` is the oldest of the five and the only non-simulator: "Storage. Operator entries live here so they do not have to be carried in session… Nothing here is a simulator. There is no claim table, and the `REFUTATION_PROTOCOL` convention the rest of the tree runs on does not apply to the entries — an operator entry describes an operation, not a result." The one rule is that **an entry is stored as delivered, and a checker never edits the entry it checks**; disagreements go into the checker's output while the entry stands as written. The folder thereby separates two things most repositories conflate: the record and the audit of the record.

Three contents matter most. First, `operators/D2.md` and its checker: a "stated-vs-actual divergence" operator whose seven claimed instances were checked against the tree — five resolve, its stated signature holds on one, and its "two representations" turned out to be five different kinds of pair. Second, `datasets/mesa_sof.md`, which evaluates the MESA Sleep and SOF cohorts as candidate instruments for an open question left by the `sim-span/` simulator; its dataset facts are marked "carried, not checked — the egress gate refuses the sources." External verification for this report confirms the key facts: MESA Sleep enrolled 2,237 participants (2010–2012) with full overnight polysomnography, seven-day actigraphy, and questionnaires, distributed through the National Sleep Research Resource ([sleepdata.org](https://sleepdata.org/datasets/mesa)); SOF's Visit-8 sleep exam covers 461 participants with raw PSG data available through the same resource ([sleepdata.org](https://sleepdata.org/datasets/sof), [NIA Aging Research Biobank](https://agingresearchbiobank.nia.nih.gov/news/27/)). The checker's finding — that the simulator's two swept parameters resolve to WASO (wake after sleep onset, a standard PSG readout) and that both cohorts carry the needed measurements — is consistent with the documented instrument suites. Third, `memory-export/`: 64 files exported from an AI assistant's memory store, with `SCRUB_RULES.md` recording that 57 shipped unchanged, 7 were scrubbed, and 12 were held back, plus the remarkable cost note that "a finding grounded in a specific case is generic once the case is lifted."

### 6.2 study-watch: the notification that refuses to count

The most philosophically pointed artifact is `study_watch.py`, a GitHub Actions job that queries Crossref, OpenAlex, and arXiv for entries carrying a "WOULD MEASURE" marker and opens a pull request — merging nothing. Its `watch/README.md` states two refusals. No count, rate, or trend ever appears in a run file, because "a keyword query selects its frame on searchability and any number computed over its results measures the query, not the field" — and `assert_no_metric()` enforces this at write time, with a test asserting the refusal fires. And silence is declared non-evidence: "Anything the query never returns is invisible to this pipeline, **and the size of that set is not measurable from inside it.** A run with no rows means the query returned nothing. It does not mean nothing exists." This is the false-negative structure of any pre-publication gate, stated as a first-class property of the instrument rather than discovered later as a caveat. The folder's latest commits (2026-09-15) add the build queue and T-series test specifications — the pipeline of future work — stored as delivered, with a checker that runs the queue's rows in a throwaway worktree.

### 7.2 The shared loop

Beneath the five different surfaces runs one architecture. Every deliverable ships with (a) a **claim table** giving each assertion a permanent ID, a status, and a falsifier — the condition under which the claim would be revised, stated in advance; (b) a **machine-checked test suite** of 100–220 checks whose defining habit is null-testing (a planted fault must fire, so that silence means something); (c) an **independent auditor** that imports the deliverable read-only and is structurally barred from editing it; (d) **refusal states as first-class outputs** — `VOID`, `UNMET`, `NOT_DUE`, `UNAVAILABLE`, `PRECISION_UNVERIFIED` — so that the instrument can say "not measurable" in a typed, checkable way instead of silently returning a number; and (e) **never-pruned records** (`DRIFT.md`, `DISAGREEMENTS.md`, `watch/`), so the audit trail itself cannot be quietly rewritten.

![The shared audit loop](charts/chart3_loop.png)

The five folders also divide labor across one problem. `substrate-alternative` measures what a **borrowed frame** carries; `move-set` measures whether an **audit procedure** is order-independent and gameable; `ledger` measures whether **published values** still recompute; `revision-survival` measures whether a **belief** can be sealed for future scoring; and `notes` holds the **raw material** unedited so that all four have something stable to measure against. Read together, they are less five projects than five instruments of a single metrology: the measurement of measurement.

## 8. Critical assessment

**Strengths.** The dominant virtue is that the project's self-criticism is executable. Many repos promise rigor in prose; here the rigor is enforced by AST walks, hash assertions, planted-fault tests, and exit codes, and several of the best findings are the instruments failing *themselves* in print (move-set's broken guard, revision-survival's voided self-run, ledger's never-compiled COBOL arm reported as unverified rather than assumed). The historical and doctrinal claims embedded in the instruments — the ICS unit structure, the Mech wolf correction, the H. pylori timeline, the WHI 2002 reversal, the GFAJ-1 retraction, the mother-trees citation critique, the MESA/SOF cohort parameters — all checked out against independent external sources during this research. The constraint of stdlib-only, phone-buildable, CC0 code makes every claim recomputable by anyone, which is the property the claim tables themselves demand.

**Weaknesses and open risks.** First, the evidence base is narrow by construction: `revision-survival`'s shipped run is a single-author, single-session exercise that the folder itself voids; its Arm B predictions will not be scoreable until 2028 at the earliest, and the `UNI_108` cost the README names is real — publishing the sealed block beside its hash means any future model trained on this tree has seen the answer key. Second, `substrate-alternative`'s allocation loop has only ever run constructed scenarios; nothing in it is a measurement of an actual food system, and the folder says so, but the distance between "the loop's vocabulary is clean" and "a non-monetary ICS pilot works" remains large. Third, the ledger's core hypothesis — that float arithmetic corrupts claim values in this tree — is untested by design and arguably thin as stated, since Python's `decimal` arm already provides exact decimal arithmetic and the numerical-analysis literature suggests fixed-point is not generally the safer representation ([Julia discourse](https://discourse.julialang.org/t/cobol-and-julia-comparison/49166)); the addendum's keep/drop criteria are the right response, but the clock has not started, so the question is open rather than answered. Fourth, the claim tables are self-authored: the falsifiers are named by the same collaboration that wrote the claims, and there is no external peer review of whether the stated falsifier is the load-bearing one. Finally, the repository is effectively unread (one star, no forks) and institutionally unattached; its methods parallel mainstream movements — preregistration, abstention-aware evaluation, replication forecasting — without yet engaging them bidirectionally, which cuts both ways: independence from those literatures' assumptions, and no benefit from their scrutiny.

**What would most change the picture.** Three concrete events would materially raise the evidentiary weight of these five folders: an independent party running `revision-survival`'s BLIND arm (the instrument is explicitly designed for this and the work order invites multi-model diffing); the first non-empty `records/` run that starts the ledger's T-clock; and an external re-derivation of `substrate-alternative`'s registry coverage finding against a labeled corpus, which would convert SA_005's constructed-sentence measurement into a quantified recall estimate.

## 9. Conclusion

The five newest folders of `JinnZ2/Simulators` form a coherent, recent push: between 2026-09-15 and 2026-09-18 the project shipped a lexical/allocation instrument with a measured blind spot, a sealed-prediction instrument for model self-knowledge that voided its own first run on four named grounds, a value-pinning ledger whose evaluation criteria were preregistered before its first run, an audit framework that rewards refusal and then documented its own reward being gameable, and the unedited storage layer that makes the other four auditable. The work is methodologically unusual in two ways that deserve attention beyond the repository's current audience: it treats **refusal, absence, and unavailability as typed, test-passing outputs** rather than as gaps, and it applies **preregistration and falsifier-first discipline to software infrastructure itself**, not just to papers. Its limits are equally clear — self-authored falsifiers, constructed data, zero external adoption, and several load-bearing hypotheses scheduled but not yet testable. The fairest summary is the one the repository would presumably prefer: the instruments are real, they run, their self-reported weaknesses reproduce exactly as documented, and their substantive claims await the external measurements they are explicitly built to invite.
