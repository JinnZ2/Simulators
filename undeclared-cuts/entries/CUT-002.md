## The case

A septic drainfield and a hydroelectric plant. Both are unsaturated and
saturated flow through porous and open channels under a head gradient;
both are governed by the same continuity and momentum relations, with
different scale, boundary conditions and material.

```
    shared            separated by
    ----------------  ---------------------------------------
    continuity        two regulators
    head gradient     two licence classes
    Darcy / channel   two degree programmes
      flow            two journal sets
    conservation      two trade bodies
                      two inspection regimes
                      two funding lines
                      ... enumerate from the record
```

The order carries a count of 7+ institutional cuts and 0 physics cuts
for this pair. That count is the entry's starting position, not its
result: it has not been enumerated under a stated rule and is carried
here to be checked, raised, or lowered.

## Procedure

1. Write the shared equation set. Enumerate every term. This fixes the
   physics-cut candidate list before any corpus is opened.
2. Declare, in advance, whether scale, boundary condition and material
   differences count as physics cuts. Both answers are defensible; the
   run must pick one and say so, because the count moves with it.
3. Enumerate institutional cuts from documentary evidence only --
   a named regulator, a named licence, a named curriculum, a named
   journal. A cut with no document is not counted.
4. Report two counts side by side, with the enumeration rule attached.

## Why the counts are not combined

An institutional cut and a physics cut are different objects with
different remedies. A ratio implies one scale. The entry returns a pair.

## Not in scope

Whether discipline boundaries are good or bad, whether the two fields
should merge, and whether an embedding that encodes an institutional
boundary is thereby defective. The entry measures what the geometry
encodes; it takes no position on what it should encode.
