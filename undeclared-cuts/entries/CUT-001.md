measurand: For a pair of quantities coupled in the world and held apart in a text corpus, whether any corpus-derived geometry separates MEASURED-AS-SEPARATE (the two were co-measured and found to vary independently) from NEVER-MEASURED-TOGETHER (no joint measurement enters the record). Two conditions with different downstream consequences; the quantity is whether the record distinguishes them at all.
control: Physics-grounded coupling pairs whose coupling is established independently of the corpus -- conservation relations, shared state variables, a quantity appearing on both sides of one equation. Coupling known before the geometry is read.
scope_limits: One corpus, one embedding family, one distance function per run; result is a property of that triple and does not transfer to another without re-running. Requires pairs whose world-coupling is established outside the corpus, which restricts the control set to domains with closed-form relations. Says nothing about pairs that are genuinely uncoupled. Does not measure how a cut arose, only whether its two origins remain distinguishable downstream.
thin_links: Embedding distance as a stand-in for co-measurement is an inference over an inference -- the embedding is fitted to co-occurrence, co-occurrence is a proxy for joint reporting, joint reporting is a proxy for joint measurement; three hops, and the entry does not establish any of them. Treating a physics-grounded relation as evidence the two quantities were ever measured in one apparatus is a second thin link: an equation can relate quantities nobody has co-measured.
would_change: A separating statistic on the control set moves the entry from unrecoverable to recoverable and makes arm 2 (applying it to uncontrolled pairs) runnable. Any corpus carrying explicit negative-result records of co-measurement changes the measurand, since MEASURED-AS-SEPARATE would then be readable from text rather than inferred from geometry. A demonstration that the two conditions have identical co-occurrence statistics by construction would close the entry as unrecoverable-in-principle rather than unrecovered.

## What is being asked

Take a pair of quantities that are coupled in the world. In the text
record they appear in separate literatures, separate journals, separate
measurement traditions. Two histories produce that separation and they
are not the same:

```
    MEASURED-AS-SEPARATE          NEVER-MEASURED-TOGETHER
    somebody co-measured them     no apparatus ever held both
    found independence            no joint reading exists
    the separation is a RESULT    the separation is an ABSENCE
```

The first is a finding about the world. The second is a fact about
instrumentation and funding. A downstream reader of the corpus sees one
thing in both cases: two bodies of text that do not cite each other.

## Arm 1 procedure

1. Assemble a control set: pairs whose coupling is fixed by a relation
   established outside the corpus (shared state variable, conservation
   constraint, one quantity appearing in the definition of the other).
2. For each control pair, record the coupling strength from the
   independent relation, not from the corpus.
3. Compute the corpus geometry for each pair under a stated embedding
   family and distance function, declared before the run.
4. Ask whether any statistic over that geometry orders the control pairs
   by their independently-known coupling.

## The result condition

If no statistic separates them, that is the finding and it is reportable
as-is. It states: the record does not carry the distinction, so any
downstream instrument reading only the corpus cannot recover which
condition produced a given separation. Indistinguishable is not a failed
run; it is the measurement.

Arm 2 -- applying a separating statistic to pairs whose coupling is not
independently known -- is not runnable unless arm 1 returns a separating
statistic. It is not specified here.

## Not in scope

Whether any particular separation in any particular literature is one
condition or the other. This entry measures the discriminability of the
two conditions, not the assignment of a case to one of them.
