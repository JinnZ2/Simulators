## 1. EMPIRICAL — The fine ledger as a threshold-exceedance record

**Gap:** Star-route mail contracts (Post Office, RG28) did not pay
for missed trips regardless of weather, and fined unexcused failure
at up to three times the trip price — so weather is inadmissible as
a cause in that record and enters only as a FINE (the parent
mechanism, instanced). Read forward, the fine is a proposed
instrument: it fires only when conditions exceeded what a specific
operator — maximum incentive to complete the trip, full local
knowledge, calibration built from years on that ground — judged
passable. A rain gauge records magnitude on a fixed external scale;
the fine records a locally-calibrated threshold crossing. Nobody has
read the fine ledger as a weather series.

*Provenance: the fine-as-threshold reading is stated by Kavik and is
not in the literature. It is rendered here as a proposed instrument,
not a reported finding.*

**Knowledge state:** NOT_STUDIED

**Research question:** Do fines per route per month, 1877-1890,
carry a recoverable environmental signal — a seasonal cycle of
normal impassability, anomalies above it marking events severe
enough to stop someone who would not stop, and spatial clustering
across adjacent routes drawing an event's footprint — off the rail
and telegraph network, exactly where the Signal Service was not?

**Disciplines:** historical climatology, archival history, economic
history (for the enforcement covariate)

**Data sources:**
  EXISTING RECORD: NARA RG28 star-route contracts and
    correspondence (see step 0); Signal Service station records for
    the validation regions; the enforcement-regime record (contract
    churn, the 1870s star-route scandals and their aftermath)
  YOUR OWN DATA: the fine series itself — fines per route per
    month, transcribed; route geographies from contract endpoints
  SOMEONE'S HANDS: `ARCHIVE_SOURCES.md` carries the holdings and
    access classes

**Method:**
0. VERIFY THE WINDOW FIRST. The contract route registers have a
   coverage gap across exactly 1877-1890 (registers exist
   1828-1870 and 1917-1960); contracts and correspondence survive.
   What survives for the window must be established at NARA RG28
   before any design rests on it. This is step zero, not a caveat.
1. Transcribe fines per route per month for the surviving window.
2. Model the two thresholds SEPARATELY, as a design requirement:
   an excuse granted is the AGENCY's threshold for
   beyond-human-capacity; a fine landed is the OPERATOR's threshold
   overruled. Both are data. Do not collapse them.
3. Model enforcement regime as a COVARIATE, not a constant:
   fine counts move with contract-holder churn and with the 1870s
   star-route scandals and their aftermath.
4. Fit the seasonal cycle per route; read anomalies above it.
5. Validate: test fine anomalies against Signal Service station
   records on nearby routes, same months. Where they agree, the
   series is calibrated.
6. A calibrated series is then usable on the routes with no
   station — which is most of them, and the point: star routes are
   by definition not on rail lines, so the series maps the space
   the instrumental network does not cover.

**Expected deliverable:** a calibrated fines-per-route-per-month
series with its two-threshold and enforcement structure explicit,
and event footprints drawn from adjacent-route clustering off the
instrumental network.

**Falsifier:** fine anomalies show no relationship to independently
recorded conditions on routes where both exist, once enforcement
regime is controlled.

**What it opens:** a validated instance of the parent mechanism run
in reverse — the transform inverted to recover the variable — which
is the template for every other liability-encoded archive in
`ARCHIVE_SOURCES.md`; and a spatial record for exactly the ground
the homestead gap below reads by a second route.

## 2. EMPIRICAL — Homestead proving-up files as an unread ground record (G-07)

**Gap:** Homestead Land Entry Case Files (GLO, RG49) hold, per the
National Park Service's own description, improvements to the
property: houses constructed, wells dug, crops planted, trees
cleared, fences built — claimant testimony plus two witness
affidavits, sworn, dated, at legal land description precision,
produced in volume across the settlement front, for legal reasons
rather than scientific ones. Well depth and crop choice under oath,
across a front that crossed 1877-1890, is a distributed groundwater
and growing-condition record that nobody has read as one series.
The ground enters this record as PROOF OF LABOR — the parent
mechanism again. Every located use of these files is genealogical
or family-history: not tried and failed, not pointed at.

**Knowledge state:** NOT_STUDIED (zero located climate or
environmental use)

**Research question:** Do well-depth and crop-choice testimony,
read as dated geolocated series across the settlement front, carry
coherent spatial and temporal structure — a groundwater and
growing-condition record for ground the instrumental network never
covered?

**Disciplines:** historical climatology, hydrology, archival
history

**Data sources:**
  EXISTING RECORD: NARA RG49; BLM GLO Records — patents searchable
    by name, location, legal description, with partial state
    coverage online; NPS Homestead Records Project; some
    county-level holdings
  YOUR OWN DATA: the extracted series — well depths, crop choices,
    improvement dates per legal description
  SOMEONE'S HANDS: the DECISION entry below routes the
    which-state-first choice

**Method:**
1. Pick the starting digitized set (the DECISION below).
2. Extract per file: legal description, dates, well depth, crops,
   improvements, from claimant testimony and both affidavits.
3. Map against Signal Service station coverage. The placement
   asymmetry is the design point: stations were placed by existing
   Army posts, existing telegraph, and a coastal/Great Lakes
   storm-warning charter — they follow the rail and wire network.
   Homestead claims went where land was OPEN, ahead of that
   network by definition. THE NON-OVERLAP IS THE INFORMATIVE PART:
   where claims exist and no station does, the affidavits are the
   only record of that ground.
4. Calibrate in the overlap region; read the non-overlap.

**Expected deliverable:** the first environmental series extracted
from proving-up files, with its calibration region and its
only-record region reported separately.

**Falsifier:** well-depth and crop testimony show no coherent
spatial or temporal structure beyond what is explained by
claim-filing chronology and boilerplate.

**What it opens:** a second, independent instrument for the same
off-network space the fine ledger reads — two liability-encoded
archives cross-checkable against each other where no instrumental
record exists at all.

## 3. DECISION — Which state's digitized set to start from (G-07's fork)

**Fork:** G-07 needs a starting corpus, the BLM GLO online coverage
is partial (~9 states per one located guide: AZ IL IN IA NE NV OH
UT WY), and the validation design needs a calibration region —
so the first state is a choice the record does not settle.

**Options:**
  A. Start calibration-first — a state with dense Signal Service
     station coverage AND dense claims (the Iowa/Nebraska/Illinois
     end of the online set). What follows: the calibration region
     comes first and the method is validated early, at the cost of
     starting where the instrumental record is least needed.
  B. Start signal-first — a state where the non-overlap dominates
     (the Nevada/Arizona/Utah end). What follows: the series is
     read first exactly where it is the only record, at the cost
     of having no calibration region until a second state is done.

**Winning condition:** a per-state overlap computation showing
either one state carrying both a usable calibration region and
substantial non-overlap — which closes the fork on that state — or
the two axes trading off so sharply that the sequence
calibrate-then-read across two states is forced, which closes the
fork as an ordering rather than a choice.

**Discriminator:** overlap with Signal Service station coverage,
computed per digitized state — station locations and histories
against claim densities by legal description. Desk work on two
public record sets; no archive visit needed for the discriminator
itself.

**Blocked by:** access — this environment's egress refuses the
record hosts (the standing allowlist state), and the online-state
list itself is carried from one guide and unverified.

**Who could run it:** anyone with the BLM GLO search and the
station histories (MRCC holds station-by-station histories, per
the marker file) — a desk, not an archive.

**If you run it:** G-07's method step 1 resolves, and the same
overlap map prices the fine-ledger gap's validation regions for
free, since both validations lean on the same station network.

## 4. METHODOLOGICAL — The absence set as a readable document (G-08)

**Gap:** Observation programs are studied for what their data show.
The COMPLEMENT — the quantities a program could have measured and
did not — is not treated as data, but it is a dated,
institutionally-sourced statement of what that program held
constant. What is measured is what is believed to VARY; what goes
unmeasured is what is believed to HOLD. Signal Service 1870-1891,
observed set per the 1887 manual (147 pp): barometer, thermometer,
wind velocity and direction, rain gauge, dew point,
character/kind/movement of upper and lower clouds, condition of the
weather — and two-level cloud motion is an upper-air wind proxy, so
the set is built for storm ARRIVAL PREDICTION, not climate. Absent:
soil moisture, soil temperature, groundwater, streamflow, snowpack,
evaporation, growing degree accumulation, phenology, frost dates as
recorded events. The ground was not overlooked; it was CLASSIFIED
AS CONSTANT. And the absence side is the honest side: what a
program measured was argued over, budgeted and defended, while what
it did not measure was mostly never argued about at all — the
absence set is UNGUARDED, and unguarded records are usually the
honest ones.

**Knowledge state:** NOT_STUDIED — and HELD at one program: the lag
claim requires N>1 observation programs across DIFFERENT charters.
One program's absence set is an anecdote. The gap renders; no lag
figure renders until the charter set is assembled.

**Research question:** For a set of historical observation
programs, does the absence set predict the program's later
expansion — do assumed-constant quantities acquire instruments only
after a documented failure, and with what lag?

**Disciplines:** history of science, instrumentation, climatology

**Data sources:**
  EXISTING RECORD: observer manuals with explicit variable lists
    (the 1887 Signal Service manual, downloadable);
    successor-agency manuals; the 1891 War-to-USDA transfer as a
    natural experiment — same sky, changed institution, changed
    variable list
  YOUR OWN DATA: absence-set timelines per program
  SOMEONE'S HANDS: the charter set the hold condition waits on —
    `ARCHIVE_SOURCES.md` carries the candidates

**Method:**
1. Extract the variable list from each manual, by date.
2. Build the absence set: measurable-then, not measured.
3. SEPARATE held-constant from liability-displaced, per the parent
   mechanism, before proceeding — the two have opposite
   implications (nobody looked and the data does not exist, versus
   the data exists under another name in another archive and is
   recoverable), and an absence claim made before this split is
   made about the wrong population. This step is the M-A
   correction and is mandatory, not an afterthought.
4. Date each absence's later entry into the program.
5. Find the triggering failure where one exists — soil moisture
   instrumented after the Dust Bowl is the reference case: an
   unmeasured quantity reads as a stable quantity and stays stable
   in the record until it fails loudly enough to get an
   instrument.
6. Measure the lag distribution — subject to the hold: not until
   N>1 programs across different charters are in the set.

**Expected deliverable:** absence-set timelines for several
programs, and — once the hold releases — a lag figure from
assumption-failure to instrumentation.

**Falsifier:** variable lists expand at a rate unrelated to
documented failures — by technology availability or budget alone,
with no assumption-failure signature.

**What it opens:** if the lag is regular, TODAY'S absence sets are
readable the same way: current monitoring gaps become a list of
currently-assumed constants, rankable by consequence.
Cross-reference, per the order: the same shape in a different
domain as the seam-gaps detection-floor gap — an unmeasured
quantity reads as a stable quantity; a sub-floor null reads as an
absent effect. The order and the marker cite it as G-01; the
[`../seam-gaps/`](../seam-gaps/) render tags that gap G-02, and its
ids yield to the author's list.

**Parent instrument (arrived after this render):**
[`CHARTER_SIGNATURE.md`](CHARTER_SIGNATURE.md) — this gap's method
generalized from observer manuals to any chartered scope, adding the
