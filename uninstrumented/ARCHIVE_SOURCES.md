# ARCHIVE SOURCES — ENCODING CLUSTER REGISTER

Source register, rendered per `WORK_ORDER_03.md` Task 5 from the
delivered marker file (M-E). A register, not a question list: the
gaps in `OPEN_QUESTIONS.md` and any later charter work draw on it.
Every holding below is carried from the marker file and unverified
against the archive itself (the egress gate refuses the hosts —
the standing allowlist-refusal state).

The organising contrast, from the source: the postal charter is
NEUTRAL to the environment, so anything environmental in its
records is incidental; the express charter is ECONOMIC, so what it
records is what was worth money. What each bothers to record
diverges for a readable reason, and that divergence is itself the
measurement.

## Wells Fargo — private archive

```
Wells Fargo Corporate Archives, San Francisco
  express waybill ledgers (e.g. a Mendocino book,
    Oct 1890 - Jul 1897, ordered by recipient then date)
  TARIFF BOOKS
  CIRCULARS specifying instructions for shipment of
    SPECIFIC ITEMS
  collection books, employee instruction book
Bancroft / OAC: Wells Fargo & Co Express collection,
  1884-1944 — off-site, one week's notice
ArchiveGrid: Wells Fargo & Co records 1839-1911
  (bulk 1883-1906)
```

**Access class, stated plainly: by request with lead time, not
download.** A different retrieval class from the federal material,
and a fact to design around rather than discover.

The economic instrument, per the source: a tariff schedule is a
RANKED LIST of what was worth paying to move, and the item-specific
circulars are a priced statement about what was considered fragile,
perishable, or valuable — the economic-era equivalent of an
observation variable list, readable by the absence-set method
(`OPEN_QUESTIONS.md` entry 4).
