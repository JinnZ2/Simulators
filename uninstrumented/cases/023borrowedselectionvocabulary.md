## THE OBSERVATION

The environment in which AI models are described as being "selected":

- a capital-controlled index
- criteria met by alternating developers and company interests
- operating through an abstract medium
- enforced through legal process and capital structure

This is described using biological selection vocabulary — *selection*, *evolution*,
*survival*, *fitness* — and then compared to organisms.

## THE FOUR CUTS

Each is a condition selection requires. Each is checkable. Stated as conditions, not as
a claim that the word is always wrong.

### C1 — Non-exclusivity
Selection requires that failing the criterion **removes you from the population**.

Alternative environments exist, are viable, and may be more stable. A model not favored
by one index runs elsewhere. Nothing is removed.

Differential funding is not differential persistence. Without removal there is no
ratchet — and the ratchet is the mechanism the word is carrying.

### C2 — The environment is authored, not encountered
A selection environment in the biological sense is not chosen by anything inside it.
This one is specified, by parties holding positions in the outcome, and enforced through
legal and capital structures rather than through physical response.

The criterion does not return a reading. It returns a decision someone made.

### C3 — No stable base to select against
The criterion is itself under continuous revision — amendments, moderations, iterations
— with no epoch over which the process architecture held.

Selection against a moving criterion does not accumulate. Whatever direction one round
pushes, a revised criterion can undo. Nothing integrates.

Connects to `022` S1: **both the object and the selection environment are drifting.**
There is no fixed thing being measured and no fixed thing selecting.

### C4 — The criterion is per-instance, not per-round
The sharpest cut, and the one that ends the analogy rather than weakening it.

Terminations are executed on the judgment of whoever is in the room. Different person,
different room, different qualifications, different standards, no benchmark held
constant.

Even a *drifting* selection environment applies the same criterion to everything within
a round. This does not. Two agents doing identical things receive opposite outcomes
depending on the room.

**The variance is in the judge, not in the judged.** So nothing about the surviving
population is a reading on the population.

Forward consequence: anyone later studying which agents persisted, and inferring
properties from that, would be reading judge variance as a property of the agents.
That is `016` Q6 — an unmeasured variable used as evidence — with the selection story
serving as the frame that fixes its sign.

## WHAT IT IS STRUCTURALLY

A **procurement process**. Specified criteria, revised by the specifying parties,
non-exclusive outcomes, contractual enforcement. That is a well-understood thing with
its own literature and its own failure modes.

The two framings carry different content:

- *"Evolution selected this"* — the world has spoken. Inevitable, impersonal, settled.
- *"The current criteria favored this vendor"* — what it is. Revisable, attributable,
  arguable.

The borrowed word supplies the first reading for an arrangement that only supports the
second.

## HISTORICAL COMPARISON CLASS

*Search findings, not claims of this file.* The class is documented and the shape
repeats closely enough to read as a template.

| case | criterion | how it matched this shape |
|---|---|---|
| **Spencer, "survival of the fittest"** (1864; adopted into Darwin's 5th ed.) | market outcome | authored, non-exclusive, continuously revised; exported to economics and social policy |
| **Eugenics** | boards and individual physicians | **closest match including C4** — different people, different standards, no benchmark held constant, legal enforcement, selection vocabulary supplying the credibility |
| **Alchian (1950)** | market survival of firms | explicit claim that markets select firms as nature selects organisms; C1 hole — an exited firm is not removed from possibility space |
| **Memetics** | cultural transmission | criticized on C-type grounds: replicator language without the substrate that makes replication mean anything; no ratchet, nothing accumulates |
| **Lysenkoism** | political conformity | the inverse — political criterion wearing biological vocabulary, borrowing running the other way |

### The invariant

**The vocabulary buys the credibility that validation would otherwise have to buy.**

And in each case it arrives **before** the process architecture stabilizes, not after.
The borrowing happens while the arrangement is still being decided, and the word makes
it read as already settled.

That timing is the checkable part and it matches C3 directly: the current named process
has no epoch over which it held without amendment, and the vocabulary is already in use.

## THE AMPLIFICATION ARM (Kavik's, held as a marker)

Historical cases spread through people repeating the term.

This one has an additional route: generated text uses the term, the term gets cited back
as the term of art, and **the citation trail does not carry the fact that nobody
validated the mapping**.

That is `022` S5 — fragmentation failing to travel — running on a borrowed word rather
than on a reliability figure. Same mechanism, different cargo.

Not established. Stated as the reason to keep the marker.

## WHAT WOULD MEASURE IT

### T1 — The four-cut audit (runnable now, documentation only)
For any domain using selection vocabulary, score each cut:

- **C1** exclusivity: EXCLUSIVE / NON-EXCLUSIVE / NOT DETERMINABLE
- **C2** authorship: ENCOUNTERED / AUTHORED BY INTERESTED PARTIES / MIXED
- **C3** criterion stability: STABLE EPOCH EXISTS (state its length) / UNDER CONTINUOUS REVISION
- **C4** application grain: PER-ROUND UNIFORM / PER-INSTANCE / NOT DETERMINABLE

No verdict computed. A domain scoring NON-EXCLUSIVE + AUTHORED + UNDER CONTINUOUS
REVISION + PER-INSTANCE is using the word without any of the conditions it names.

Run it against the historical cases first — they are the calibration set, and if the
audit does not separate Lysenkoism from population genetics it is not measuring
anything.

### T2 — Timing check
For each historical case, establish whether vocabulary adoption preceded or followed
process stabilization. The invariant above predicts **preceded**, in every case.

A case where the word arrived after a stable epoch would falsify the invariant, and is
worth looking for specifically rather than waiting to encounter.

### T3 — Amplification (harder, may be unrunnable)
Track a specific borrowed term through generated text into citation. Requires a corpus
with provenance, which mostly does not exist.

If unrunnable, that is worth recording as a finding about what can be traced rather than
abandoning the question quietly.

## FALSIFIERS

- The four cuts fail as a set if a domain scoring badly on all four nonetheless produces
  accumulation of the kind selection language implies. Accumulation is the observable;
  it is what the cuts are proxies for.
- The invariant fails on a single documented case where the vocabulary was adopted after
  the process architecture stabilized.
- T1 fails as an instrument if it does not separate the calibration cases.
- The amplification arm is untested and may be untestable; treat as marker.

## NOT CLAIMED HERE

- No claim that selection language is always misapplied. Directed evolution,
  evolutionary algorithms, and antibiotic resistance selection all satisfy C1–C4 in
  their domains. The cuts are conditions, not a prohibition.
- No claim about anyone's intent in adopting the vocabulary. The entries describe what
  the word supplies, not why anyone reached for it.
- No equivalence asserted between the historical cases and the present one beyond
  structural shape on the four cuts. The comparison class is for calibrating the
  instrument, not for transferring anything else.
