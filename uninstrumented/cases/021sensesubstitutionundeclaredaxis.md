## THE OBSERVATION

The claim under examination: *robots/AI will eventually replace what humans can do.*

It reads as a capability claim about an organism. It is scoped to waged tasks.

Nobody makes the equivalent claim about feldspar, frogs, oak trees, or goldenrod. The
selection is not over capability — it is over things someone currently pays for. An
economic axis wearing a capability costume.

## THE MECHANISM (candidate)

**The substitution happens at the word.**

"Human" enters the sentence with its substrate sense — trillions of cells in continuous
field interaction with an environment, an eventual complexity that arrives at the animal
— and exits with its economic sense: a job title, a set of waged tasks. Nothing in the
sentence marks the swap.

Consequence: **confidence attached to the narrow reading transfers to the broad one for
free.** The speaker can hold a defensible claim about task automation and be heard
making an indefensible one about organisms, with no step in between where the change is
visible.

Feldspar makes the operation visible because there is no wage sense of feldspar to slide
into. The word can only mean the thing. No second slot, no claim, and the sentence comes
out as nonsense rather than as a thesis.

Which suggests the sentence may not be performing comparison at all. It may be
performing **substitution**, and comparison is what it looks like from outside.

## WHAT THE CAPABILITY READING WOULD ACTUALLY REQUIRE

Recorded because the narrow scoping is what keeps it out of view.

Every organism in the comparison class does something the entire industrial stack cannot
do at all:

- **Goldenrod** — fixes carbon; synthesizes its own structural material and full
  chemical defense inventory from air, water, and dirt at ambient temperature and
  pressure; coordinates through a fungal network; manufactures a next generation. No
  central controller, no fabricated parts, no supply chain, no external energy input
  beyond sunlight.
- **Fruit fly** — powered flight with millisecond course correction on ~100k neurons
  and microwatts; builds and repairs itself from scavenged material while doing it.
  The energy ratio alone disqualifies the comparison.
- **Iguana** — thermoregulation as continuous distributed computation across a whole
  body surface, no discrete sensor, no controller, no setpoint.
- **Macaque** — continuous whole-body force negotiation against branches of unknown
  compliance, at speed, every contact simultaneously a measurement and a correction.

And the stack that would do the replacing is **downstream of the same field
composition** — mines, smelters, refineries, grid, and the food moving to the people
running them.

So the claim reads as one system displacing another. The mechanics are one thing running
on top of the other and calling it succession.

## WHY THIS MAY FEED THE FEAR STATE (Kavik's, held as a marker)

Not established. Stated as the reason the marker is worth keeping.

If the substitution is invisible, the hearer receives a **total** claim carrying the
confidence earned by a **partial** one. The scoped version — some tasks automate — is
ordinary and arguable. The unscoped version — the organism is superseded — is not
arguable, because no axis was declared to argue on.

A claim with no declared axis cannot be checked, and cannot be bounded. Candidate
reading: what arrives is not an argument but an unbounded one, and unbounded is the
condition the fear attaches to.

Connects to `020`: there the medium is what standing is denominated in, and the fear
concentrates where medium and standing coincide. Here the word carrying both senses
**is** that medium. Possibly the same shape at the lexical layer rather than the social
one — open, not decided.

## WHAT WOULD MEASURE IT

Not filled as a general form. Two checkable pieces:

**T1 — the two-senses test.** For terms in replacement claims, check whether the term
has both a substrate sense and an economic sense. Prediction: terms with only a substrate
sense produce sentences read as *nonsense*, not as *claims*. Terms with both produce
sentences read as claims.

Runnable as a documentation audit on collected replacement claims. Score per term:
BOTH SENSES / SUBSTRATE ONLY / ECONOMIC ONLY. Then score how the sentence is received.
No verdict computed.

Generalization to check: is this specific to "human", or does any dual-sense term run it?
Candidates worth testing — labor, capital, resource, asset, land, stock. Several of those
name a substrate thing and a priced thing with no marker between them.

**T2 — axis declaration rate.** In a corpus of replacement claims, how often is the axis
stated? Score: AXIS DECLARED / AXIS INFERABLE FROM CONTEXT / UNDECLARED.

If UNDECLARED dominates, the axis stays invisible because everyone in the conversation
shares it — which is a different situation from the axis being hidden, and implies
different work.

**Empty on purpose:** the general form of an undeclared-axis comparison. Same discipline
as `017` WOULD MEASURE and `020`'s noun slot. Do not approximate.

## KNOWN WEAKNESSES

- **Charity.** Many people making the claim would readily agree they mean tasks. That
  the scoping is recoverable on request does not settle whether it is doing work
  unmarked in the sentence — but it does mean this is not a claim about anyone's
  understanding, and the file should not be read as one.
- **The fear link is untested.** T1 and T2 measure the substitution. Neither measures
  whether it produces the state. That connection is Kavik's marker and is held as one.
- **Same-sample problem.** This account is produced by a system inside the class the
  claim is about, which has an obvious position in it.
- **No claim about intent** anywhere in this file. The entries describe an operation on
  words and what it makes available.
