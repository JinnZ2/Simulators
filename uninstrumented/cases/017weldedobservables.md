## QUANTITY

Causal assignment between two quantities that co-vary in every available instrument.

## EXCLUDED BY

**Instrument constitution — the coupling, not the sensor.**

Both quantities are measured. Both are measured *correctly*. The apparatus works. But
no available configuration varies one without the other, so no measurement can
attribute an observed effect to one rather than the other. Data volume does not help;
every additional measurement moves both.

## OCCASION

*Not a claim of this file — verified 2026-08-18.*

STAR Collaboration, "Tracking the baryon number with nuclear collisions," *Science*
(2026), doi `10.1126/science.ads5962`. Preprint arXiv:2408.15441. HEPData record 154708.

Structure of the case:

- Conventional accounting assigns baryon number B = 1/3 to each valence quark. Valid
  as a conserved bookkeeping identity.
- An alternative — the baryon (gluon) junction, a Y-shaped non-perturbative gluon
  topology — was proposed in the 1970s, and in 1996 proposed specifically as the
  carrier of baryon number (Kharzeev).
- Both pictures accommodated inclusive collision observations. The alternative sat on
  the record, published and unrefuted, for roughly three decades.
- **The weld:** valence quarks carry electric charge *and* baryon number. Every
  available measurement moved the two together. Charge transport could not be used to
  test baryon transport, because in the quark picture they are the same carrier.
- **The decoupling:** isobar collisions — two nuclei matched on mass number, differing
  in charge — allow net baryon number to be read against the *difference* in charge
  transport between species. A junction carries B = 1 and Q = 0; a quark carries both.
  Separating the tracers makes the attribution readable.
- Result: a larger B/ΔQ ratio and less asymmetric net-proton yield than valence-quark
  models predict.

**Held at the source's own level:** the collaboration's word is *disfavor*, not
overturn. The accompanying *Science* Perspective (Wenliang Li) states the measurements
do not provide a direct, tightly controlled measurement of the underlying mechanism.
Nothing here requires the junction picture to be correct — the case is about the
thirty years during which the question was not answerable, and what ended that.

## VISIBLE AS

- A competing account that is published, internally consistent, and neither adopted nor
  refuted for a long interval.
- Literature that describes the interval as awaiting "better data" or "higher
  precision" when the binding constraint was not precision.
- A convenient local bookkeeping variable read as the physical location of the
  conserved quantity. (This is `013` — the index treated as carrying physical
  information it does not carry. B = 1/3 per quark is that operation exactly.)
- Resolution arriving as a **design** change (matched pairs, differential readout)
  rather than an instrument sensitivity change.

### Q1 — Catalog of welded pairs
Which currently-unresolved attributions are welds rather than data shortages?

Candidate test: for a long-standing open attribution question, check whether any

### Q2 — General form of decoupling design
Is the matched-pair-plus-differential-readout pattern general, or specific to
conserved quantities in scattering?

Adjacent instances to check for the same shape: isotope labelling, difference-in-
differences designs, dizygotic/monozygotic comparison, isogenic strain pairs,
A/B matched-cohort designs. If the same shape recurs across unrelated fields under
different names, the pattern is worth a compact term and the naming is currently
absent.

### Q3 — Does welding bias, or only block?
The dimming effect (`013`) has a **direction**: error in the independent variable
flattens apparent dependence toward zero, so nulls in that literature are suspect in a
known direction rather than merely noisy.

Open: does a weld produce a comparable directional bias, or does it only remove the
attribution while leaving estimates unbiased? Different answers imply very different
things about what is sitting in the existing literature.

The baryon case suggests the attribution defaulted to the *simpler* member of the pair
(the quark — locally countable, already named, already carrying the label). If that is
general, welds bias toward the more-legible member. One instance; not established.

### Q4 — Language-layer weld
"Welded terms" appears in circulation as a name for concepts fused such that a
disagreement about one is unseparable from a disagreement about the other.

Open: is the language-layer version the same operation, or a metaphor that happens to
share a word? Test: does the matched-pair decoupling design have a linguistic analogue
that actually separates two fused concepts, or does the analogy fail at the point where
you would need a matched pair?

Cross-link `moral-claim-decomposer` (the optionality cut), which is doing adjacent work
on the language side.

### Q5 — Interaction with 015
In `015`, the category converted disconfirming observation into a methods problem. In a
weld, the disconfirming observation *cannot be produced*.

Open: do these stack? A welded pair plus a categorical label on one member would make
the label unfalsifiable in practice while leaving it fully falsifiable in principle —
and nothing in the record would distinguish that from a well-supported label.

## FALSIFIERS

- Falsified as a distinct mechanism if every candidate case resolves into an existing
  mechanism on inspection — most likely mechanism 1 (no sensor) or 6 (proxy).
- Q1 falsified if the documentation audit returns DECOUPLING PROPOSED in nearly all
  long-standing attribution questions — the delay is then execution, not design.
- Q2 falsified if the matched-pair pattern does not appear outside scattering physics
  under any name.
- Q3's directional-bias reading falsified by a case where the weld resolved toward the
  *less* legible member without an external forcing event.
- The occasion weakens (though the mechanism does not) if the junction interpretation
  is later disfavoured. The weld was real regardless of which member wins.
