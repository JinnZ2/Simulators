# ATTRIBUTION AND TENURE

A case file of open threads, not a finding. **Nothing in it is resolved.**

It belongs in `uninstrumented/` rather than in `coupling_audit/` because the
binding limit identified is **instrument constitution**, not routing.

**Provenance.** All six threads are markers under exploration. The shapes, the
cuts, and the possession discontinuity are the author's; field names,
structural restatements, and this write-up are model-generated. Cited
literature is source material, not authored claim.

## Thread 1 — attribution as one-way vs coupled read

A reader holds a mapping from an interior state to a required signal, fixed in
advance from the reader's own grammar. The other's actual signal is scored
pass/fail against that template; **no read of the other's grammar occurs in
either direction.** Absence of the required signal reads as absence of the
state.

    reader holds:  state X is indicated by signal S
    the mapping X -> S is fixed in advance, from the reader's grammar
    the other's actual signal is scored against S, pass/fail
    => absence of S reads as absence of X

**Worked case.** A dog jumping and barking, read as affection, where the
behaviour is a status and space assertion in dog grammar and the human's
push-off never enters the loop. The signal was a carrier; the content came
from the reader's current state.

**Contrast case.** A reading that requires the animal to receive something —
tail wag plus response to direction — where the animal's reply is a term and
the read can be corrected.

**The cut is not accuracy. It is whether the loop is closed.** An uncoupled
read cannot be wrong and therefore does not update.

## Thread 2 — cultural template specifying the required signal

The template in Thread 1 is supplied culturally, not derived. Narrative
material — rescue plots, specified displays of devotion — sets which signal
counts.

**Open:** whether the template generates the identification, or the
identification selects the template. Those predict differently and **no
separating measurement is proposed here.**

## Thread 3 — retreat tenure as a term in bond persistence

**Author's structural claim.** Earlier household configurations assigned
separate domains with separate holders, so a conflict retreat existed and was
held by default rather than granted. Where provisioning purchases the interior
domain as well, the retreat term is deleted while the surrounding vocabulary
is unchanged.

**Mammal literature supports the resource half.**

- **Kit fox** (*Journal of Mammalogy*): a fox requires both a territory
  providing dens and food *and* a pair-mate; pair persistence is linked to
  scarcity of breeding territories and to the importance **to both sexes** of
  retaining one once acquired.
- **Dunbar** (*Frontiers in Ecology and Evolution*, 2022): female dispersion
  is **necessary but not sufficient** for pairbonded monogamy, and the
  overdispersion claim had not previously been formally tested.
- Active mate switching documented in alpine marmots, Ethiopian wolves,
  African wild dogs, fat-tailed dwarf lemurs, woodland voles. General finding
  across taxa: divorce follows breeding failure or a better option appearing.

**Not supported, and marked as unmeasured.** The literature measures **joint
tenure of a shared territory**, not a within-territory retreat separately held
by the female. The causal arrow in the vole and marmot work runs
failure → dissolution, not loss-of-retreat → dissolution. The field states its
own limit: mate fidelity and divorce are far less studied in mammals than in
birds.

So the prediction is **not contradicted. It is unmeasured, in a subfield that
knows it is thin.**

## Thread 4 — den siting under male occupancy, with a discriminator

- **Grizzly bear den-site selection** (*PLOS One* 2011, "Despotism and Risk of
  Infanticide Influence Grizzly Bear Den-Site Selection"): adult females
  select den sites avoiding areas occupied by adult males, driven by despotism
  and infanticide risk. Den siting as a function of male occupancy, measured,
  in a large mammal.
- Rodent mothers increase vigilance under infanticide risk, with
  counterstrategies including drawing the male **away from the nest**
  (*Scientific Reports* 2019).
- Females discriminate **sire from unknown male odour** at the nest and
  allocate between current and future reproduction accordingly (PMC 2018) — so
  male **identity** is resolved, not merely presence.
- **Van Schaik and Kappeler 1997**: male infanticide almost invariably follows
  the disappearance of the protective male, so a male can be the threat or the
  protection depending on tenure.
- Meerkats: infanticide plus **expulsion** of females, with expelled females
  repeatedly attempting to return. Hamadryas: takeover, coercive herding,
  female dispersal from the natal unit.

**Author's variant, not tested.** A site entered by a male stays devalued
after the intrusion, independent of his current presence — a memory of
intrusion attached to the **place**.

**Discriminator.** The infanticide model predicts avoidance tracking current
occupancy and resetting on his absence; the author's model predicts persistent
site-level devaluation. The scent-discrimination apparatus plus repeated site
choice would separate them. The sire-vs-unknown work shows the female holds
male identity, so place-plus-identity memory is not a stretch — but
**site-level persistent devaluation after intrusion was not found tested.**

## Thread 5 — risk concentration, established; mechanism, open

Intimate partners and family members constitute the largest identified
category for both female homicide and child homicide victims, with risk
concentrating **around separation**.

**Open:** whether this is the same mechanism as the evolved male reproductive
strategy identified in the animal literature — paternity uncertainty,
reproductive access — or a **different mechanism landing on similar
structure.** Comparative work has argued both.

**The load-bearing point does not require resolving it:** retreat availability
is protective in both accounts, and its removal raises risk. That is supported
on both sides.

## Thread 6 — possession as a candidate discontinuity, not a difference of degree

In the animal case the male controls **access** — to territory, to den site,
to mating — and the female is a party competed over. Under possession she is a
**holding**.

This reclassifies exit: in the access frame separation is an **outcome**;
under possession it is a **loss of property**, and the response is to a
property loss. **This predicts the separation spike better than a
reproductive-access model, which has no term for retaliation once access is
already gone.**

**Author's extension.** Possession is affordable only at distance from
environmental feedback. Where subsistence is tight, a suppressed adult's
capability is an immediate loss to the household and the cost registers; where
the household is buffered from its own production, the cost stops registering
and the configuration persists uncorrected.

**Prediction:** possession intensity tracks buffering from subsistence
feedback — not region, not doctrine.

**Counter-notes carried deliberately.** Buffering is heavily confounded. Tight
subsistence constraint does **not** imply egalitarian outcomes — the claim
concerns whether the cost **registers**, not whether outcomes are good.

## Testable now, on public data

**D-PLACE** (d-place.org, open access) aggregates the **Ethnographic Atlas**
(1291 societies) and the **Standard Cross-Cultural Sample** (186 societies,
2000+ variables, contributed by ~100 studies). Descent, marital residence,
inheritance of **real** property and inheritance of **movable** property are
**separately coded**, so household authority, residence, descent and property
can be treated as independent axes rather than collapsed into a single label.

**Precedent for the dimensional handling:** "A worldwide view of matriliny"
(PMC 2019) tested 126 binary traits against descent **and** residence
separately.

### Proposed run

Hold subsistence mode fixed, count distinct configurations across the axes,
and test whether configuration **variance narrows as subsistence intensifies**.

The reframe: not *which cultures have possession*, but *how many distinct
configurations existed at a given level of subsistence coupling*. A **diversity
measurement**, not a category comparison.

### Limits to carry with any result

- **Galton's problem.** Cases are not independent — shared configuration may
  be inheritance or diffusion rather than function. SCCS was built to reduce
  this and does not eliminate it. This **bears directly on a variance claim.**
- **"Tabulated nonsense? Testing the validity of the Ethnographic Atlas"** —
  a validation paper matching Atlas codes against 791,086 DHS respondents from
  317 ancestral societies. Read it before trusting any single variable.
- **The pinpointing convention.** Each society is dated to the earliest period
  with adequate ethnography, making the dataset a **snapshot rather than a
  time series.**

So: the diversity measurement is available. **The collapse-over-time claim
requires a second dated source and is not testable on D-PLACE alone.**
