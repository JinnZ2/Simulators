## WHAT THE PAPER ESTABLISHES

```
THREE ORTHOGONAL INDEPENDENCE AXES for audit assurance:
  1 PRINCIPAL   independence of who commissions / who is served
  2 SUBSTRATE   independence of what the audit runs on
  3 EVIDENCE    independence of where the evidence comes from

AGGREGATION:  weakest-link
COMMON CAUSE: beta-factor model
RESULT:       Monte Carlo showing conventional internal audit surfaces
              ~5.9% of faults, and NONE AT ALL in half the fault classes
```

The three axes all ask: are the PARTIES separated?

---

## THE PROPOSED FOURTH AXIS

```
4 CRITERION   independence of the standard the audit is run against
              from BOTH parties
```

**The question it asks.** Not "who is auditing whom" but "who holds the
thing being measured against."

```
Axes 1-3 can all be fully satisfied while the criterion is held by a party.
Two fully independent principals, on independent substrates, with
independent evidence, checking against a criterion one of them authored,
are not independent on the quantity that decides the outcome.
```

**Grading (PROPOSED, not validated):**
```
C0  criterion authored by the audited party
C1  criterion authored by the auditing party
C2  criterion authored by a third party, but discretionary in application
C3  criterion is a re-runnable procedure: conservation law, closure check,
    physical measurement, or any test whose result does not depend on
    who runs it
```
C3 is the only level at which the criterion is not a claim by someone.

---

## WHY THIS IS NOT A FOURTH FLAVOUR OF THE SAME THING

```
Axes 1-3 reduce CORRELATED ERROR between parties.
Axis 4 asks whether the measurand itself is party-held.

Test that separates them:
  a system can score maximum on 1-3 and C0 on axis 4
  -> the beta-factor common-cause model will not detect this,
     because the common cause is not a shared condition between
     the parties; it is the ruler.
```

If that holds, weakest-link aggregation across four axes returns a
materially different assurance number than across three, and the 5.9%
figure is an OVERSTATEMENT for any audit where the criterion is party-held.
That is the checkable consequence.

## THREE INDEPENDENT ARRIVALS AT THE SAME OBJECT

This axis is not new work. It is already implemented in three places that
were not built together, which is the argument that it is a real axis and
not a preference.

```
1 PHYSICS AS TRUST FLOOR
  Physics is not a source you accept, it is a procedure you re-run.
  A floor not because it is trustworthy but BECAUSE IT IS NOT A CLAIM.
  -> exactly C3. Arrived at from the distrust side.

2 THE ADJUDICATION FINDING
  Generators produce the surface of a mis-posed problem and cannot label
  their own output. Consequence: THE KEY IS THE CLAIM UNDER TEST, not
  ground truth. Keys were found self-contradicting with no statement
  present (condition B of the A/B/C/D audit-isolation design).
  -> the failure mode axis 4 predicts, observed in a run.
  -> "key-holder contamination" is C0/C1 stated in different words.

3 C4 IN THE AUDIT-PROTOCOL WORK ORDER
  A mechanical check testing whether any existing check has a MEASURAND
  EXTERNAL TO THE PROTOCOL.
  -> axis 4 applied to a single repo, built before the axis was named.
```

**DERIVED:** three separate routes — trust floor, benchmark adjudication,
repo self-audit — converge on criterion externality. None of the three was
derived from the others. That convergence is the evidence.

## SCOPE LIMITS

Not a critique of the paper. The three axes are not disputed and the
aggregation model is what makes R2 and R4 runnable at all — the
contribution is parasitic on the framework being correct.

C3 is not always reachable. Many domains have no re-runnable criterion
available, and saying so is the point: an audit regime that CANNOT reach
C3 should declare that, rather than reporting an assurance number computed
as though it had.

No claim that adding the axis raises assurance. It lowers the reported
number. That direction is the useful one.
